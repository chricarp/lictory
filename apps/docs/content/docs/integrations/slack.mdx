title: Sync channels with Slack
description: Link Lictory channels to Slack channels so your team keeps one conversation while it moves over. Each message stays owned by the side it was written on.
---

Your team does not have to leave Slack on day one. Link a Lictory channel to
a Slack channel and both sides see the same conversation: messages, thread
replies, edits, deletions and reactions. When everyone works in Lictory,
unlink it. Both keep their history.

## How ownership works

A message belongs to the system it was written in.

- A message written in **Slack** shows a small **Slack** mark in Lictory.
  Only Slack edits or deletes it; Lictory offers neither.
- A message written in **Lictory** is posted to Slack with its author's name
  and avatar. Edit or delete it in Lictory; Slack follows. Edits made to it
  in Slack are ignored.
- **Reactions** cross both ways. A reaction made in Lictory appears in Slack
  as the Lictory app's.
- **Files** shared in Slack appear as links to Slack, which keeps checking
  who may open them. Files attached in Lictory appear in Slack as links to
  the message in Lictory.
- **Promotion cards and agent cards** appear in Slack as short summaries
  with an **Open in Lictory** button.

A Slack person whose confirmed Slack email is the verified email of a member
of your organization writes as that member. Everyone else (guests, people
without an account, other bots) appears as **Name (Slack)** and has no
Lictory identity or permission.

Text from Slack is information, never permission. A Slack message can mention
an agent (`@forge`) only when its author is such a member and is allowed to
use that agent; otherwise the mention stays plain text.

## Connect Slack (organization administrators)

Each organization installs its own Slack app, created from a manifest
Lictory writes for your installation. It is an internal app of your Slack
workspace: nobody else installs it.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Create the app from the manifest

In **Organization settings → Integrations**, under **Slack**, copy the
manifest. Open [api.slack.com/apps](https://api.slack.com/apps?new_app=1),
choose **From a manifest**, pick your workspace, paste it and create the
app. The manifest already contains the events, interactivity and redirect
URLs of your Lictory installation and only the permissions Lictory uses.

</Step>
<Step>

### Save the app's credentials

On the app's **Basic Information** page, copy the **App ID**, **Client ID**,
**Client Secret** and **Signing Secret** into Lictory and choose **Save
app**. Lictory asks Slack to check the Client ID and Client Secret before it
saves them, and names the one Slack refused. They are encrypted at rest and
never shown again.

Slack offers no check of the Signing Secret on its own: it is proven when
Slack verifies the events URL. If Slack says the URL is not verified, open
**Event Subscriptions** in the app and choose **Retry** once the app is saved
in Lictory. If it still fails, copy the Signing Secret again and save.

</Step>
<Step>

### Install it

Choose **Install in Slack**, review the permissions and allow them. Slack
returns you to Lictory, which shows **Connected** and your workspace.

</Step>
</Steps>

**Reinstall** after updating the app from a newer manifest, or when Lictory
shows **Needs attention** (the app was uninstalled or its token revoked).
**Disconnect** revokes Lictory's token and unlinks every channel; messages
stay on both sides, and installing again needs no new app.

### Lictory Intelligence and Slack messages

Messages written in Slack are withheld from Lictory Intelligence (Litto and
other AI features) until an administrator turns on **Let Lictory
Intelligence read Slack messages**. It only takes effect while Lictory
Intelligence is on for the organization. Lictory never sends Slack content
to an AI provider otherwise.

## Link a channel

1. In Slack, invite the app to the channel: `/invite @Lictory`.
2. In Lictory, open the channel and choose **⋯ → Link to Slack channel…**.
3. Pick the Slack channel. A public channel links only to a public Slack
   channel and a private one only to a private Slack channel, so linking
   never widens who can read the conversation. Direct messages are not
   linked.
4. Optionally import the **last 25 to 200 messages** of the Slack channel,
   once. Lictory never copies a channel's whole history.

Anyone who manages the channel (its members, and organization administrators
for public channels) links and unlinks it. The header then shows **Synced with
#channel in Slack**, linking to it, with a red dot when the sync fails (for
example, when the app was removed from the Slack channel).

A Lictory message that could not reach Slack says **Not sent to Slack** with
the reason and a **Retry** button for its author and the channel's managers.
Lictory retries by itself first, waiting when Slack asks it to.

**⋯ → Unlink from Slack** stops the sync. Both channels keep everything that
was said; nothing more is sent either way.

## Self-hosted installations

- Slack must reach your installation: `PLATFORM_URL` must be a public HTTPS
  address (for testing locally, a tunnel).
- Set `DELIVERY_CREDENTIALS_KEY`, which also encrypts Slack credentials
  (see the [configuration reference](/self-hosting/configuration)).
- The worker sends and receives messages; it needs outbound HTTPS to
  `slack.com`.
