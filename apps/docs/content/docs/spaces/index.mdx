**Lictory Spaces** is where a team keeps what it knows that is not a work
item: handbooks, runbooks, architecture notes, decision records, onboarding
guides. Pages are written together in real time and changed through the same
reviewed history as specifications, so you can always see what a page said,
what changed, who changed it and who approved it.

![A published page in a space: the page tree on the left, an item chip and a decision card in the text, a live work list, and the version line under the title](/screenshots/spaces-page-reader.webp)

## Spaces and pages

```text
Organization
└─ Space        a home for knowledge, optionally belonging to a project or a team
   └─ Page      nested pages; each has its own history
      ├─ main         the published version readers see
      └─ branches     proposed changes, each with its own live draft
```

- A **space** belongs to the organization and, optionally, to one project or
  one team. That **location** decides where the space is listed and who can
  edit it by default. See [create and manage spaces](/spaces/create).
- A **page** belongs to one space and can sit under another page of the same
  space. It can start from a [template](/spaces/templates). See
  [pages and the page tree](/spaces/pages).
- Every page has a **published version**, the one readers see, and a
  **history** of every earlier version. See
  [versions and history](/spaces/history).
- People write in a **live draft**: a shared working copy that several
  editors change at once. A live draft is never history. Only what someone
  **publishes** or **commits** becomes a version. See
  [writing together](/spaces/editing).

## Pages and work items

Pages are not work items. They have no key, status, estimate, iteration or
readiness, and a page never becomes delivery work by itself. Specifications
stay on their work items, where they are refined, reviewed and checked for
readiness ([specifications](/concepts/specifications)). The two are
connected by [knowledge links](/knowledge) instead:

- **In a page**, type `#` to mention a work item: readers see a chip with its
  key, title and status, and a card with its refinement state, readiness,
  owner and iteration. Decision cards and live work lists show what Work
  holds now ([blocks](/spaces/blocks)). Select text, or use an open task, to
  **Create work item** from it ([work in your pages](/knowledge/in-pages)).
- **On a work item**, the **Knowledge** section lists the pages that mention
  it, are attached to it or that its specification cites, and **Propose page
  update** opens a branch of a page from the item
  ([pages in your work](/knowledge/in-work)).
- **On the board, backlog and iterations**, planners keep
  [context pages](/knowledge/context-pages) one click away.

Every link is visible only to people who can read its page, and a link never
grants access to anything. Both are written in the same editor, and both
have a live draft that several people edit at once.

|                   | Specification                                     | Page                                               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Lives on          | A work item of a project                          | A space                                            |
| Edited by         | People whose discipline edits specifications      | People with edit access to the space               |
| Becomes a version | **Save revision** (main) or **Commit** (a branch) | **Publish** (main of a Direct space) or **Commit** |
| Reviewed by       | The project's disciplines (Product, QA…)          | Anyone who can comment on the space                |
| Feeds             | Readiness checks and delivery                     | Nothing: pages are knowledge, not work             |
| Who can see it    | Every organization member                         | Decided per space: Inherited or Restricted         |

## How a page changes

Each space chooses one publishing policy:

- **Direct** (the default): editors change the page's live draft together and
  **Publish** it. Every published version stays in the history.
- **Reviewed**: the published page is protected. Changes are proposed on a
  branch, reviewed in a pull request and published when it merges.

<ReviewedChange />

Both policies keep the same guarantees: versions never change after the
fact, approvals belong to the exact result a reviewer inspected, and nothing
is merged or published that nobody saw. See [publishing](/spaces/publishing)
and [branches and pull requests](/spaces/branches).

## Tips

- Put knowledge that outlives one piece of work on a page (how a service
  works, a runbook, a decision record) and the behavior you are about to
  build in a specification. Link them both ways.
- Choose the location by who maintains the knowledge: a team's runbooks
  belong to the team, a product area's designs to its project.
- Use a Reviewed space where others rely on what a page says, and a Direct
  space for notes a team keeps current day to day.
