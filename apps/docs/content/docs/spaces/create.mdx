## Create a space

Any member of an organization can create a space. Choose **New space** on
the **Spaces** page, the **+** next to **Spaces** in the sidebar, or
**Create space** in the command palette.

![The New space dialog: an icon button and a Name field, Customize address, an optional Description, and Access & publishing summarized as "Northwind · everyone can edit · direct publishing" with Configure](/screenshots/spaces-new-space.webp)

<Steps>
<Step>

**Name and icon.** Name the space for what it holds (_Engineering
Handbook_, _Payments_, _Architecture decisions_). The button beside the name
picks an icon, shown wherever the space is listed.

</Step>
<Step>

**Address.** Lictory derives the space's address from its name, for example
`/northwind/spaces/engineering-handbook`. **Customize address** changes it:
lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens. A space's address is part of
the address of each of its pages, so links people copied or bookmarked keep
working only while it stays the same. Page links inside pages (`[[`) are
unaffected: they point at the page itself and always use the current
address.

</Step>
<Step>

**Description** (optional). One sentence about what belongs here. It is
shown on the spaces list and on the space's home.

</Step>
<Step>

**Access & publishing.** One line summarizes where the space will live, who
can open it and how it publishes, for example _Northwind · everyone can edit
· direct publishing_. **Configure** changes it:

- **Location**: the organization, one of its projects or one of a project's
  teams. The location decides where the space is listed in navigation and,
  while access is inherited, who can edit it.
- **Access**: **Inherited** (the location's members can edit; everyone else
  in the organization can view) or **Restricted** (only people you add can
  open it, and you get edit access). See [access](/spaces/access).
- **Publishing**: **Direct** (editors publish changes straight to the page)
  or **Reviewed** (changes are proposed on a branch and published only after
  a review approves them). See [publishing](/spaces/publishing).

</Step>
<Step>

**Create space.** The space opens on its home, ready for a first page
(blank or from a [template](/spaces/templates)), which needs edit access to
the space. An administrator who creates an
Inherited space for a team or project they are not a member of can view it
but not write in it, until they join or name themselves with edit access.

</Step>
</Steps>

Locations follow your organization's structure: an archived project or team
cannot become the location of a new space.

## Choose a location

| Location         | Listed in                                | While access is inherited, who can edit |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Organization** | The sidebar's **Spaces**, for everyone   | Everyone in the organization            |
| **Project**      | The project's navigation                 | Members of the project                  |
| **Team**         | The project's navigation, under its team | Members of the team                     |

A space owns no delivery configuration. Moving work items between projects
or teams never changes a space, and moving a space never changes work
items. Pick the location by who maintains the knowledge: a team's runbooks
belong to the team, a product area's decisions to the project, a company
handbook to the organization.

## The spaces list

**Spaces** in the sidebar lists the organization's spaces and the spaces of
your projects and teams; **All spaces** opens every space you can read,
organization spaces first and then by project. Each row shows the space's
icon, name and description, how many pages it has and when it was last
updated. A filter appears when the list grows, and **Show archived spaces**
reveals the archived ones.

![The Spaces page: the organization’s Engineering Handbook first, then the Payments project’s space, each row with its icon, description, page count and last update, and New space](/screenshots/spaces-list.webp)

Spaces you cannot read are not listed. The one exception is for
administrators: Restricted spaces you administer without reading them appear
under **Restricted spaces you administer**, by name only (see
[administrators](/spaces/access#administrators)).

## A space's home

Opening a space shows its home:

![The home of a project space: its name and facts (location, publishing policy, your access), New page, the page tree with Find a page, Recently updated, and Linked work with how much of it is done](/screenshots/spaces-space-home.webp)

- **The header** names the space with its location, its publishing policy
  (**Direct publishing** or **Reviewed publishing**), **Restricted** when it
  is, and your own access (**Can view**, **Can comment** or **Can edit**).
- **New page** adds a page at the end of the space (editors only).
- **Space settings** (the sliders button) opens the settings, for editors
  and administrators.
- **Pull requests**, in Reviewed spaces, lists the open proposals across the
  space's pages.
- **Pages** is the space's tree, with **Find a page** to narrow it to the
  titles that match, wherever they sit. See [pages](/spaces/pages).
- **Recently updated** lists the latest published versions with who
  published them and their message, so you can see what changed without
  opening each page.
- **Linked work**, on the home of a project or team space, lists the work
  items its pages are linked to: mentioned by a published version, created
  from a page, attached to an item or cited by a specification. Each item
  appears once with how it is linked (_Mentioned, attached · Capture retry
  design_), open work first, with how many are in progress and done. Items
  you cannot see are left out. See [work in your pages](/knowledge/in-pages).

## Space settings

Every setting applies as soon as you change it (text fields when you leave
them or press Enter) and says **Saving…**, then **Saved**, or why it was
refused, right beside it.

| Group                     | Settings                                            | Who can change them                                          |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Details**               | Name, icon, address, description                    | Editors of the space                                         |
| **Location & publishing** | Location; publishing policy (Direct or Reviewed)    | [Administrators](/spaces/access#administrators) of the space |
| **Access**                | Access mode (Inherited or Restricted); named access | Administrators of the space                                  |
| **Archive**               | Archive or restore the space                        | Administrators of the space                                  |

Changes that decide who can read or edit ask first:

- **Moving an Inherited space** to another location says where it will be
  listed and who will be able to edit it before anything changes.
- **Restricting** says that members of the location lose access unless they
  are named, and that you keep edit access.

Switching a space from Direct to Reviewed is refused while a page has
unpublished changes: publish or discard them first, so nothing typed in a
live draft is stranded. Switching back to Direct reopens each page's live
draft at its published version.

## Archive a space

**Archive space** hides the space from navigation and freezes its pages:
they stay readable through their links and history, but nobody can edit,
publish or comment until it is restored. Nothing is deleted. Archived spaces
appear under **Show archived spaces**, and **Restore space** (on the space or
in its settings) brings everything back as it was.

Archiving a project or a team does not archive its spaces or change who can
read them.

## Tips

- Give each project a space of its own. Its spaces and recently published
  pages then show on the project's **Refinement** view, and its home lists
  the work its pages are linked to.
- Write the space's description as the one sentence that tells someone
  whether a page belongs here.
