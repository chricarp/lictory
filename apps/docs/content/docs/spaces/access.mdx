---
title: Access
description: Who can view, comment on and edit a space - inherited from its location or restricted to named people - and how administrators manage it without reading it.
---

Access is decided per space, never per page: every page, with its history,
branches and discussions, is open to exactly the people who can open the
space.

## Inherited and Restricted

![Space settings of Payments: name, address and description, the location and Direct or Reviewed publishing, and Access with Inherited or Restricted and named access](/screenshots/spaces-access.webp)

| Access                  | Who can edit                                                                               | Who can read                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| **Inherited** (default) | Members of the location (organization, project or team), and people named with edit access | Every organization member     |
| **Restricted**          | People named with edit access                                                              | Only people with named access |

<SpaceAccess />

- **Inherited access follows membership, live.** Joining a team gives edit
  access to its spaces at once; leaving takes it away. Everyone can edit an
  organization-level space.
- **A Restricted space ignores location membership**: only named people can
  open it. Whoever restricts a space (or creates it Restricted) keeps edit
  access.
- **Switching back to Inherited** keeps every named access.

Project or team membership grants nothing on work items; that comes from
[disciplines](/concepts/organizations#roles-and-disciplines).

## Levels

| Level           | What it allows                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Can view**    | Read published pages, any earlier version, branches, pull requests and discussions                                                              |
| **Can comment** | Also start and take part in discussions, resolve them, mark them blocking, and be asked to review pull requests                                 |
| **Can edit**    | Also create, move and archive pages, edit and publish live drafts, start branches, open and merge pull requests, and change the space's details |

Editing a branch also requires being its author or a collaborator, and
merging is subject to the [merge checks](/spaces/branches#merge). In an
Inherited project or team space, everyone else in the organization can view
but not comment until named.

## Named access

In **Space settings → Access**, **Give access** adds a person; choose **Can
view**, **Can comment** or **Can edit**.

Each row shows the person's effective access and why (_Can edit · member of
Payments_, _Can comment · named access_). Removing a row first says what they
would keep: _Without it, Dev can only view._ or _Without it, Dev loses
access._

- In an Inherited space, named access adds to what the location gives, for
  example to let outsiders comment or edit.
- In a Restricted space, named access is the only access.
- Named access counts only while the person is an organization member.
  Someone who leaves gets it back on rejoining; deleting their account
  removes it.

## Administrators

A space's **administrators** are its creator, its project's administrators
while the project is active (for a project or team location), and the
organization's administrators, while they are organization members:

| Who                             | Can change                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Editors** of the space        | Name, icon, address and description                               |
| **Administrators** of the space | Location, publishing policy, access mode, named access, archiving |

Managing a Restricted space does **not** let an administrator read it. It is
listed by name only on the **Spaces** page under **Restricted spaces you
administer**, with **Grant myself access…**: pick the level and **Grant
access (audited)**. The grant is audited with their name and shows as
_Self-granted · audited_, where other administrators can remove it.

Changes to access mode, named access, location, publishing policy and
archiving are recorded in the organization's audit log.

## No page-level restrictions

To keep part of a tree private, put it in its own Restricted space with the
same location, so it is listed beside the open one.

## Links to work grant nothing

A [knowledge link](/knowledge) is visible only to people who can read its
page:

- A Restricted page attached to an item, cited by its specification or
  mentioning it appears in the item's **Knowledge** section only for its
  readers. Everyone else sees no row, title or count.
- Every member reads work items. **Create work item** from a Restricted page
  warns that the quoted text is copied into the item, visible to people who
  cannot read the page.
- A work item mention or decision card never lets the page's readers change
  the item or the decision.

When someone loses access, its pages leave their open **Knowledge** sections
at once, and a page chip in a specification shows only its author's words,
with no card. Every member reads those words: describe a Restricted page by
something you are happy for everyone to see.

## Enforced on the server

Every check runs on the server, for the browser, live editing, agents and
notifications:

- a space or page you cannot read looks exactly like one that does not
  exist: "not found", no title, no count;
- removed access refuses every later save, and closes the open live editing
  connection when the editing service hears of it, or at its next check of
  every connection (every 30 seconds);
- notifications reach only current readers, and disappear for someone who
  loses access.
