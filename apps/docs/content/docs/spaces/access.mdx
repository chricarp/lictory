---
title: Access
description: Who can view, comment on and edit a space - inherited from its location or restricted to named people - and how administrators manage it without reading it.
---

Access is decided per space, never per page. Every page of a space, with its
history, branches and discussions, is open to exactly the people who can
open the space.

## Inherited and Restricted

![Space settings of Payments: name, address and description, the location and Direct or Reviewed publishing, and Access with Inherited or Restricted and named access](/screenshots/spaces-access.webp)

| Access                  | Who can edit                                                                               | Who can read                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| **Inherited** (default) | Members of the location (organization, project or team), and people named with edit access | Every organization member     |
| **Restricted**          | People named with edit access                                                              | Only people with named access |

<SpaceAccess />

- **Inherited access follows membership, live.** Joining a team gives edit
  access to its spaces at once; leaving takes it away. A space whose location
  is the organization can be edited by everyone in it.
- **A Restricted space ignores membership** of its location: only named people
  can open it, whatever their role in the project or team. Whoever restricts
  a space (or creates it Restricted) keeps edit access, so nobody locks
  themselves out by accident.
- **Switching back to Inherited** keeps every named access.

Membership of a project or team gives access to its spaces only. It still
grants nothing on work items: what you can do with work items comes from
your [disciplines](/concepts/organizations#roles-and-disciplines).

## Levels

| Level           | What it allows                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Can view**    | Read published pages, any earlier version, branches, pull requests and discussions                                                              |
| **Can comment** | Also start and take part in discussions, resolve them, mark them blocking, and be asked to review pull requests                                 |
| **Can edit**    | Also create, move and archive pages, edit and publish live drafts, start branches, open and merge pull requests, and change the space's details |

Editing a branch also requires being its author or one of its collaborators,
and merging is subject to the [merge checks](/spaces/branches#merge). In
an Inherited space with a project or team location, everyone else in the
organization can view, but not comment, until they are named.

## Named access

In **Space settings → Access**, **Give access** adds a person to the space's
named access; then choose their level: **Can view**, **Can comment** or
**Can edit**.

Each row shows what the person can do in the end and why, for example _Can
edit · member of Payments_ or _Can comment · named access_. Removing named
access (the remove button on its row) first says what they would keep: _Without it,
Dev can only view._ or _Without it, Dev loses access._

- In an Inherited space, named access only adds to what the location gives.
  Name people from outside the project or team to let them comment or edit.
- In a Restricted space, named access is the only access.
- Named access counts only while the person is a member of the organization.
  Someone who leaves keeps their rows and gets the access back if they
  rejoin; deleting their account removes it.

## Administrators

A space's **administrators** are its creator, the administrators of its
project while that project is active (for a project or team location) and
the organization's administrators, as long as they are members of the
organization. They manage what decides who can read what:

| Who                             | Can change                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Editors** of the space        | Name, icon, address and description                               |
| **Administrators** of the space | Location, publishing policy, access mode, named access, archiving |

Managing a Restricted space does **not** let an administrator read it. They
find it on the **Spaces** page under **Restricted spaces you administer**, by
name only, and can choose **Grant myself access…**: pick the level and
**Grant access (audited)**. The grant is recorded in the audit log with their
name, shows as _Self-granted · audited_ in the space's named access, and
other administrators can see and remove it.

Every change to a space's access mode, named access, location, publishing
policy and archiving is recorded in the organization's audit log.

## No page-level restrictions

Pages cannot be restricted one by one. To keep part of a tree private, put
it in its own Restricted space; it can have the same location as the open
one, so it is listed right beside it.

## Links to work grant nothing

Pages and work items link to each other ([knowledge links](/knowledge)), and
every link is visible only to people who can read its page:

- A Restricted page attached to an item, cited by its specification or
  mentioning it appears in the item's **Knowledge** section only for people
  who can read that page. Everyone else sees no row, no title and no count.
- Work items are read by every member of the organization. When you
  **Create work item** from text on a Restricted page, the dialog warns that
  the quoted text is copied into the item, where people who cannot read the
  page will see it. Decide what to copy before you create it.
- A mention of a work item on a page never lets the page's readers change
  the item, and a decision card never lets them change the decision.

When someone loses access to a space, its pages leave the **Knowledge**
sections they have open at once, and a page chip in a specification shows
only the words its author wrote, with no card. Those words are part of the
specification, which every member reads: describe a Restricted page by
something you are happy for everyone to see.

## Enforced on the server

Every check runs on the server, for the browser, live editing, agents and
notifications alike:

- a space or page you cannot read looks exactly like one that does not
  exist: the same "not found", no title, no count;
- when someone's access is removed, every edit they try to save is
  refused from then on, and their open live editing connection is closed as
  soon as the editing service hears of the change, or at its next check of
  every connection, made every 30 seconds;
- notifications reach only people who can read the space at that moment,
  and disappear for someone who loses access.
