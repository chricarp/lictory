---
title: Blocks and chips
description: Show work items, other pages and recorded decisions in a page, add a live list of work, and know what each reader sees.
---

A page can show the work it is about without copying it. Item and page
chips, decision cards and live work lists are read from Lictory Work each
time someone opens the page, with that reader's own access. The page stores
only which item, page, decision or list it means; the work keeps its own
state, and nothing on a page changes it.

![A published page with item chips in a sentence, a decision card on its own line and a live work list of an epic’s children](/screenshots/spaces-page-reader.webp)

## When to use it

- **Item chips** when a sentence is about a piece of work: _Retries follow
  #PAY-2_.
- **Page chips** to send readers to another page, or to cite one exact
  version of it.
- **Decision cards** to show an agreed conclusion where people read about
  the topic, without retyping it.
- **Work lists** for the work behind a page: an epic's stories on its
  requirements page, a sprint's items on its notes.

## Item chips

Type `#` and pick an item, or choose **Insert → Work item**
([writing together](/spaces/editing#work-items-page-links-and-decisions)).
Readers see a chip with the item's status, key and current title.

![The card of the PAY-2 chip: Story, Retry failed card captures, In Progress, In review, 2 of 4 checks with what is still missing (a blocking discussion, and Engineering and QA reviews of revision 2), owner Dev Patel, Sprint 1 and Open PAY-2](/screenshots/detail-item-chip-card.webp)

Point at a chip or focus it for its card:

| In the card    | What it shows                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Status**     | The item's delivery status. In a project that delivers in Jira, the status read back from Jira (_In Progress in Jira_, _Not handed off to Jira_) |
| **Refinement** | The refinement state, for types with a specification                                                                                             |
| Readiness      | How many checks pass (_2 of 4 checks_) and up to two that are still missing                                                                      |
| **Owner**      | Who it is assigned to, or _Unassigned_; the delivery tool's assignee for external projects                                                       |
| **Iteration**  | The sprint or cycle it is planned in, when it has one                                                                                            |
| **Open PAY-2** | Opens the item                                                                                                                                   |

An archived item says **Archived**. Escape closes the card.

## Page chips

Type `[[` and pick a page, or choose **Insert → Link to a page**. The chip
shows the space's icon and the page's current title, even after the page is
renamed or moved.

To cite one exact version, press `Shift Enter` in the picker, or choose
**Pin this version** beside the option: the chip adds `vN`, opens that
version, and shows a small marker once a newer version is published. Its
card names the space and path, who published it last, and for a pinned chip
_Version N is published._ with **Compare in history**; **Open this version**
or **Open page** follows the link.

People who cannot open the linked page see _Unavailable page_ and no card.

## Decision cards

A decision is recorded on a work item during refinement
([decisions](/specifications/decisions)). A page can show it:

1. In the editor, type `/` and choose **Decision**, or **Insert → Decision**.
2. **Find a decision…**: decisions you can read, by title.
3. Press Enter. On a line of its own the decision becomes a **card**; inside
   a sentence it is a chip with its title.

![A decision card: Decision, the title “Retries reuse the original idempotency key”, its conclusion, the item PAY-2 Retry failed card captures and “Recorded on revision 1”](/screenshots/detail-decision-card.webp)

The card shows the decision's title and conclusion, the work item it was
recorded on and _Recorded on revision N_. The words stay in Work: when the
decision is withdrawn there, the card says _Archived: withdrawn from active
product knowledge, kept for history_, and an open page notices without a
reload (when you return to the window, and every minute while it is
visible).

If the page cannot check the decision again, for example while you are
offline, the card keeps what it read and says so: _Couldn’t refresh · last
checked 3m ago_ with **Retry**. A decision that cannot be read at all shows
_This decision is unavailable._

You can also start from the work item: **Add to a page** on one of its
decisions proposes a change that adds the card to a page you can edit
(**Add the decision to a page**, find the page, **Propose change**). It opens
a pull request; the page gets the decision once it is merged, never by
editing the published page directly.

## Live work lists

<Steps>
<Step>

**Insert.** In the editor, type `/` and choose **Work list**.

</Step>
<Step>

**Choose what to list** under **Show**:

- **An epic's work**: the children of one epic.
- **An iteration**: everything planned in a sprint or cycle.
- **A label**: work with one label in a project.
- **Work this page mentions**: the items mentioned with `#` on this page.

Pick the **Project** and the epic, iteration or label, and optionally a
**Title** (otherwise the list is named after what it lists, such as
_Children of PAY-1_). Choose **Insert list**.

</Step>
<Step>

**Publish** the page as usual. Readers see the list from then on.

</Step>
</Steps>

![A live work list titled “Resilient card captures: delivery”, “Children of PAY-1 · 0 of 5 done”: each row has the item’s status, key, title, delivery status, refinement state and estimate, and “Live from Lictory Work. Open an item to change it.” below](/screenshots/detail-work-list.webp)

Each row shows the item's status, key, title, refinement state and estimate
(the delivery tool's status in external projects, without estimates). The
header counts _N of M done_ among the items shown; canceled items count as
neither. A list shows ten rows, and **Show N more** adds ten at a time.

To change a list, open the editor and use the list's settings button
(**Change what this list shows**), then **Save**. Nothing in a list edits an
item: open the item to change it.

## Who sees what

| Block     | Readers who can see the target       | Everyone else who can read the page                              |
| --------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Item chip | Status, key, title, and the card     | The key stored in the page, no card                              |
| Page chip | The current title, version and card  | _Unavailable page_, no card                                      |
| Decision  | Title, conclusion, item and revision | The title stored in the page, or _This decision is unavailable._ |
| Work list | The matching items they can see now  | Only the items they can see; counts cover only those             |

Everything is read with the reader's own access at the moment they read.
Work items are visible to every member of the organization; pages follow
their space's [access](/spaces/access). A chip or list never grants access
to what it shows.

## What to watch for

- **Lists are not snapshots.** A work list stores its query, not its items.
  An old version of the page shows today's items.
- **Agents and Litto get a plain list.** When an agent or
  [Litto](/ai/litto) reads a page, each work list is turned into ordinary
  item references as of that moment, read with their access. Pasting that
  Markdown back gives plain references, not a live list.
- **Links follow published versions.** A chip typed in a live draft links
  the page to the item only once the page is published
  ([knowledge links](/knowledge#references-follow-published-versions)).
- **External projects** show the delivery tool's status and owner. Native
  delivery details (estimates, iterations) do not apply to them.

## Tips

- Put a live work list on each requirements page and design doc, so readers
  see how far the work is without leaving the page.
- Cite decisions instead of retyping them: when a decision is withdrawn, the
  card says so everywhere it appears.
- Pin a page chip when the exact wording matters (a policy, an agreed
  limit), and leave it unpinned when readers should always get the latest.
