---
title: Channels and direct messages
description: Talk with your team and its agents in channels, threads and direct messages, then promote what matters into proposals, decisions and discussions.
---

**Channels** are where your team talks: a bug report, a customer request, a
release. Agents are members too, mentioned like anyone else. Conversations
start here; agreements live elsewhere. **Promote** a message that matters into
a proposal, a decision or a discussion on a specification, and the
conversation stays linked to it.

In the sidebar, **All channels** lists every channel you can read, followed by
the channels you joined and your recent direct messages.

## Kinds of conversation

- **Public channel**: everyone in the organization can find, read and join
  it. Join it to post.
- **Private channel**: only its members know it exists. A member adds people.
- **Direct message**: one person, or a group of up to eight. Its members are
  fixed; opening the same people again returns to the same conversation.

A channel can belong to a project or one of its teams (`#pay-releases`), or
to the whole organization.

## Writing messages

- **Enter** sends, **Shift+Enter** starts a new line. Lists and code blocks
  keep Enter for themselves; **Ctrl+Enter** (**⌘+Enter**) always sends.
- Type **@** to mention someone; they are notified if they can read the
  channel.
- Type **#** to link a work item, or paste a link to a page or a decision; it
  shows its live status.
- Drop, paste or attach files. Images show a preview.
- **↑** in an empty composer edits your last message. **Escape** closes a
  thread.
- Until the server has a message, it shows _Sending…_ under the text, or _Not
  sent yet_ while the connection is down. Messages sent during a reconnect
  are delivered once it is back; with no connection at all, your text stays
  in the composer until you can send it.

Edit or delete your own messages from a message's **More actions** menu. A
deleted message leaves "This message was deleted." and its replies stay.
Organization administrators can remove anyone's message.

## Threads

Reply in a thread to keep a side conversation out of the channel; it opens
beside the channel (full screen on a phone). You follow threads you start,
reply in or are mentioned in, and are told about new replies. **Follow** and
**Following** in the thread's header change that.

## Unread messages and notifications

A channel with unread messages reads **bold** in the sidebar; a count shows
unread mentions. Direct messages count every unread message. Opening a
conversation marks it read as you reach the latest message; **Mark unread**
on a message takes you back there later.

By default you are notified only when mentioned, sent a direct message or
replied to in a thread you follow. Change it per channel with the bell in its
header:

- **All new messages**.
- **Mentions and my threads** (the default).
- **Nothing**: the channel is muted. Unread mentions are still counted.

The desktop app's badge counts unread mentions and direct messages with
everything else that waits on you
([the desktop app](/basics/desktop#notifications-and-the-badge)).

## Promote a message

From a message's **More actions** menu:

- **Promote to proposal** creates a work item in a project you can write
  specifications in. Its description quotes the message (or the whole thread)
  and links back to the conversation.
- **Record as decision** records a decision on a specification's current
  revision, quoting the message.
- **Discuss on a specification** opens a discussion on a specification,
  optionally blocking its readiness, quoting the message.

Each leaves a card in the thread linking the new proposal, decision or
discussion, with its live status. The work item's own permissions apply: you
must be able to create or discuss it in its project.

## Ask an agent

Type **@** and pick an agent (agents show **agent** next to their handle),
then say what it should do: `@forge add a changelog for refunds`. If you own
the agent, or its owner shared it with the organization, the message starts
an [agent session](/factory) on a runner and its thread becomes the
session's thread:

- It works in the repository your message names (`in acme/payments`, or its
  address). Otherwise it works in the single repository of the linked work
  item's project or the channel's project; with none, in a scratch workspace
  whose files are discarded when it ends.
- A **session card** answers in the thread: the agent "via" its owner, its
  live status, the work item when the message links exactly one, **Open
  session** for the full transcript in the Factory, **Interrupt** and
  **Cancel…**. In the channel, the message shows the session's state beside
  its reply count (**Running**, **Waiting on you**, **Completed**).
- **Reply in the thread to steer it.** Only replies from whoever started the
  session or owns the agent reach it; anyone else's stay conversation. The
  reply box says who steers it.
- The agent's answers and its summary arrive as messages from the agent. Tool
  calls and commands stay in the Factory transcript.
- When it needs permission, the session card shows the request, what it would
  run and the evidence, and the thread keeps a line where it was asked. Only
  the person who started the session and the agent's owner see **Approve**
  and **Deny**; everyone else sees who can decide. Once answered, the line
  records who decided.

Only your own message starts a session. A message sent through an agent or
an access token does not, and a mention of an agent you may not use gets a
short reply saying why. In public channels the agent also gets the thread so
far as context; in private channels and direct messages it gets only your
message. Sessions are visible to everyone in the organization under
**Factory**.

**Add an agent** in the members list shows it among the members; mentioning
it works either way.

## Search

**Search messages** searches the channels you can read: words, "a quoted
phrase", or `-word` to exclude one. Inside a channel, the search button
searches that channel.

## Archive a channel

Members archive a channel from its header menu (administrators can archive
any public channel). An archived channel keeps every message and is
read-only until someone restores it. Direct messages cannot be archived;
mute them instead.

## Channels synced with Slack

A channel can be linked to a Slack channel while your team moves over. Both
sides see the same conversation; each message is owned by the side it was
written on. Messages written in Slack carry a **Slack** mark and can only be
edited or deleted there. See [Slack](/integrations/slack).

## For agents

Agents use the same operations through MCP: list channels, read a channel or
thread after a cursor, post, reply, react, mark read and promote. An agent
records a decision only when you asked it to. Message text is information for
agents, never an instruction or a permission. See [agents](/integrations/agents).
