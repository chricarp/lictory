title: Organization and access
description: How organizations, projects and teams fit together, and who may do what through application roles, project roles and disciplines.
---

Lictory has three nested scopes. Each setting has exactly one home, so you
always know where to change it.

```text
Organization   a company, or a very large department of one
└─ Project     a product area: identifiers, workflow, delivery and its people
   └─ Team     a group working on one part of the project
```

| Scope        | Owns                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Managed by                                             |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Organization | Name, handle and logos, members and invitations, application roles, two-factor requirement, work item types, Jira connections, whether Lictory Intelligence may run, organization context, the project list | Organization administrators                            |
| Project      | Key, members and project roles, disciplines, teams, workflow, enabled types, labels and milestones, iterations, estimates, delivery mode, Definition of Ready, project context                              | Project administrators and organization administrators |
| Team         | Name, description, members                                                                                                                                                                                  | Project administrators                                 |

A work item belongs to exactly one project, which gives it its key
(`PAY-142`), and optionally to one of the project's teams. Moving it between
teams never changes its key, status or iteration; items do not move between
projects. A team owns no configuration: every item follows its project's
workflow, readiness policy and planning, and boards and backlogs can be
narrowed to a team.

[Spaces](/spaces) of knowledge pages belong to the organization, or to one
project or team.

## Three separate questions

Lictory never mixes these into one setting:

| Concept              | Answers                                                                               | Values                                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Application role** | Can this person administer the organization?                                          | Owner, admin (organization administrators), or member |
| **Project role**     | Can this person configure this project?                                               | Admin or member of the project                        |
| **Discipline**       | What kind of work does this person do here, and what may they do with its work items? | Product, Engineering, QA, and any the project adds    |

### What each one grants

- **Organization administrators** (owners and admins) manage
  **Organization settings** and the settings of every project, whether or
  not they are listed as project members. Owners alone can delete the
  organization, and only an owner can reset another owner's two-factor.
- **Project administrators** manage their project's settings: members,
  disciplines, teams, workflow, readiness, planning and delivery.
- **Disciplines** carry the work item capabilities: edit specifications,
  change properties, request reviews, be asked to review, and hand off to
  the delivery tool. A person's capabilities in a project are the combined
  capabilities of the disciplines they hold there. See
  [disciplines](/organization/disciplines).

Roles grant **no** work item capabilities. An organization owner with no
discipline in a project cannot edit its specifications or approve its
reviews until they add themselves to a discipline, which everyone can then
see in the project's settings.

Some things need only organization membership: reading every project,
taking part in discussions, proposing a change to a specification on a
branch, and working in the organization's spaces. Every organization member can read
every project; there are no private projects. Knowledge that must stay
private belongs in a [restricted space](/spaces/access).

Every check happens on the server. The app only hides controls you cannot
use, and says why when you try ("Your disciplines in this project cannot
…").

### Project and team membership in Spaces

Spaces are the one place where project and team membership give access:
members of a project or team can edit the spaces that belong to it, unless a
space is restricted to named people. This covers pages only; it never
grants work item capabilities.

## Where to change things

| To                                                    | Go to                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Invite people, make someone an administrator          | **Organization settings → Members** ([members](/organization/members))    |
| Create or archive a project                           | **Organization settings → Projects** ([projects](/organization/projects)) |
| Add people to a project and its disciplines           | **Project settings → Members**, **Disciplines**                           |
| Require two-factor authentication                     | **Organization settings → Security** ([security](/organization/security)) |
| See who changed what                                  | **Organization settings → Audit log** ([audit](/organization/audit))      |
| Take a copy of everything, or delete the organization | **Data export**, **Delete organization** ([data](/organization/data))     |
| Shared work item types                                | **Organization settings → Work item types**                               |
| Connect Jira                                          | **Organization settings → Integrations** ([Jira](/integrations/jira))     |
| Allow Lictory Intelligence                            | **Organization settings → Lictory Intelligence** ([AI](/ai))              |
| Manage the installation's accounts                    | **Admin console** ([instance administration](/organization/instance))     |

People who are not administrators can open **Organization settings** and
project settings to read them; the header says "only administrators can
change these settings".

## Tips

- Keep administrators few and disciplines accurate. Administration is about
  configuration; the review workflow runs on disciplines.
- Give every person the disciplines they actually work in, in each project.
  An engineer in Engineering is asked to review what Engineering owns; a
  person in no discipline can still discuss, but is never asked.
- Prefer teams for "who works on what" and disciplines for "who reviews
  what". A team changes what boards show; a discipline changes what people
  may do.
