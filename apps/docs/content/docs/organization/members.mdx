---
title: Members and invitations
description: Invite people to an organization, place them in a project and its disciplines, and manage who administers the organization.
---

People join an organization by invitation. An organization administrator
invites them, and they become members when they accept. Everything else
(which projects they work in, which disciplines they hold) is set in each
project.

![Organization settings, Members: the invitation form with a project and disciplines, and the member list with roles and projects](/screenshots/org-members.webp)

## Invite someone

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open Members

Go to **Organization settings → Members**. Only organization administrators
can invite.

</Step>
<Step>

### Enter the person

Under **Invite to the organization**, type their email address, or the
`@username` of someone who already has an account on your installation.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose a project and disciplines (optional)

Pick a **Project** (or keep **Organization only**) and tick the disciplines
they will work in, for example Engineering. These apply when they accept, so
they arrive ready to review.

</Step>
<Step>

### Send it

**Send invitation** emails them a link. The page also shows the invitation
link so you can share it another way. It is accepted by signing in with the
invited email address.

</Step>
</Steps>

People who have no account yet create one from the invitation, as your
installation's [access policy](/organization/instance#access-policy) allows
(by default, a new account needs a pending invitation). People with an
account see the invitation when they sign in.

### Pending invitations

**Pending invitations** lists people who have not joined yet. **Withdraw**
cancels an invitation: its link stops working.

## Accept an invitation

Open the link from the email and sign in with the address you were invited
at (or create your account the way the page offers), then **Accept
invitation**. The organization opens; if the invitation placed you in a
project and disciplines, you are already there. If that project or a
discipline is no longer available, you still join, and Lictory tells you
what could not be added so an administrator can add it.

## Roles

The member list shows each person's application role (**Admin**, or
**Admin · owner**) and the projects they belong to, with their project role.

- **Make admin** lets a member manage the organization and every project.
  **Make member** takes it back. An organization needs at least one
  administrator, so the last one cannot be demoted.
- **Owners** are administrators who can also delete the organization and
  reset other owners' two-factor. Whoever creates an organization is its
  owner; an instance administrator can also add someone as an owner from the
  [admin console](/organization/instance).

Being an administrator grants no work item capabilities: those come from
[disciplines](/organization/disciplines) in each project.

## Add people to a project

In **Project settings → Members**, **Add people** adds organization
members to the project. Each person has a project role (admin or member)
and the disciplines they hold, which you toggle in the same row.

- Organization administrators manage every project without being listed,
  and are marked **Organization admin** when they are.
- Adding someone to a discipline or a team also makes them a project member.
- Removing someone from the project also removes their disciplines and team
  memberships in it.

## What to watch for

- **Removing someone from the organization** is not available in
  **Organization settings**. To stop a person's access to the installation,
  an instance administrator can deactivate their account (reversible) in the
  [admin console](/organization/instance#people); people can also
  [delete their own account](/basics/account#delete-your-account). Removing
  them from projects and disciplines takes away their capabilities there,
  not their organization membership.
- **Changing an owner** has no control in Organization settings yet. Plan who
  owns an organization when it is created.
- **Invitations by username** reach only people who already have an account.
  With the installation's sign-up mode set to `closed`, invitations by email
  reach existing accounts only too.
- People who leave keep their name on what they contributed, shown as a
  former member.

## Tips

- Invite with the project and disciplines filled in. A new reviewer who
  lands in Engineering of the right project can be asked to review on day
  one.
- Keep two administrators, so access never depends on one person.
- Use disciplines, not the administrator role, to let someone review or
  edit specifications.
