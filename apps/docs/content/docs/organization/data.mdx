---
title: Export and delete an organization
description: Take a complete copy of an organization's data, what the archive contains, and how scheduled deletion and its cancellation work.
---

Your organization's data is yours. Administrators can take a complete copy
at any time, to keep a record, to leave the installation or to move to
another one. Owners can delete the organization; deletion waits for a grace
period during which it can be cancelled.

## Export the data

<Steps>
<Step>

### Start the export

Go to **Organization settings → Data export** and choose **Export data**.
Organization administrators only. One export runs at a time; while it is
being prepared the button says **Export in progress**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Wait, or leave

The archive is prepared in the background, so you can leave the page. Large
organizations take longer; nothing else slows down meanwhile.

</Step>
<Step>

### Download it

When it is ready, **Download** saves the archive. Archives can be downloaded
for 7 days, then they expire; the list of exports stays as history.

</Step>
</Steps>

![Organization settings, Data export: what an export contains, Export data, and No exports yet](/screenshots/org-export.webp)

Starting and downloading exports are recorded in the
[audit log](/organization/audit).

### What the archive contains

A complete copy of the organization as of one moment:

- the organization, its logos, members with their application role, former
  members by name, and invitations;
- projects, project members, disciplines, teams, labels, milestones, work
  item types, workflow statuses, iterations and their history, and every
  version of each Definition of Ready;
- work items, relations and links to Jira issues;
- every specification revision, branch, commit and pull request; reviews and
  approvals, each with the revision it applies to; readiness checks and
  sign-offs; suggestions; discussions with every message; decisions;
  activity;
- spaces, pages with every version, branches, page pull requests and their
  reviews, page discussions, and live drafts as they stand;
- knowledge links: pages attached to work items, the page content items were
  created from, page updates proposed from items, and context pages;
- attachments (the files themselves when file storage is configured);
- Jira connections (without credentials), Lictory Intelligence runs and
  context;
- the audit log.

Not included: anyone's private Litto conversations, personal notifications
and preferences, and credentials of any kind (passwords, sessions, tokens,
Jira credentials).

The archive is a `.tar.gz` of JSON Lines files (one per kind of record), a
`work-items.csv` for spreadsheets, the attachment files, and a
`manifest.json` that lists everything in it. Identifiers are kept exactly,
so references between files (an approval to its revision, a reply to its
discussion) resolve within the archive.

<Callout title="Import is not available yet">
  The format is designed so an archive can be imported later, but Lictory cannot
  import one today.
</Callout>

## Delete an organization

Only owners can delete an organization.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Export first

**Organization settings → Delete organization** shows the latest export, or
says there is none, with a link to **Data export**. Take one if you might
need anything later.

</Step>
<Step>

### Confirm with the handle

![Organization settings, Delete organization: what scheduling the deletion does, Export the data first, the field for the organization’s handle, and Schedule deletion](/screenshots/org-delete.webp)

Type the organization's handle where the page asks for it and choose
**Schedule deletion**.

</Step>
</Steps>

Scheduling locks the organization at once: nobody can open its projects or
change anything in it, and every member is told by email. Members still see
it in their list of organizations, with the date it will be deleted.

After the grace period (30 days, unless your installation sets another),
its projects, work items, specifications and every revision, reviews,
approvals, discussions, decisions, spaces and pages with their history,
attachments, Jira connections with their credentials, and its audit log are
permanently deleted.

### During the grace period

- **Owners** can still start and download data exports, and **Cancel
  deletion**, which opens the organization again as it was.
- **Administrators and members** see the locked page and can ask an owner
  to cancel.
- Work that was already queued (a Jira hand-off, an analysis) still
  finishes; nothing new starts.
- **Instance administrators** see that the deletion is scheduled, but
  cannot cancel it or bring it forward.

### What remains afterwards

- People's accounts, with their other organizations, are not affected.
- The installation's history keeps a record that the organization existed,
  who scheduled its deletion and what was removed.
- Lictory does not revoke the Jira token at Jira: the Jira administrator
  revokes it there.

## What to watch for

- If you are the organization's **only owner** and delete your account after
  scheduling the deletion, it still happens on its date, but nobody can
  cancel it any more.
- The export does not leave the server by itself: download it and store it
  where your company keeps records.
- Archives are kept in the database until they expire; a very large export
  takes database space for those days.

## Tips

- Export before every big change (switching delivery to Jira, a large
  reorganization), not only before deletion. It costs nothing to keep a copy.
- Before deleting, tell members a few days ahead and give them the export
  date, so nobody is surprised by the locked page.
