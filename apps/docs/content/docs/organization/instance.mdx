title: Instance administration
description: Manage your installation's accounts, instance administrators and organizations from the admin console, and understand the access policy.

One Lictory installation serves one company. **Instance administrators**
manage the installation itself: who has an account, who else administers
the installation, and which organizations exist. They are different from
organization administrators, who run one organization
([organization and access](/organization)).

The two are independent. Being an instance administrator gives no access to
any organization's work: an instance administrator who is not a member sees
nothing inside it, and adding themselves is a recorded action. An
organization owner has no say over the installation.

The first instance administrator is created when the server is installed;
with shell access to the server, `sudo lictory admin grant <email>` appoints
one ([terminal guide](/self-hosting/terminal)).

## The admin console

Instance administrators open it from their name at the bottom of the
sidebar → **Admin console** (or `https://<your host>/admin`). Agents cannot
use it: installation administration happens only in the browser or on the
server.

![The admin console: Overview with counts of accounts, administrators, organizations and pending invitations, and the access policy](/screenshots/admin-console.webp)

### Overview

Counts of **Accounts**, **Instance administrators**, **Organizations** and
**Pending invitations**, warnings when something needs attention (anyone can
sign up on an open installation, no outbound email is configured, only one
person administers the installation), and the **Access policy** below.

### People

Every account on the installation. Search by name, email or handle and
filter by **Everyone**, **Active**, **Administrators** or **Deactivated**.

- **New account** creates an account for any email address, optionally as
  an **Instance administrator**, with a password you share privately, or
  without one (they sign in with single sign-on or choose a password with
  "Forgot password").
- For an account: **Make administrator** or **Remove administrator**,
  **Send password reset link**, **Set a new password** (which signs them out
  everywhere), and **Deactivate…** or **Reactivate**.

**Deactivating** signs a person out everywhere and refuses every sign-in
method and every agent token from then on. Nothing else changes: their
memberships, history, reviews and approvals stay, so **Reactivate** restores
the account as it was. You cannot deactivate yourself, and the console
refuses to remove or deactivate the last active instance administrator.

The console does not delete accounts; people
[delete their own](/basics/account#delete-your-account). A deleted account
is listed as **Deleted**, and nothing about it can be changed.

### Organizations

Every organization, with its owners. **New organization** creates one and
makes the person you name its owner; it is set up exactly like one created
in guided setup. **Add** puts someone in an organization as a member,
administrator or owner; the organization's own audit log records it,
naming you.

Organizations scheduled for deletion show their date (or **Being
deleted**). Instance administrators cannot cancel or bring forward a
deletion; only the organization's owners can cancel it.

### Activity

Accounts created, deactivated and reactivated, administrators granted and
removed, organizations created, and organization deletions scheduled,
cancelled and completed, whether done in the console or on the server.
Entries cannot be edited or deleted.

## Access policy

The operator sets the policy in the server's configuration, so it is
reviewed and deployed like the rest of the setup; the console shows it
read-only.

| Setting                   | Choices                                                                                                             | Default                 |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **New accounts**          | By invitation only (a pending invitation is needed), closed (only instance administrators create accounts), or open | By invitation           |
| **Email domains**         | The domains self-service and single sign-on accounts must use                                                       | Any domain              |
| **New organizations**     | Instance administrators create them, or anyone with an account                                                      | Instance administrators |
| **Organization deletion** | The grace period before a scheduled deletion happens                                                                | 30 days                 |

The console also shows the **Sign-in** methods the installation offers and
whether **Outbound email** is configured.

The rules apply in this order: accounts an administrator creates are always
allowed; every other new account must use an allowed domain; your company
identity provider decides who gets an account through single sign-on; and
everyone else follows the sign-up mode. The server enforces this for every
sign-in method; the app only hides what would fail. The settings are in
[configuration](/self-hosting/configuration#access-policy).

## History

The installation keeps its own history, which no organization owns: the
activity above, and what it must remember after an organization is gone (who
scheduled its deletion and what was removed). It names the administrator or
the server's command line, never passwords.

## Tips

- Keep at least two instance administrators, so access never depends on one
  account.
- Prefer **Deactivate** to anything permanent when someone leaves: it is
  reversible, and their history stays attributed.
- Leave sign-up on invitation and organization creation with
  administrators unless the installation is on a private network for
  evaluation.
