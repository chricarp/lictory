Most sign-in settings belong to people and to the installation, not to an
organization:

| Setting                                      | Who sets it                 | Where                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Password, two-factor, passkeys, sessions     | Each person                 | [Account settings](/basics/account#sign-in-and-security)                                               |
| Require two-factor for an organization       | Organization administrators | **Organization settings → Security** (below)                                                           |
| Single sign-on, Google and Microsoft sign-in | The installation's operator | The server's configuration ([sign-in and SSO](/self-hosting/configuration#sign-in-and-single-sign-on)) |
| Who may create an account or an organization | The installation's operator | The [access policy](/organization/instance#access-policy)                                              |

## Require two-factor authentication

In **Organization settings → Security**, switch on **Require two-factor
authentication**. Members without it are asked to turn it on before they
can open the organization: they stay members and can sign in and set it up,
but see none of the organization's work until they do. Setting it up lifts
the hold at once.

- You need two-factor on your own account first, so whoever turns the
  requirement on can still get in.
- The section lists every member and whether they have two-factor on, and
  says how many cannot open the organization yet.
- Turning the requirement off is always possible for an administrator who
  is not held.
- It applies to password sign-ins. Google, Microsoft and company single
  sign-on rely on the provider's own checks; use your identity provider's
  multi-factor policy for those.
- A passkey does not count as two-factor authentication.

The requirement is enforced on the server for everything: the app, agents
connected through MCP and Litto. Email notifications are not affected.

## Reset a member's two-factor

When someone has lost both their authenticator and their backup codes, an
administrator can remove their second factor: in the member list, choose
**Reset two-factor**, then **Confirm reset**. Confirm the person's identity
outside Lictory first, for example by a call.

- Their password and passkeys stay. If the organization requires two-factor,
  they set it up again before they can open it.
- You need two-factor yourself, and cannot reset your own (use Account
  settings). Only an owner can reset an owner.
- Turning the requirement on or off and every reset are recorded in the
  [audit log](/organization/audit).

## Single sign-on

An installation can let people sign in with your company's identity
provider over OpenID Connect (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Keycloak,
Authentik, Google Workspace and others). It is set up once for the whole
installation by its operator; the sign-in page then shows **Continue with**
followed by your provider's name. An installation can also turn password
sign-in off so everyone signs in through the provider.

Single sign-on decides who can sign in; it grants nothing inside Lictory.
Organization membership still comes from invitations, and roles and
disciplines from administrators. People connect the provider to an existing
account from **Account settings → Connected accounts**.

<Callout title="Not available">
  Lictory has no per-organization single sign-on, no SAML, and no SCIM or group
  synchronization. Groups in your identity provider do not create memberships or
  disciplines.
</Callout>

## Sessions

Each person sees how many devices are signed in and can **Sign out other
sessions** from Account settings. Changing a password signs out every other
session. An instance administrator who deactivates an account signs it out
everywhere ([instance administration](/organization/instance#people)).

## Name, handle and logos

**Organization settings → General** holds what every member sees:

- **Organization name**, shown in the sidebar and wherever the organization is named.
- **Handle**, the organization's part of every address
  (`lictory.example.com/northwind`). Changing it changes every link:
  previously shared links stop working, so share the new ones.
- **Logomark**, a square symbol (at least 64×64), shown when the sidebar is
  collapsed and in the list of organizations; and **Full logo**, a wide logo
  that includes the name (about 6:1) and replaces the name in the sidebar.
  Logos are PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 512 KB. Without one, the initial is
  shown.

## Tips

- Require two-factor once your administrators have it, then watch the
  member list: it shows exactly who is still held.
- If your company uses single sign-on, ask your operator to configure it and
  use the provider's multi-factor policy; that covers every sign-in, not
  only passwords.
- Change the organization handle rarely, and before you share many links.
