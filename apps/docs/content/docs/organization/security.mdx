Most sign-in settings belong to people and the installation, not an
organization:

| Setting                                      | Who sets it                 | Where                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Password, two-factor, passkeys, sessions     | Each person                 | [Account settings](/basics/account#sign-in-and-security)                                               |
| Require two-factor for an organization       | Organization administrators | **Organization settings → Security** (below)                                                           |
| Single sign-on, Google and Microsoft sign-in | The installation's operator | The server's configuration ([sign-in and SSO](/self-hosting/configuration#sign-in-and-single-sign-on)) |
| Who may create an account or an organization | The installation's operator | The [access policy](/organization/instance#access-policy)                                              |

## Require two-factor authentication

In **Organization settings → Security**, switch on **Require two-factor
authentication**. Members without it stay members and can sign in, but see
none of the organization's work until they set it up; doing so lifts the
hold at once.

- You need two-factor on your own account first.
- The section shows each member's two-factor state and how many cannot open
  the organization yet.
- Any administrator who is not held can turn the requirement off.
- It applies to password sign-ins. Google, Microsoft and company single
  sign-on rely on the provider's own checks; use your identity provider's
  multi-factor policy for those.
- A passkey does not count as two-factor authentication.

The server enforces it for the app, agents connected through MCP and Litto.
Email notifications are not affected.

## Reset a member's two-factor

When someone has lost both their authenticator and backup codes, confirm
their identity outside Lictory (a call, for example), then choose **Reset
two-factor** in the member list and **Confirm reset**.

- Their password and passkeys stay. Where two-factor is required, they set
  it up again before opening the organization.
- You need two-factor yourself, and cannot reset your own (use Account
  settings). Only an owner can reset an owner.
- Requirement changes and resets are recorded in the
  [audit log](/organization/audit).

## Single sign-on

The installation's operator can let people sign in with your company's
identity provider over OpenID Connect (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Keycloak,
Authentik, Google Workspace and others), once for the whole installation.
The sign-in page then shows **Continue with** and the provider's name.
Password sign-in can be turned off so everyone uses the provider.

Single sign-on decides who can sign in and grants nothing inside Lictory:
membership comes from invitations, roles and disciplines from
administrators. People connect the provider to an existing
account from **Account settings → Connected accounts**.

<Callout title="Not available">
  Lictory has no per-organization single sign-on, no SAML, and no SCIM or group
  synchronization. Groups in your identity provider do not create memberships or
  disciplines.
</Callout>

## Sessions

Each person sees how many devices are signed in and can **Sign out other
sessions** from Account settings. Changing a password signs out every other
session. An instance administrator who deactivates an account signs it out
everywhere ([instance administration](/organization/instance#people)).

## Name, handle and branding

**Organization settings → General** holds what every member sees:

- **Organization name**, shown in the sidebar and wherever the organization is named.
- **Handle**, the organization's part of every address
  (`lictory.example.com/northwind`). Changing it breaks every previously
  shared link.
- **Logomark**, a square symbol (at least 64×64), shown when the sidebar is
  collapsed and in the list of organizations; and **Full logo**, a wide logo
  that includes the name (about 6:1) and replaces the name in the sidebar.
  Without one, the initial is shown.
- **Browser icon**, a square favicon (at least 48×48) for the browser tab
  while someone works in the organization. Without one, the tab shows the
  logomark, or Lictory's icon when there is none.
- **Brand color**, one color (for example `#1d4ed8`) that tints primary
  buttons and accents for everyone in the organization. Lictory adjusts the
  shade so text stays readable in light and dark mode; the preview shows the
  result before you save. **Reset** returns to Lictory's colors. People with
  their own color theme in Account settings keep it.

Images are PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 512 KB.

The organization's invitations and notification emails show its full logo
(or logomark beside its name) and use the brand color for buttons and links.
Verification and password-reset emails belong to the account, so they keep
Lictory's look, as do the sign-in pages.

## Tips

- Require two-factor once your administrators have it, then watch the
  member list for who is still held.
- With single sign-on, rely on the provider's multi-factor policy; it covers
  every sign-in, not only passwords.
- Change the organization handle rarely, and before you share many links.
