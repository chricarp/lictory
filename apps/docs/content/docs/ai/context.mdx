**Lictory Intelligence Context** is what your organization tells Lictory
Intelligence once, so people do not have to repeat it in every discussion:
product vocabulary, rules, conventions and constraints. Entries are written
notes or uploaded documents. They can apply to the organization, a project
or a team.

Context grounds [AI reviews](/ai/reviews). It does not change their rules or
how they report, and it is never quoted as evidence.

## When to use it

- Terms reviewers keep defining: "A capture is final once the gateway
  confirms it."
- Rules every specification must respect: "Every payment API is idempotent
  on the client's request ID."
- Standards a team works to: accessibility level, supported browsers, the
  definition of a breaking API change.

For documentation people read and edit together, use
[Lictory Spaces](/spaces). For one conclusion about one item, record a
[decision](/specifications/decisions).

## Scopes

![Project settings, Intelligence context of Payments: Write note and Upload document, the Project · Payments scope, the character budget, and No project context yet](/screenshots/project-context.webp)

| Scope            | Applies to                       | Managed in                                                                | Who can change it                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Organization** | Every review in the organization | **Organization settings → Lictory Intelligence**                          | Organization administrators                                  |
| **Project**      | Reviews of the project's work    | **Project settings → Intelligence context**, or the organization settings | The project's administrators and organization administrators |
| **Team**         | Reviews of the team's work       | Same as its project                                                       | Same as its project                                          |

Every member can read the context. The server checks who may change it.

A review of work in a project and team receives the organization's active
entries, the project's and the team's. When they conflict, the narrower
scope wins: team over project, project over organization. Project settings
say how many organization entries also apply (**Also applies: N
organization entries**).

## Add an entry

<Steps>
<Step>

### Choose the scope

In organization settings, choose it under **Context scope**
(**Organization**, **Project · Payments**, **Team · Checkout**). In project
settings, choose the project or one of its teams.

</Step>
<Step>

### Write a note or upload a document

- **Write note**: give it a **Title** and the **Content (Markdown)**, then
  choose **Add note**. State facts and rules plainly.
- **Upload document**: give it a **Title**, choose the **Document** and
  choose **Upload**. PDF, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, OpenDocument, RTF, EPUB,
  CSV, Markdown and text files up to 4 MB are converted to text on your
  server.

</Step>
</Steps>

Conversion happens on your server; documents are not sent anywhere to be
read. Scanned PDFs without text are rejected, because reading them would
need an outside OCR service: upload a text-based version. Lictory keeps the
converted text with the file's name, format, size and checksum, not the
uploaded file.

Each scope holds at most 50 active entries, and an entry at most 100,000
characters.

## Revise an entry

Open an entry and choose **Edit**, then **Save new version**. For a
document, choose **Replace file**. Every change adds a version; earlier
versions are kept unchanged.

**History (N)** lists the versions with who saved them and when. Opening an
earlier one says **Viewing version 2. Lictory Intelligence uses version 3.**

## Archive and restore

Choose **Archive** to stop an entry from reaching Lictory Intelligence. Its
versions stay. **Show archived (N)** lists archived entries; **Restore**
brings one back.

## How reviews use context

- **Budget.** A review receives at most 40,000 characters of context across
  all scopes. Narrower scopes are kept first. An entry that does not fit is
  shortened or left out, and the review records that. The meter beside the
  scope shows how much of the budget its active entries use.
- **Citations.** A finding names the entries it relied on (**Based on**).
  A review lists every entry it received and its version (**Grounded in N
  Lictory Intelligence Context entries**), marked **partly included** when
  shortened. So "what was Lictory Intelligence told?" stays answerable after
  the context changes.
- **Proposals.** Drafting proposals with Litto uses the same organization and
  project context.

Lictory Intelligence never proposes edits to context entries.

## What reaches the AI provider

Nothing is sent while Lictory Intelligence is off for your organization.
When it is on, Lictory sends the provider only what each activity needs:

| Activity                                        | What is sent                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Assessment of a work item](/ai/reviews)        | The specifications of the item, its parents and children (truncated when long), the first messages of their open discussions, statuses, estimates and relations, and the applicable context. |
| [Pull request review](/ai/reviews)              | The same for the item, the pull request's changed lines, and the applicable context.                                                                                                         |
| [Litto](/ai/litto)                              | Your messages, with the page context of side-panel messages and any text you selected, and the results of what Litto reads for you with your permissions, such as a page it opens.           |
| [Drafting proposals](/ai/litto#draft-proposals) | The material you provide, the project's discipline names and descriptions, excerpts of recent decisions and approved or delivered revisions, and the applicable context.                     |
| [Dictation](/ai/litto#dictate)                  | Your voice, streamed from your browser straight to the provider. Lictory neither records nor stores it.                                                                                      |

What is never sent:

- content you cannot read. A page you lost access to is withheld from Litto,
  including from earlier turns of a conversation;
- live drafts, which are not history yet;
- credentials, sessions and tokens.

Requests ask the provider not to store the content. Which provider is used,
and its terms, is set by whoever runs your server: see
[privacy](/self-hosting/privacy) and
[configuration](/self-hosting/configuration#lictory-intelligence).

## What to watch for

- **Context is not authority.** It informs reviews; it does not override an
  approved specification or a recorded decision.
- **Keep it short and current.** Long entries crowd out narrower ones within
  the budget, and outdated rules produce outdated findings. Archive what no
  longer applies.
- **Everyone can read it.** Do not put anything in context that some members
  must not see.

## Tips

- Start with five or six notes: the product's key terms and the rules people
  restate most often in discussions.
- Put team conventions on the team, not the organization. They then win
  where they apply and stay out of other teams' reviews.
- When a finding cites an entry that is wrong, fix the entry: the next
  review uses the new version.
