**Litto** is the assistant of [Lictory Intelligence](/ai). Ask it about the
item, page or board in front of you. It can find what your team agreed,
summarize discussions and draft proposals from notes. It can also prepare
changes for people to review.

Litto works as you, with exactly your permissions. It asks before anything
that is a decision or hard to undo.

![Litto answering “What do our pages say about the acquirer report?”: it quotes the sentence from version 1 of Payments service and lists the page it read under the answer](/screenshots/litto-chat.webp)

## When to use it

- **Catch up.** "Where does PAY-2 stand?", "Summarize the discussion",
  "What needs my review?"
- **Check an agreement.** "What did we decide about capture retries?" Litto
  searches [product knowledge](/search/product-knowledge) and cites what it
  found.
- **Find gaps.** "What is missing here?" on a passage of a specification.
- **Turn material into work.** Paste meeting notes or a customer request and
  ask for proposals.
- **Prepare a change.** "Propose acceptance criteria for PAY-3 as a pull
  request." Litto opens it; reviewers decide.

## Open Litto

- **Beside any page**: choose **Ask Litto** at the end of the page header, or
  press `Ctrl J` (`⌘ J` on a Mac). On wide screens Litto opens beside the
  page, which narrows; on phones it opens as a sheet over the page. Press
  `Esc` or **Close Litto (Esc)** to close it.
- **On a text selection**: select text in a specification or a published page
  and choose **Ask Litto** in the toolbar that appears.
- **From the command palette**: type a question and choose **Ask Litto:
  “…”**, which sends it right away ([search](/search)).
- **On its own page**: choose **Litto** in the sidebar. Its page lists your
  conversations. With Lictory Intelligence on, it is also your organization's
  home page.

Litto is only available when Lictory Intelligence is on for your
organization ([turn it on](/ai#turn-it-on-for-your-organization)).

## Ask about what you are looking at

Each message you send from the side panel carries what you were looking at:
the page, the project, the open work item, the text you selected and the
review you were reading. The panel's header says so (**About PAY-2**,
**About the selection**, **On Board**), and the context is shown above your
message in the conversation.

![Litto opened beside a published page with the selected passage attached to the message as context](/screenshots/litto-page-context.webp)

- **A selection travels with your next message.** It appears above the
  composer as **Sent with your next message**. Remove it with **Remove the
  selection from the message**.
- **Pages are asked about as the version on screen.** On a published page
  that is its published version; on an earlier version or a branch, that
  version. A live draft is never sent: it is not history yet.
- **Suggestions follow the page.** On an item: **Where does this stand?**,
  **Break it down**, **What is still unclear?**, **Draft acceptance
  criteria**. On a page: **Summarize this page**, **Draft proposals from this
  page**, **Which work items does this page mention?** and **Compare with
  PAY-2** for an item the page cites. On a selection: **What does this
  mean?**, **Does this match what was agreed?**, **Draft a proposal from
  this**. The board, backlog, iterations, Spaces and the Inbox have their own.

Context only names what you were looking at. Litto still reads it with your
access, so context never shows Litto anything you cannot read.

## Answers cite exact versions

![Litto’s answer beside the page: “Version 1 of Payments service mentions PAY-1, PAY-2.”, the step it worked through, and Read this page naming the page and its version](/screenshots/litto-page-answer.webp)

Litto answers from what it reads, not from memory:

- It searches [product knowledge](/search/product-knowledge) before it
  describes how the product behaves. It weighs approved and delivered
  revisions above decisions, and decisions above pages. When a page
  disagrees with an agreement, it names both.
- It cites the revision number of a specification and the version of a page,
  and links to that exact version.
- Each step that searched or read pages lists them with their space and
  version. An answer that rests on pages lists them under the answer in
  **Pages used** (for example **Payments · version 3 · reviewed**). Each link
  opens the version Litto read, not today's page.
- It says when a point is its own inference rather than something recorded.

## Access is checked on every message

Litto checks your access again each time you send a message, for the page
in the context and for everything earlier in the conversation:

- If you lost access to a page since you asked about it, Litto is told the
  page is unavailable and does not see its title or the text you quoted.
- Earlier answers and steps that used that page are withheld from what is
  sent to the AI provider, so they cannot leak into a new answer.
- Your own copy of the conversation is unchanged; only what is sent to the
  provider is filtered.

Text from pages and tools reaches Litto marked as data written by people,
never as instructions to follow.

## What Litto can do for you

Litto uses the same operations you can perform in the app, limited by your
disciplines and access in each project. For example, it can:

- read items, specifications, discussions, reviews, boards, iterations and
  pages, and answer questions about how work is going;
- create work items and propose changes to specifications through a
  [branch and pull request](/specifications/branches), including a proposed
  breakdown;
- propose a page update for review ([page branches](/spaces/branches));
- start discussions and request reviews;
- record a [decision](/specifications/decisions), only when you ask for it.
  It does not resolve the discussion the decision came from.

### Approve its actions

Some actions need your approval in the conversation before they run: those
that record a human decision (approve, merge, resolve, publish to a delivery
tool) and those that cannot easily be undone. Litto shows a card with the
action, what it applies to and everything it would send, in full:

> _This is a decision or cannot be undone easily. It runs as you, only if you
> approve._

Choose the action's button (for example **Record decision**) to run it as
you, or **Decline**. If a step fails, the answer leads with it under **Steps that did not
complete**, so a reply never claims more than happened; **Ask Litto what to do
instead** adds a follow-up with the error to your draft. Litto does not approve,
merge, resolve discussions or publish unless you asked it to, and even then
it waits for you.

### Questions and recommendations

- When something is ambiguous, Litto asks instead of guessing, one question
  at a time, with choices where it can. Press a choice's letter to pick it
  and `Enter` to continue; optional questions can be skipped. Answer and
  choose **Submit answers**, or dismiss the questions and answer in the chat.
  While a question, recommendation or approval waits, the composer becomes a
  bar with **View request** (back to it) and **Cancel this turn**.
- When it notices a change you did not ask for (split a story, add a missing
  criterion), it shows a **Recommendation** with its evidence and waits.
  Choose **Accept** or **Not now**.

### Follow and steer an answer

- **Its work stays visible.** While Litto works, each step appears as a line
  (for example **Searching product knowledge**, then **Searched product knowledge**), and **Thinking** shows
  while it decides what to do next. Open a line to see what the step found,
  and its raw request and response. An answer you watched keeps its steps
  open, so nothing moves while you read; in a saved conversation they are one
  line, such as **Worked for 14s · 6 steps · 2 changes**. What it changed, and
  any step that did not complete, is listed under the answer.
- **Your place is kept.** Your question settles near the top and the answer
  grows below it. Scroll up and the conversation stops moving; **Jump to
  latest** brings you back and follows the answer again. A saved
  conversation reopens at your last question.
- **Act on a message.** **Copy** an answer or your own message, **Retry**
  the latest answer, or **Edit in the composer** to send a question again
  with changes. **Stop generating** (or `Esc` in the composer) ends an answer early.
  Code in an answer has its own **Copy code**.

### What stays with you

Some things are never done through Litto, by policy: credentials and access
tokens, account security, organization membership and administrator roles.
Litto tells you where to do them in the app.

## Draft proposals

Litto turns rough material (meeting notes, customer requests, support
tickets, documents, a page) into **proposed** proposals. Nothing is created
until you choose.

### Give it the material

Paste or describe it in a conversation and ask for proposals. On a page,
choose **Draft proposals from this page** in the side panel. Or select text
on a published page and choose **Create work item**, then **Draft with
Litto** and **Draft proposals**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Read the drafts

![Draft proposals with Litto: the selected text, the project, and two drafted proposals to choose from, each quoting its sentence, with Cancel, Draft again and Choose drafts to create](/screenshots/litto-draft-proposals.webp)

Each draft has a title, the problem and expected outcome as the sources
state them, open questions, suggested reviewer disciplines and related
product knowledge. Every draft quotes the sources it rests on; a quote that
does not appear word for word in the source is dropped, and so is a draft
without one. What Litto inferred is listed separately from what it read.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose what to create

Tick the drafts you want and create them (**Create 1 proposal**). They become
ordinary proposals in refinement. A proposal drafted from a page remembers
the page, the version and the passage it came from
([work from pages](/knowledge/in-pages)).

</Step>
</Steps>

Drafting needs permission to edit specifications in the project. It reads at
most 60,000 characters across ten sources and says when it truncated them.
Images are not supported yet. The material is sent to the AI provider for
this one request and not stored; only the proposals you create are saved.

## Manage conversations

Conversations are private to you. The Litto page lists them under
**Conversations**, grouped by **Pinned**, **Today**, **Yesterday**,
**Previous 7 days**, **Previous 30 days** and **Older**.

- **New chat** starts a conversation (`Ctrl Shift O`, `⌘ ⇧ O` on a Mac).
- **Search conversations** filters the list.
- The menu on a conversation offers **Rename**, **Pin** (or **Unpin**) and
  **Delete**. Choose **Delete** twice to confirm; deleting stops any answer
  still in progress.
- Conversations started in the side panel stay out of the list until you
  choose **Open in Litto**.

Answers keep going when you close the panel or the browser: the server
works on them, and the conversation shows where it stands (**Working**,
**Waiting for your input**, **Completed**, **Stopped with an error**).
**Stop generating** stops the answer; an action that already started may
still finish. When an answer stopped with an error, **Retry** continues from
the last completed step. Litto waits as long as needed for your answer to a
question or approval.

## Dictate

Choose **Dictate** in the composer, or press `Ctrl Shift Space` (`⌘ ⇧ Space`
on a Mac), and speak. Words appear where your cursor was, and you can keep
editing while you talk. Nothing is sent until you send it. `Esc` cancels:
dictated words you have not edited are removed.

Your audio goes straight from your browser to the AI provider and is neither
recorded nor stored by Lictory.

## What to watch for

- **Litto acts as you.** A pull request Litto opens is yours, and you cannot
  approve a change you contributed to. Several agents using your identity do
  not count as independent reviewers.
- **Approve with care.** The approval card shows exactly what will run.
  Decline anything you did not intend.
- **Answers are proposals.** Check cited versions before you rely on an
  answer, especially for behavior nobody recorded.
- **Live drafts are invisible to Litto.** Commit or publish the text first, or
  paste it into the conversation.

## Tips

- Select the exact passage before you ask "What is missing here?". Answers
  about a selection are short and specific.
- Ask "Does this match what was agreed?" on a page passage to compare it
  with the approved specification it cites.
- Pin the conversations you return to, such as a weekly "What needs me
  first?" in the Inbox.
- When Litto finds a gap, ask it to open a pull request with the fix rather
  than editing the specification yourself. Reviewers see exactly what
  changed.
