Work rarely stands alone. A Story waits for an API change, a Bug duplicates
another report, two Tasks touch the same code. **Relations** record those
links so nobody has to remember them, and **parents** group work into a
hierarchy.

## Relations

A relation is a directed link between two work items. There are three
kinds:

| Relation       | Seen from the other item | Use it when                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Blocks**     | **Blocked by**           | One item cannot finish before the other.      |
| **Duplicates** | **Duplicated by**        | Two items describe the same work.             |
| **Related to** | **Related to**           | The items are connected, without an ordering. |

Relations can link items across projects and teams of the organization.

![The Relations section of PAY-2: Blocks PAY-4 Refund status in the customer portal, and Related to PAY-5 Duplicate capture after gateway timeout, each with its status, and Link](/screenshots/detail-relations.webp)

### Add a relation

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the item

Scroll to **Relations**, below the item's description and knowledge.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose Link

Choose **Link**, pick the kind (**Blocks**, **Related to** or
**Duplicates**), then search by title or identifier and pick the other item.

</Step>
</Steps>

To remove a relation, point at it and choose **Remove relation**. You need a
discipline that can **Change properties** in the project.

### What to watch for

- **No loops of two.** If PAY-3 already blocks PAY-4, PAY-4 cannot also
  block PAY-3: _These items already block each other the other way round._
- **Relations inform; they don't enforce.** A blocked item can still be
  started. The preview and the item show what blocks it, so the team can
  decide.

## Parents and children

Every item can have one parent of a higher level in the same project: a
Story under an Epic, a Task under a Story, a Subtask under a Task. Set it
with **Parent** in the item's **Properties**, or add children from the
parent's **Children** section. See
[break down a specification](/delivery/breakdown).

Parents show their children's progress (`2/4 done`) and the sum of their
estimates. Moving an item to another parent keeps its identifier, status and
iteration.

## Preview an item

On the board, the backlog and team backlogs, clicking an item opens a
**preview** beside the list instead of leaving it. The preview lets you plan
without opening every item:

- change **Status**, **Owner**, **Priority**, **Estimate**, the **Sprint**
  (or **Cycle**) and the **Team**;
- see a specification's **Readiness checks**, **Required approvals** and
  open discussions, and whether starting it would be blocked by the
  [readiness policy](/delivery#readiness-policy);
- see **Subtasks done** and the items it is **Blocked by**, each one a click
  away.

![The preview of PAY-2 beside the backlog: status, owner, priority, estimate, sprint and team, the pages about the item, and its readiness checks, required approvals and discussions](/screenshots/detail-item-peek.webp)

**Open** opens the full item page. `Esc`, **Close preview** or a click
outside closes it. Clicking another item swaps the preview. The previewed
item is in the address, so a reload or a shared link opens it again. Hold
`Ctrl` or `⌘` while clicking to open the item in a new tab instead.

## Tips

- Record a **Blocks** relation as soon as you find a dependency, even across
  projects. During planning, the preview's **Blocked by** list shows what has
  to move first.
- Mark duplicates instead of archiving them silently: the link explains to
  whoever finds the duplicate later where the work went.
- Plan a sprint from the backlog with previews: set estimates, priority and
  the sprint one item after another without leaving the list.
