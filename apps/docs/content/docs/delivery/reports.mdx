title: Iteration reports
description: Read an iteration's burndown, scope changes and velocity, and understand why a completed report never changes.
---

Every sprint and cycle has a **report**: what the team committed to, what
it finished, what was added or removed along the way, and how the work
burned down day by day.

Open it from the backlog: **Report** on the active iteration, or a finished
iteration under **Completed**. Completing a sprint also offers **View
report**.

![The Sprint 1 report: Committed at start, Completed, Added after start and Removed after start, the burndown chart with its dashed ideal line, and the sprint’s context page under Pages](/screenshots/delivery-iteration-report.webp)

## What the report shows

- **Totals**: **Committed at start**, **Completed**, **Added after start**,
  **Removed after start** and, once the iteration is completed, **Carried
  over**. Each shows points and item count.
- **Burndown**: remaining points per day. The dashed line is the ideal pace
  from the committed scope to the last day; the upper line shows the scope
  as it changed. The chart appears once the iteration has started.
- **Velocity**: committed and completed points of up to eight recent
  completed iterations of the project.
- **Pages**: the iteration's [context pages](/knowledge/context-pages), with
  **Add page** and **Create iteration notes**.

## How the numbers are made

Reports are not computed from today's state of the items. Every change that
matters to an iteration is recorded when it happens, in the same step as the
change itself: an item added or removed, an estimate changed, an item
completed or reopened. The burndown replays those records.

When an iteration is completed, its totals are frozen. Editing an item
afterwards, re-estimating it or moving it elsewhere does not rewrite a
finished sprint's report.

## Points or items

Reports count points when the project uses estimates, and items when it
doesn't (**No estimates** in **Project settings → Planning**). When a
project uses estimates but nothing in the iteration is estimated yet, the
burndown counts items and says so. Velocity needs estimates; without them
it is not shown.

Estimates in T-shirt sizes are stored as points (XS 1, S 2, M 3, L 5, XL 8),
so they burn down and add up like any other points.

## What to watch for

- **Late estimates count as scope change.** Estimating an item after the
  sprint started raises the scope line. Estimate before starting for a
  clean burndown.
- **Reopened work comes back.** Reopening a completed item in an active
  iteration adds its points back to what remains.
- **Velocity is the project's.** On a backlog narrowed to a team, sprints
  don't compare their plan with it: it is not one team's capacity.
- **Jira projects have no reports here.** Iterations are planned in Jira in
  external mode.

## Tips

- Read **Added after start** at the retrospective: frequent additions
  usually mean work started before it was ready.
- Link the retrospective notes to the iteration so the report and its
  conclusions stay together. See
  [iteration notes](/delivery/iterations#iteration-notes).
