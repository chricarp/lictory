---
title: Deliver the work
description: Choose where a project's work is delivered, and turn a ready specification into an accepted, delivered agreement.
---

Delivery is where an agreed specification gets built. Each project chooses
one place to deliver:

- **In Lictory**: a board, a ranked backlog, sprints or cycles, estimates and
  reports, next to the specifications.
- **In Jira**: Lictory refines, reviews and approves; ready work is handed
  off to Jira, which runs delivery. See [Deliver in Jira](/integrations/jira).

Exactly one system owns delivery state. A native project's status, assignee
and iterations live in Lictory; a Jira project's status and assignee live in
Jira and are only read back. Refinement, reviews, approvals and readiness work
the same in both modes.

## Choose the delivery mode

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the project's delivery settings

Project administrators open **Project settings → Delivery**, section
**Where work is delivered**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Pick a mode

- **Deliver in Lictory**: board, backlog, sprints or cycles and estimates;
  readiness can gate starting spec-backed work; nothing is handed off.
- **Hand off to Jira**: ready work is handed off to one Jira project; status
  and assignee are read back; iterations and estimates are planned in Jira.

</Step>
<Step>

### Confirm the switch

Switching to Jira turns off iterations and estimates and replaces the board
and backlog with the **Hand-off** view. Items, specifications and history are
kept, and you can switch back. Complete any active sprint or cycle first so
its report is frozen and its unfinished work has a home.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Ready is not accepted

- **Ready for delivery**: the project's readiness checks pass for the
  current revision. The work _can_ be selected. See
  [readiness](/review/readiness).
- **Accept for delivery**: the decision that the team will build it,
  recording the exact revision.

Planning an item into a sprint or giving it a **Todo** status does not accept
it; the plan stays tentative until someone accepts the scope.

An accepted item shows **Accepted for delivery** and its revision. When it
moves to a done status, Lictory records that revision as **Delivered**. Both
stay visible on the item and in its [version history](/specifications/history).

## Accept a specification for delivery

Native projects only. You need a discipline that can **Change properties**
in the project.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Find ready work

The **Ready** tab of **Refinement** lists specifications whose checks pass
and that nobody has accepted yet. The backlog links to it with **Choose ready
work →**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Accept the revision

![PAY-7 Refund reason codes, accepted for delivery: the workflow card shows Ready, revision 1 and 4 of 4 checks, and Accepted revision 1 under Delivery; both required reviews approved revision 1](/screenshots/accepted-scope.webp)

Open the item and choose **Accept revision N for delivery** on its workflow
card. Revision N becomes the team's agreement. An item in a backlog status
moves to the workflow's first unstarted status (**Todo** by default).

</Step>
<Step>

### Plan and build it

Plan it into a [sprint or cycle](/delivery/iterations), break it down into
[child items](/delivery/breakdown) and move it across the
[board](/delivery/board).

</Step>
</Steps>

### Accepting automatically

Moving an item into a started status (such as **In Progress**) records the
current revision as accepted when no agreement exists yet. Moving it straight
to a done status records it as accepted and delivered at once. The readiness
policy applies to this first commitment as it does to the button.

### Plain work

Items without a specification, such as Tasks and Bugs, have no refinement;
their scope is recorded when they start. A plain item under a specification
follows that specification's readiness, so splitting unsettled behavior into
Tasks does not get around the gate.

### Epics

Higher-level items (Epic by default) are delivered through their children:
their Stories and Tasks appear on the board and backlog, they don't. An Epic's
own specification is still refined, reviewed, approved and accepted. Its
workflow card offers **View breakdown**.

## When the specification changes later

![The workflow card of PAY-2 after revision 3: “Revision 3 changes the accepted specification. Accept it to change what the team delivers.”, Compare with accepted revision, Accept revision 3 for delivery, and the warning that accepting now records an exception because readiness checks are incomplete](/screenshots/detail-scope-amendment.webp)

The accepted revision stays the agreement while a later one is refined;
changing the specification never moves it silently. Delivery continues with
the accepted revision, and the workflow card offers:

- **Compare with accepted revision**: both revisions side by side in the
  version history.
- **Accept revision N for delivery**: once the team agrees to build the
  change.

An amendment that is not ready does not stop the item or its children from
starting. Missing evidence on the accepted revision itself, such as a new
blocking discussion on it, still applies the policy.

A done or canceled item cannot accept a new revision; reopen it first.
Completing it again records the new delivered revision; the earlier one stays
in its activity.

## Readiness policy

The readiness policy decides what happens when work that is not ready
starts or is accepted. Project administrators set it in **Project settings →
Definition of Ready**, under **When work starts before it's ready**:

| Choice    | What happens                                                                                                                                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Allow** | Readiness is informative only.                                                                                                                    |
| **Warn**  | The default. The move or acceptance goes through, the failing checks are recorded in the item's activity, and the item shows it was an exception. |
| **Block** | Only ready specifications can start. The board refuses the move, and the accept button stays disabled until the checks pass.                      |

In Jira projects the policy does not apply: only ready specifications are
handed off.

## What to watch for

- **Jira projects accept by handing off.** There is no **Accept for
  delivery** in external mode; the handed-off revision is the scope boundary.
  See [Deliver in Jira](/integrations/jira).
- **Agents ask first.** Litto and MCP agents cannot start or complete a
  specification without an accepted revision; they ask you to accept the
  scope. Plain work keeps its fast path. See [agents](/integrations/agents).
- **Old items may have no recorded scope.** Items delivered before scope was
  recorded say _No delivery scope recorded_.
  Lictory does not guess what was agreed.
- **Done is not deployed.** Delivered records the revision the team accepted
  and finished. It does not claim the change was deployed or verified.

## Tips

- Accept when the team commits, not when work is merely planned; the record
  then answers "what did we agree to build?".
- Review amendments with **Compare with accepted revision** before accepting
  them.
- Keep goals and runbooks one click away from the work with
  [context pages](/knowledge/context-pages) on the board, backlog and each
  sprint.
