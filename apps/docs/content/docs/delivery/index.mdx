---
title: Deliver the work
description: Choose where a project's work is delivered, and turn a ready specification into an accepted, delivered agreement.
---

Refinement ends with a specification the team agrees on. Delivery is where
that agreement gets built. Each project chooses one place to deliver:

- **In Lictory**: a board, a ranked backlog, sprints or cycles, estimates and
  reports, next to the specifications.
- **In Jira**: Lictory refines, reviews and approves; ready work is handed
  off to Jira, which runs delivery. See [Deliver in Jira](/integrations/jira).

Exactly one system owns delivery state for a project's work. A native
project's status, assignee and iterations live in Lictory. A Jira project's
status and assignee live in Jira and are only read back. Nothing is tracked
twice. Refinement, reviews, approvals and readiness work the same in both
modes.

## Choose the delivery mode

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the project's delivery settings

Project administrators open **Project settings → Delivery**. The section
**Where work is delivered** offers two choices.

</Step>
<Step>

### Pick a mode

- **Deliver in Lictory**: board, backlog, sprints or cycles and estimates;
  readiness can gate starting spec-backed work; nothing is exported.
- **Hand off to Jira**: ready work is exported to one Jira project; status
  and assignee are read back; iterations and estimates are planned in Jira.

</Step>
<Step>

### Confirm the switch

Switching to Jira turns off iterations and estimates for the project and
replaces the board and backlog with the **Hand-off** view. Items,
specifications and history are kept, and you can switch back later. If a
sprint or cycle is active, complete it first so its report is frozen and its
unfinished work has a home.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Ready is not accepted

Two different facts tell you where a specification stands:

- **Ready for delivery** means the project's readiness checks pass for the
  current revision. It is eligibility: the work _can_ be selected. See
  [readiness](/review/readiness).
- **Accept for delivery** is the decision that the team will build it. It
  records the exact revision it accepted.

Planning an item into a sprint or giving it a **Todo** status does not
accept it. Until someone accepts the scope, the plan stays tentative.

Once a revision is accepted, the item shows **Accepted for delivery** and the
revision it covers. When the item moves to a done status, Lictory records
that revision as **Delivered**. Both stay visible on the item and in its
[version history](/specifications/history).

## Accept a specification for delivery

Native projects only. You need a discipline that can **Change properties**
in the project.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Find ready work

The **Ready for delivery** queue on **Refinement** lists specifications whose
checks pass and that nobody has accepted yet. The backlog links to it with
**Choose ready work →**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Accept the revision

![PAY-7 Refund reason codes, accepted for delivery: the workflow card shows Ready, revision 1 and 4 of 4 checks, and Accepted revision 1 under Delivery; both required reviews approved revision 1](/screenshots/accepted-scope.webp)

Open the item. Its workflow card says **Ready for delivery** and offers
**Accept revision N for delivery**. Accepting records revision N as the
team's agreement. An item still in a backlog status moves to the first
unstarted status of the workflow (**Todo** by default).

</Step>
<Step>

### Plan and build it

Plan it into a [sprint or cycle](/delivery/iterations), break it down into
[child items](/delivery/breakdown) and move it across the
[board](/delivery/board).

</Step>
</Steps>

### Accepting automatically

You don't have to accept by hand. Moving an item into a started status (such
as **In Progress**) records the current revision as accepted when no
agreement exists yet. Moving it straight to a done status records it as
accepted and delivered at once. The project's readiness policy applies to
this first commitment exactly as it does to the button.

### Plain work

Items without a specification, such as Tasks and Bugs, have no refinement.
Their scope is recorded when they start. A plain item under a specification
follows that specification's readiness: splitting unsettled behavior into
Tasks does not get around the gate.

### Epics

Higher-level items (Epic by default) are delivered through their children.
They don't appear on the board or the backlog; their Stories and Tasks do.
An Epic's own specification is still refined, reviewed, approved and
accepted like any other. Its workflow card offers **View breakdown** to see
the work below it.

## When the specification changes later

![The workflow card of PAY-2 after revision 3: “Revision 3 changes the accepted specification. Accept it to change what the team delivers.”, Compare with accepted revision, Accept revision 3 for delivery, and the warning that accepting now records an exception because readiness checks are incomplete](/screenshots/detail-scope-amendment.webp)

An accepted revision stays the agreement while a later revision is refined.
Changing the specification never moves the agreement silently.

When the current revision differs from the accepted one, the item says so:
_Revision N changes the accepted specification. Accept it to change what the
team delivers._ The workflow card says delivery continues with the accepted
revision, and offers:

- **Compare with accepted revision**, which opens the two revisions side by
  side in the version history;
- **Accept revision N for delivery**, once the team agrees to build the
  change.

Work on the accepted revision keeps going in the meantime: the amendment
losing readiness does not stop the item or its children from starting.
Missing evidence on the accepted revision itself, such as a new blocking
discussion on it, still applies the policy.

Delivered work is history. A done or canceled item cannot accept a new
revision; reopen it first. Completing the reopened item records its new
delivered revision, and the earlier one stays in its activity.

## Readiness policy

The project's readiness policy decides what happens when work that is not
ready starts or is accepted. Project administrators set it in **Project
settings → Readiness**, under **When work starts before it's ready**:

| Choice    | What happens                                                                                                                                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Allow** | Readiness is informative only.                                                                                                                    |
| **Warn**  | The default. The move or acceptance goes through, the failing checks are recorded in the item's activity, and the item shows it was an exception. |
| **Block** | Only ready specifications can start. The board refuses the move, and the accept button stays disabled until the checks pass.                      |

With **Warn**, an accept button for work that is not ready is marked as an
exception: _Readiness checks are incomplete: accepting now records an
exception._ With **Block**: _Complete the readiness checks before accepting
this revision._

In Jira projects the policy does not apply: only ready specifications are
handed off at all.

## What to watch for

- **Jira projects accept by handing off.** There is no **Accept for
  delivery** in external mode; the handed-off revision is the scope boundary.
  See [Deliver in Jira](/integrations/jira).
- **Agents ask first.** Litto and MCP agents cannot start or complete a
  specification that has no accepted revision. They ask you to accept the
  scope, which you confirm. Plain work keeps its fast path. See
  [agents](/integrations/agents).
- **Old items may have no recorded scope.** Items delivered before scope was
  recorded say _No delivery scope was recorded for this historical item._
  Lictory does not guess what was agreed.
- **Done is not deployed.** Delivered records the revision the team accepted
  and finished. It does not claim the change was deployed or verified.

## Tips

- Accept on purpose. Accept a revision when the team commits to it, not when
  it is merely planned; the record then answers "what did we agree to
  build?".
- Review amendments before accepting them. **Compare with accepted
  revision** shows exactly what the change adds to the team's work.
- Keep goals and runbooks one click away from the work with
  [context pages](/knowledge/context-pages) on the board, backlog and each
  sprint.
