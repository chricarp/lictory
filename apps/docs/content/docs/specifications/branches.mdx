title: Proposed changes
description: Propose a change to a specification, ask the right disciplines to review the exact result, resolve conflicts and apply it as a new revision.

A **proposed change** is a separate draft of one specification: you and the
collaborators you invite edit it while the current revision stays as it is.
You save **versions** of it as you go, then **send it for review**. Once the
required reviewers approve the exact result, someone who can edit
specifications **applies** it, and it becomes the specification's next
revision.

The behavior is Git-inspired (a proposed change is a branch, a version a
commit, its review a pull request, applying it a merge), but you never need
those words to use it.

![A proposed change on PAY-7, “Add chargeback as a reason code”: applying is blocked until the QA and Engineering approvals arrive, no blocking discussions and no conflicts, the required reviewers Pending, and Apply change disabled](/screenshots/work-pull-request.webp)

## When to use it

- **The specification is ready.** It is then protected, and every change is
  proposed and reviewed.
- **You want a change looked at before it lands**, even while the
  specification is still open for direct editing.
- **You want to propose a breakdown** into child items: it is reviewed with
  the specification. See [break down a
  specification](/delivery/breakdown).

### A protected specification

While a specification takes shape, people whose discipline can edit
specifications change it directly in its [live
draft](/specifications/live-drafts). The first time the specification becomes
[ready](/review/readiness), it is **protected** and stays protected: the item
shows **Protected** next to the current revision (_This specification is
ready. Changes are proposed and reviewed before they apply._), and direct
edits are refused. This holds even if the specification later stops being
ready.

## Start a change

Below the specification, a box shows the current revision and **Proposed
changes**, with the number of changes in review. It also lists the changes in
review and your own changes (_Your change · continue editing_).

Under **Start a change**, enter a **Name of the change** (for example

### Start change

**Start change** opens its editor.

</Step>

Two shortcuts start a change for you:

- **Suggest a change** on selected text that has formatting, and **Larger
  change** in the suggestion composer, start a change named `suggestion` and
  open its editor with that text selected. A plain passage is [suggested
  beside the text](/specifications/suggestions) instead.
- **Propose breakdown** opens your `breakdown` change, or starts one, at the
  breakdown editor.

Anyone who can open the item can start a change and read every change.
Nobody is asked to review anything until you send it for review.

## Edit and save versions

![The chargeback-reason change of the protected PAY-7 specification: the steps Edit, Save a version, Send for review and Review & apply, the shared editor, the save box, collaborators and the open review](/screenshots/work-branch.webp)

The change's screen shows where it is: **Edit**, **Save a version**, **Send
for review**, **Review & apply**. Its tabs are **Document** (the editor),
**New edits** (your edits since the last saved version) and **Versions**.

The change has its own live draft: edits are saved as you type and shared
with its collaborators, who can edit at the same time
([live drafts](/specifications/live-drafts)). The specification is not
affected.

When the change is ready to be looked at, save a version:

1. In the save box (**Save a version of** _name_), say **What changed?** and
   why. It is required.
2. Choose **Save version**. The version is an immutable snapshot with your
   name and words.

**Discard changes** throws away the edits that are not in a version yet and
starts the draft again from the last saved version.

### Collaborators

A change belongs to its author. The author invites people with **Invite
collaborator…** and removes them with **Remove**; the author cannot be
removed. Collaborators edit, save versions, bring in newer text and send the
change for review. Anyone else who opens the change reads it (_You're viewing
someone else's proposed change._) and can ask the author for an invitation.

## Send for review

**Send for review** proposes what the change has saved. While you have edits
that are not in a version yet it is unavailable: save or discard them first.

1. Choose what to **Apply to**: the current specification by default, or
   another change. The form says whether it _Applies cleanly_ or has
   _Conflicts with newer text_, which you resolve after sending.
2. Enter a **Title** and a **Description** (required): why the change is
   needed and what reviewers should focus on. **Preview the change** shows the
   diff.
3. Choose **Send for review**, or **Save as draft** to share it before anyone
   can approve it. A draft becomes reviewable with **Ready for review**;
   **Convert to draft** takes it back.

When the change applies to the specification, the required reviews of the
current revision are copied onto it, so the same disciplines are asked again.
Add more with **Add a reviewing discipline…** and **Request** (they are always
required). The review's author and the change's collaborators manage it: they
mark it as a draft or ready, resolve its conflicts and, when their discipline
can request reviews, add reviewers.

New versions of the change are added to its open review automatically and ask
reviewers to approve again.

## Review a proposed change

The review has four tabs:

- **Conversation**: the description, the proposed breakdown and its
  discussions.
- **Versions**: what it brings in.
- **Actions**: the [Lictory Actions](/ai/reviews) that ran on it.
- **Changes**: the resulting title and the changes, word by word, against the
  current specification (the same view as [comparing
  revisions](/specifications/history)), with the breakdown below.

Under **Reviewers**, each requested discipline shows **Required**, who may
decide (_Any Engineering member_ or one person) and its state: **Pending**,
**Approved**, **Changes requested** or **Review required again**. A member of
the discipline decides with **Approve** or **Request changes**, and can
**Revoke** that decision.

### Approvals bind to the result

An approval records the exact result the reviewer inspected: the resulting
title, content and breakdown. Approving a change to the specification
approves the whole specification as it reads once the change applies, not
only the change, so applying it records that discipline's approval of the new
revision; the reviewer row says so beside **Approve**. _Approvals apply to the
exact result, the whole specification once this change applies; applying
records them as approvals of revision 4. A new version on either side asks
reviewers again._ A change that leaves the result identical keeps the
approval.

People who saved versions of the change cannot approve it (_Contributors
cannot approve their own changes._), unless they are the organization's only
member. Another member of the discipline approves instead.

### Discuss a change

Discussions on a review belong to it, not to the specification's panel. Start
one in **Conversation**, or with **Discuss this change** in **Changes**. Tick
**Blocks applying** for something that must be settled before the change
applies. These discussions are attached to the change's latest version.

## Apply

The requirements box lists everything still in the way, and says **Applying
is blocked** or **Ready to apply**:

| Requirement                                    | What to do                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| _This review is a draft._                      | Choose **Ready for review**                                 |
| _Request at least one required review._        | Request a reviewing discipline                              |
| _Engineering approval is pending_ (or _stale_) | The discipline approves this exact result                   |
| _A reviewer requested changes._                | Save a fix; the reviewer approves or revokes their decision |
| _Blocking discussions remain open._            | Resolve the review's blocking discussions                   |
| _This change conflicts with newer text._       | **Resolve conflicts** (see below)                           |
| _This change has nothing left to apply._       | The specification already says it all; delete the review    |

A project may also require a passing Lictory Actions check.

**Apply change** is available, once nothing is in the way, to people whose
discipline can edit specifications (to the specification) or to the other
change's collaborators (to another change). Applying a change to the
specification:

- creates the next revision of the specification (_Applying makes this result
  revision 5 of the specification._);
- records every valid approval of the review as an approval of that revision,
  so nobody approves the same text twice
  ([approvals](/review/approvals#carried-approvals));
- creates or changes the child items of a reviewed breakdown;
- closes the change, unless another open review still uses it.

The item's activity says what each new revision did to the approvals in
force: which became outdated, or that they carried over because the text did
not change.

Applying to another change adds a version to that change only.

## Conflicts

When the change and the specification both changed since the change started,
Lictory combines edits that do not overlap. Overlapping edits become
conflicts, which are never combined silently. The change cannot be reviewed or
applied until they are resolved.

1. In the requirements box, choose **Resolve conflicts** (the author and the
   change's collaborators).
2. Each conflict shows three columns: **Base** (where both started), your
   change and the current specification. Choose **Keep mine**, **Keep
   theirs**, **Keep both**, or **Merge manually** to edit the text. A title
   conflict offers **Use my title** and **Use their title**; a breakdown
   changed on both sides asks you to keep one complete breakdown.
3. Choose **Save the resolution to** _name_. **Keep all my changes** and
   **Discard my changes** settle every conflict at once.

The resolution is a new version of the change, so reviewers approve the new
result. If either side moved while you were resolving, Lictory refuses rather
than guess (_The change or the specification changed. Inspect the updated
result before continuing._).

### Bring in newer text

A long-lived change can take in what changed in the specification meanwhile:
under **Bring in newer text**, choose where from and **Compare & update**.
Without conflicts it adds a version; with conflicts you resolve them in the
same way, then **Save the update**.

## Delete

- **Delete change** (its author, or anyone whose discipline can edit
  specifications) removes it from the workspace. Its versions and activity
  stay in history. A change in review cannot be deleted: apply or delete the
  review first.
- **Delete review** (its author, or a specification editor) removes a review
  that has not applied; its open discussions are resolved so none is left
  hanging. An applied review stays on the item: it records who approved which
  result.

A deleted change is not restored: start a new one instead.

## Agents and Lictory Intelligence

Litto, Lictory Intelligence and agents connected through
[MCP](/integrations/agents) use the same proposed changes. Their tools keep
the Git names (`createBranch`, `createPullRequest`, `mergePullRequest`):

- Lictory Intelligence proposes **corrective changes** from the findings you
  select in an [item review](/ai/reviews), one per item, and invites you as a
  collaborator. It never approves or applies.
- An agent acts with its person's permissions: it starts changes, saves
  versions and sends them for review, but Lictory refuses it every approval
  and every apply. The approval is the person's.

## What to watch for

- **Specification changes and page pull requests differ.** A specification's
  reviewers are disciplines and its contributors may approve when they are the
  only member; pages ask named people and have no such exception. See [page
  branches](/spaces/branches).
- **Only saved versions are reviewed.** Edits not in a version are never part
  of a review.
- **One review per change and target.** Sending the same change for review
  twice is refused.
- **There is no close or reopen.** Convert a review to a draft to take it out
  of review, or delete it.
- When a change and the specification have no single common version, Lictory
  cannot combine them. Start a new change from the current specification and
  make the edit there.

## Tips

- Keep one concern per change. A small change is reviewed quickly, and its
  approval is less likely to go stale.
- Write the description for the reviewer who was not in the conversation:
  what changes, why, and what to look at first.
- Bring a long-lived change up to date before asking for review, so reviewers
  approve the result that will actually apply.
- When a review finds a gap in a page your specification cites, propose the
  page update too ([knowledge in work](/knowledge/in-work)).
