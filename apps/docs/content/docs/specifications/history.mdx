title: Versions and changes
description: See every revision of a specification with who wrote it and who approved it, compare any two word by word, read an old one and restore it.
---

Every saved change to a specification is a **revision**: an immutable
snapshot of its title and text, with its author, time and change summary.
Revisions are never edited afterwards, not by later edits, restores or
merges. That is what lets a team answer, months later: _what did
Engineering approve, and what changed afterwards?_

## Open the history

- **Changes** in the item header compares the current revision with the
  one you last looked at (or the one before it). On a phone it is
  **Compare revisions** in the **More actions** menu.
- In the review rail, the **Revision** section shows the current revision
  and offers **vs. revision N** (the previous one), **Since my approval
  (rN)** when you approved an earlier revision, and **History**.
- When the text changed since your last visit, a notice above the title
  says so: _Changed since you last looked: revision 2 → 4 · +12 −3 lines_.
  **View changes** opens that comparison.

**Close history** returns to the specification. The history has two views:
**Versions**, described here, and **Branches & pull requests**, described in
[branches](/specifications/branches).

## The list of versions

Each revision shows:

- its number (**r1**, **r2**…) and change summary, or _First revision_ /
  _No summary_ when none was written;
- who saved it and when;
- markers: **Current**, **Accepted for delivery** (the revision the team
  agreed to build), **Delivered** (the revision recorded when the work was
  done) and **Approved:** with the disciplines whose approval of that
  revision is still valid.

Select a revision to see it on the right, with the approvals given on it:

| Approval state                                          | What it means                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **valid**                                               | The approval still applies: this is the revision being approved                  |
| **outdated** (_Specification revised (revision N)_)     | The text changed in revision N, so the reviewer needs to look again              |
| **carried to revision N: specification text unchanged** | A merge changed only the breakdown; the same approval was recorded on revision N |
| **superseded by the merged pull request's approval**    | A pull request's approval for the same discipline replaced the carried one       |
| **revoked**                                             | The reviewer withdrew it                                                         |

So an approval always says which revision it belongs to: _Engineering · Dev
Patel approved revision 2 · outdated (Specification revised (revision 3))_.
See [approvals](/review/approvals).

## Compare two revisions

A selected revision is compared with the one before it. **Compare with**
picks any other revision, older or newer, or **Read this revision** to read
it on its own.

![Revision 2 of PAY-2 compared with revision 1: Versions on the left, and on the right edited blocks shown Before · Revision 1 with “three” struck out and After · Revision 2 with “up to four”, “and jitter” and the new page citations highlighted, unchanged blocks folded](/screenshots/work-word-diff.webp)

- The summary counts the **blocks changed**: paragraphs, headings, lists,
  tables, quotes or code blocks.
- Each change is labelled **Edited**, **Added** or **Removed**. An edited
  block shows **Before** and **After**, each labelled with its revision, and
  marks the exact words that changed. A one-word fix reads as a one-word
  fix.
- Long runs of unchanged blocks fold into one line (_4 unchanged blocks_);
  open it to read the context.
- A changed title is compared word by word above the text.
- **Show full text** reads the whole revision with the changes in place;
  **Show changes** returns to the changed blocks only.

If two revisions have the same text, the comparison says _No text changes
between these revisions._ (for example after a rename).

## Read an old revision

Choose **Read this revision** in **Compare with** to read a revision in
full, as it was saved, with its references resolved for you now.

A [discussion](/specifications/discussions) whose text has since changed
links to the revision it was written on: the history opens that revision
with the quoted passage marked, so you can read what the comment referred
to. If the passage cannot be found, the history says _The quoted text is
not in this revision._

## Restore a revision

People whose discipline may _edit specifications_ can restore an earlier
revision while main is not protected:

1. Select a revision other than the current one and choose **Restore
   revision N**.
2. Read the confirmation: _Restore revision N as a new main revision.
   Existing approvals will require review again._
3. Choose **Confirm restore revision N**, or **Cancel**.

Restoring never rewrites history. It saves a **new revision** with the old
revision's title and text and the summary _Restore revision N_. Every
revision in between stays in the list. Like any new revision, it marks
approvals outdated and re-requests the reviews, and it replaces the live
draft for anyone editing it.

Once the specification has been ready, main is protected and the history
says _Main is protected. Use a branch and pull request to restore earlier
content._ Make the change on a [branch](/specifications/branches) instead.

## Link to a version

The address of the history names what is on screen: the revision shown, the
one it is compared with, and the discussion whose quote is marked
(`?history=…&compare=…&thread=…`). Copy the address to send someone exactly
that comparison; **Branches & pull requests**, a branch and a pull request
have addresses of their own.

## What to watch for

- **"Changed since you last looked" is per browser.** Lictory remembers the
  last revision you saw on each device. On a new browser the notice starts
  from the revision you open, or from your most recent approval when that is
  newer.
- **Properties are not in the history of versions.** Status, owner,
  estimate and similar changes create no revision; they appear in the item's
  activity instead.
- **Restore copies, it does not rewind.** Decisions, discussions and
  approvals of the revisions in between stay where they were.

## Tips

- **Start a re-review from your approval.** **Since my approval (rN)**
  shows exactly what changed after you approved, so you review the
  difference instead of rereading the whole specification.
- **Link comparisons in discussions.** When you ask _"is this still
  right?"_, paste the address of the comparison so others see the same
  change you do.
- **Write summaries for the list.** The list of versions shows each
  summary first; a clear one makes the history readable at a glance.
