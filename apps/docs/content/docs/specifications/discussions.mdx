A **discussion** (a thread) is attached to what it is about: a passage of the
specification, or the specification as a whole. It keeps the revision it was
written on, so nobody has to ask what a comment referred to, even after the
text has changed many times.

![The PAY-2 specification with the Discussions panel open beside it: three open discussions, one blocking, in document order, and the blocking one’s quoted passage tinted in the text](/screenshots/work-discussions-panel.webp)

## When to use it

- Ask a question about one sentence, flag a missing case or point out a
  contradiction: comment on the passage.
- Raise something that concerns the whole specification (scope, a missing
  section): start a discussion on the whole specification.
- Mark a discussion **blocking** when the specification must not become ready
  until it is settled.

To propose new wording rather than talk about it, change the text on a
[branch](/specifications/branches) instead, or select the passage and choose
**Suggest a change**.

## Start a discussion

<Steps>
<Step>

### Select the text

In the specification's reading view, select the passage you mean. A small
toolbar appears with **Comment on selection**. You can also press
`Ctrl Alt M` (`⌘ ⌥ M` on a Mac).

</Step>
<Step>

### Write your comment

The **Discussions** panel opens with the quoted passage above the composer.
Write what is unclear or wrong. Type `@` to mention someone; they are
notified. The **×** beside the quote removes it, turning the comment into a
discussion of the whole specification.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose whether it blocks

Tick **Blocks readiness** when the point must be settled before the
specification can become ready. Then choose **Post** (or **Post blocking
discussion**). The composer shows the revision the discussion will belong to.

</Step>
</Steps>

To discuss the whole specification, use the composer at the foot of the
panel (_Ask a question, flag a gap, or raise a concern…_). Pressing
`Ctrl Alt M` without a selection opens it.

### What a discussion remembers

A discussion on selected text records three things about the passage: the
quoted words, the heading they are under, and which copy of those words under
that heading you selected. Repeated phrases therefore never point at the wrong
place. Discussions are written on the current revision: the reading view, not
the [live draft](/specifications/live-drafts) you edit. Save your edits as a
revision before you ask people to comment on them.

## The Discussions panel

The **Discussions** button in the item header counts the open discussions of
the current revision, with a red dot when some block readiness (_2 open
discussions · 1 blocking readiness_). It opens the panel:

- **On a wide screen**, beside the specification, next to the review rail.
  When only one of them fits, the panel takes the rail's place and **Review &
  details** opens the rail as a drawer.
- **On a phone**, as a sheet from the bottom. `Escape` closes it.

Lictory remembers in this browser whether you keep the panel open. Until you
choose, it opens by itself beside a specification that has open discussions,
when the screen is wide enough.

The panel lists:

1. discussions whose passage is in the current revision, in the order of the
   text;
2. discussions whose text has changed or been removed;
3. discussions **On the whole specification**;
4. resolved discussions, folded under **Show N resolved**.

![A blocking discussion card: the Blocking badge, the section it is on, the revision it was written on, the quoted passage and Reply, Resolve, Resolve as decision and Mark non-blocking](/screenshots/detail-discussion-thread.webp)

Commented passages are tinted in the text, red for blocking discussions.
Pointing at a passage or a discussion marks the other more strongly; clicking
a tinted passage selects its discussion, and clicking the quote in a card
scrolls to the passage. `Alt ↑` and `Alt ↓` move between discussions
(`Alt Home` and `Alt End` to the first and last).

Each card says what it is on (_on “Failure behavior”_) and links to the
revision it was written on, where the quote is marked.

## When the text changes

A discussion is never moved to other text. In each new revision Lictory looks
for the quoted words under the same heading:

- **Found**: the discussion stays attached to the passage.
- **_The quoted text changed_**: the heading is still there but the words
  are not (for example _The quoted text changed since revision 2_). The card
  keeps showing the original quote.
- **_Original text removed_**: the heading is gone too.

In both cases the quote is shown in grey and the revision link opens the text
as it was. If the words moved to another section and occur exactly once in
the revision, the discussion follows them; if they occur several times, it is
not attached to any of them.

![A discussion whose quoted text changed: on “Behavior”, revision 1, the warning “The quoted text changed since revision 1” above the original quote “three times with exponential backoff”](/screenshots/detail-detached-anchor.webp)

## Reply, resolve and reopen

- **Reply** adds a message (`Ctrl Enter` / `⌘ Enter` sends).
- **Resolve** closes the discussion. **Reopen** opens it again.
- **Mark blocking** and **Mark non-blocking** change what it holds back.
- **Resolve as decision** resolves it and opens the decision form with its
  last message as the starting point; **Record decision** does the same for a
  resolved discussion. See [decisions](/specifications/decisions).

Resolving never deletes anything. Messages cannot be edited or deleted, and
the item's activity records who resolved or reopened a discussion and when it
became blocking or non-blocking.

### Blocking discussions

An open blocking discussion fails the readiness check _No blocking discussion
is open_ (part of the default [Definition of Ready](/review/readiness)). The
panel says _Blocking discussions hold readiness back until they are
resolved._ When a ready specification gets a new blocking discussion, it is
no longer ready until the discussion is resolved.

## Comments on plain work

Plain work (a Task or a Bug, say) has no specification to anchor comments to.
Its **Discussions** section sits under the description as one conversation:
start a discussion, reply, resolve and reopen in the same way. Comments on
plain work cannot be blocking, because plain work has no readiness checks.

## Who can do what

Anyone who can open the item can start discussions, reply, resolve, reopen and
mark discussions blocking or non-blocking. No discipline is needed. An
archived item's discussions are read-only.

## Notifications

| Notification                    | Who receives it                            | Default        |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------- |
| Mentioned                       | People mentioned in a message              | In app + email |
| Blocking discussion             | People involved in the item                | In app + email |
| New discussion                  | People involved in the item (non-blocking) | In app         |
| Reply                           | People who took part in the discussion     | In app         |
| Discussion resolved or reopened | People who took part in the discussion     | In app         |

You are involved in an item when you created it, are its assignee, requested
or were named to review it, wrote in one of its discussions, or watch it.
Change the defaults in [notification settings](/inbox/notifications).

## What to watch for

- **Discussions belong to revisions, not to the live draft.** You cannot
  comment inside the editor while you type; comment in the reading view.
- **Pull request discussions stay on the pull request.** They are about the
  proposed result, block merging rather than readiness and do not appear in
  the item's panel. See [branches and pull
  requests](/specifications/branches#discuss-a-change).
- **A blocking discussion holds back readiness only where the project's
  checks include it**, as they do by default.
- Selecting text needs a pointer. Keyboard users start a discussion of the
  whole specification, and move between existing discussions with `Alt ↑`
  and `Alt ↓`.

## Tips

- Quote the smallest passage that carries your point. A short quote survives
  later edits of the surrounding text.
- Use blocking sparingly: it says "this must be settled before anyone commits
  to building it", not "I have a comment".
- When a long discussion ends in an agreement, choose **Resolve as decision**
  so the conclusion outlives the thread.
- Pages in Lictory Spaces work the same way; see [page
  comments](/spaces/comments).
