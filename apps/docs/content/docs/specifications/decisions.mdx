---
title: Record decisions
description: Keep the conclusions a team reaches during refinement as durable records, with their author, revision and source discussion, and cite them in specifications and pages.
---

Discussions are transient; conclusions should not be. A **decision** is a
durable record of something the team agreed (_Retries reuse the original
idempotency key_), with who recorded it, the revision it was recorded on and,
when it came from one, the discussion that led to it. Months later anyone can
find it, read why, and see which text it was agreed against.

![A decision on PAY-2: “Retries reuse the original idempotency key”, its conclusion, Maria Chen and revision 1, with buttons to add it to a page and to archive it](/screenshots/detail-decisions.webp)

## When to use it

- A discussion ends in an agreement that later readers will need.
- A question was settled in a meeting or elsewhere, and the specification
  should keep the answer.
- An approach was deliberately ruled out (_Cancellation after settlement is
  unsupported_).

Recording a decision **neither approves the specification nor resolves a
discussion by itself**. It records a conclusion; reviews and readiness stay
separate.

## Record a decision from a discussion

<Steps>
<Step>

### Resolve it as a decision

On an open discussion, choose **Resolve as decision**. On one that is already
resolved, choose **Record decision**. The Decisions section opens a form
filled with the discussion's last message.

</Step>
<Step>

### Write the conclusion

Enter the **Conclusion** as one sentence a newcomer understands, and the
details: what was agreed, why, and any limits. **Source discussion** names
the discussion it came from.

</Step>
<Step>

### Save it

Choose **Resolve and save decision** (or **Save decision**). The discussion is
resolved only when the decision is saved; **Cancel** leaves it open. The
discussion then shows **Decision recorded**.

</Step>
</Steps>

A decision recorded from a discussion keeps the revision that discussion was
written on, so its context is preserved even after the text has changed.

## Record a decision directly

In the item's **Decisions** section, choose **Record decision**, write the
conclusion and details, and leave **Source discussion** on **Current
revision**. The decision belongs to the current revision. Only resolved
discussions of the specification can be chosen as a source; pull request
discussions cannot.

## Who can record decisions

People whose discipline in the project can edit specifications or review can
record decisions. Specification editors can archive and restore any decision
of the project; the author of a decision can archive or restore their own
while they can still record decisions there.

## Archive and restore

A decision that no longer holds is archived, not deleted: choose **Archive**
on it. It keeps its text and activity, and leaves active product knowledge.
**Show archived decisions** lists archived ones, and **Restore** brings one
back. Archiving an item withdraws its decisions too, and restoring the item
brings them back.

## Decisions in product knowledge

Decisions, the approved ready revision of each specification and its
delivered revision together form the organization's **product knowledge**: a
search people, Litto and MCP agents use, where every result names what kind of
evidence it is and its exact revision.

In an item's Decisions section, **Find related agreements** searches it for
the project: results read **Recorded conclusion**, **Approved intent** or
**Recorded delivered scope**. A recorded conclusion is what a team agreed,
not proof that it was built. See [product
knowledge](/search/product-knowledge).

## Cite a decision

- **In a specification or a page**, type `/` and choose **Decision** to
  search for a recorded decision and insert it. Inside a sentence it reads as
  a chip; alone in its paragraph it becomes a decision card.
- **Add to a page** on a decision proposes a change to a page you can edit
  that appends the decision as a card. It opens a page branch and pull
  request; the published page changes only when the space's reviewers (or its
  editors, in a Direct space) merge it.

![A decision card in a page: the decision’s title and conclusion, the item PAY-2 and the revision it was recorded on](/screenshots/detail-decision-card.webp)

A decision card shows the decision as it is now: its text, its item and
_Recorded on revision N_. The text stays in Lictory Work; the page never
copies it. When the decision is archived, the card says _Archived: withdrawn
from active product knowledge, kept for history_. People who cannot read the
decision see only the title it was inserted with. See [blocks](/spaces/blocks).

## What to watch for

- **A decision is not a requirement.** If the decision changes what must be
  built, change the specification too, so reviewers approve the new text.
- **Decisions are never edited.** When a conclusion changes, archive the old
  decision and record a new one; both stay in the history.
- **A decision on an earlier revision stays on it.** Later revisions do not
  move it.

## Tips

- Write the conclusion as a statement of fact, not a question or a summary
  of the discussion.
- Record ruled-out options as decisions too. They stop the same discussion
  from starting again.
- Keep a **Decision records** page in the project's space and add decisions
  to it with **Add to a page**, so they are found next to the team's other
  knowledge.
