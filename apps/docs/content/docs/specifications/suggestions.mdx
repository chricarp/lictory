A comment says what is wrong; a suggestion says how to fix it, as proposed
text that someone allowed to accept it accepts or rejects. Accepting creates
the next revision; until then nothing suggested is part of the
specification.

## Suggest new wording for a passage

<Steps>
<Step>

### Select the text

In the specification's reading view, select the passage you want to change
and choose **Suggest** in the toolbar that appears.

</Step>
<Step>

### Write what it should say

The suggestion opens beside the text with the passage filled in. Rewrite it,
add why if it helps, and check **Who accepts it**: the item's owner or author
by default, or anyone whose discipline both edits specifications and reviews.
Choose **Suggest**.

</Step>
<Step>

### The person asked accepts or rejects it

The passage is underlined and the suggestion waits beside it; the review
panel counts waiting suggestions. The person asked chooses **Accept** (it
becomes the next revision) or **Reject**. You can **Withdraw** your own. When
nobody was asked, anyone who could accept it can choose **Accept**.

</Step>
</Steps>

Anyone in the organization can suggest, without edit rights. Suggestions
made by an agent through MCP appear the same way.

<Callout title="Comment or change?">
  Not sure what the text should say? Start a
  [discussion](/specifications/discussions) and suggest wording once the
  question is settled, so the change records an agreement, not a guess.
</Callout>

## On a ready specification

Once the specification is ready, **Suggest a change** goes to its reviewers
instead of one person: the button reads **Send for review**, and Lictory puts
exactly that change up for review as a
[proposed change](/specifications/branches). The required reviews approve it
before it becomes the next revision.

## Larger changes

A passage with formatting or that spans several blocks, or a change across
the document, goes to the editor instead: **Suggest a change** then starts a
proposed change named `suggestion` with the passage selected, and the
composer's **Larger change** does the same. You save a version there and send
it for review; the required reviews approve it.

## Changes proposed by Lictory Intelligence

When [Lictory Intelligence](/ai) is on, it can propose fixes, always as
proposals:

- **From an item review.** Select the findings you agree with and choose
  **Propose a corrective change**. Lictory Intelligence applies exactly those
  edits, without another model call, as a proposed change per affected item,
  and invites you as a collaborator. Reviewers decide what applies. See
  [Lictory Intelligence reviews](/ai/reviews).
- **On a proposed change.** A Lictory Intelligence review of a proposed change
  can annotate a finding with a suggested edit. A collaborator on the change
  can choose **Apply to the change** to save it as a new version, where it is
  reviewed with the rest of the change.

Lictory Intelligence never edits the specification directly, approves,
resolves discussions or applies changes.

## What to watch for

- **Accepting is a new revision.** Like any change, it makes earlier
  approvals of the specification outdated and asks the reviews again
  ([approvals](/review/approvals)).
- **A suggestion written on an earlier revision** says so once the
  specification has moved on; its author can withdraw it and suggest again.
- **Once the specification is ready**, suggestions made before can no longer
  be accepted into it; suggest the passage again to send it for review.

## Tips

- Suggest the smallest change that settles the point; focused suggestions are
  decided faster.
- When the change answers a discussion, add why, so the person accepting it
  sees the reason.
