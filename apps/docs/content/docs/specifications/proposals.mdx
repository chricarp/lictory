A **proposal** is the first expression of a change someone wants: the
problem, who has it, what should happen instead, and whatever notes,
screenshots and links you already have. It is expected to be incomplete.
Creating one commits nobody to building it; refinement decides that.

## When to use it

Write a proposal whenever an idea needs the team's agreement before anyone
builds it: a new behavior, a change to an existing one, a customer request
that needs shaping. Work that only needs doing (a chore, a known bug fix)
can be plain work instead, a Task or a Bug without a specification.

## Create a work item

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the create dialog

- In **Proposals** or **Refinement**, choose **New proposal**.
- Anywhere in a project, press `c` (when you are not typing in a field).
- Or use the create button in the sidebar.

![The New proposal dialog: the Story type, No team, a title, an optional parent and a Markdown description, with Create another and Create & open](/screenshots/work-new-item.webp)

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose the type, project and team

The dialog starts with a sensible type for where you opened it:

- from **Proposals** or **Refinement**, only types with a specification are
  offered (Story by default);
- from the **Board**, **Backlog** or **Hand-off**, plain work comes first,
  so the item appears where you created it;
- elsewhere, the dialog starts with Story and offers every type you may
  create.

Change the type, the project (when you are in several) and the team (when
the project has teams) with the buttons at the top.

</Step>
<Step>

### Give it a title, and optionally a parent and a description

Only the title is required. **Parent** links the item to the larger piece
of work it contributes to, such as a Story under an Epic; it is offered when
the project has a type one level above. The **Description** is the first
revision of the specification, written in the [editor](/specifications/editor).

</Step>
<Step>

### Create it

- **Create & open** (`Enter`) creates the item and opens it, so you see the
  next step.
- **Create another** creates it and keeps the dialog open for the next one.
  The dialog counts what you created, and a notice with **Open** lets you
  jump to each.

</Step>
</Steps>

A spec-backed item starts in **Draft** and appears in **Proposals**. When it
is worth the team's time, open it and choose **Start refinement**: it moves
to **In refinement** and into the Refinement view. Requesting a review moves
it to **In review** even if nobody started refinement explicitly. See
[from proposal to ready](/specifications).

<Callout title="Who can create work items">
  Creating a spec-backed item needs a discipline that _edits specifications_ in
  the project; plain work also accepts a discipline that _changes properties_.
  Without either, Lictory says so and names who can help: a project
  administrator. See [disciplines](/organization/disciplines).
</Callout>

## What to put in a proposal

Start with what you know and say what you don't:

- **The problem**: who has it and what happens today.
- **The intended outcome**: what should be true afterwards, from the
  customer's side.
- **Expected behavior**, as far as you know it.
- **Open questions** you already see.
- **What is out of scope**, when it is likely to come up.

Reviewers from Engineering, QA and other disciplines add what they know:
feasibility, failure behavior, test scenarios. The proposal does not need to
answer everything before refinement starts.

### Files and screenshots

Every work item has **Attachments** under its text. Choose **Add files**, or
drop files onto the section. In the editor, paste or drop an image to place
it in the text, or choose **Insert → File or image**: images appear inline,
other files as a link, and both are kept with the item's attachments.

- Files can be up to 25 MB: images, PDF, text and Markdown, CSV, Word,
  Excel, PowerPoint, ZIP, MP4, MOV and MP3.
- Files are private to your organization. Only the person who uploaded a
  file can remove it.
- Attachments need file storage on your installation. Without it, the
  section says _Attachments are unavailable_ and your administrator can
  [configure storage](/self-hosting/configuration#file-storage).
- The create dialog has no file upload: add files once the item exists.

### Links and references

Paste ordinary links to anything outside Lictory. To refer to something in
Lictory, use a reference instead: type `#` for a work item, `[[` for a page
in Spaces, or **/ → Decision** for a recorded decision. References stay
correct when the target is renamed and show its current state. See
[references](/specifications/editor#work-items-pages-and-decisions).

For background that should travel with the item without being part of its
text, attach a page to the item instead; see
[knowledge on a work item](/knowledge/in-work).

## Draft proposals with Litto

When you have rough material (meeting notes, a customer request, a support
ticket), Litto can turn it into draft proposals for you to choose from:

- In a [Litto chat](/ai/litto), paste the material, say which project it is
  for and ask for proposals.
- On a page in Spaces, select the passage, choose **Create work item**, then
  **Draft with Litto** and **Draft proposals**. See
  [work in pages](/knowledge/in-pages).

Each draft has a title, the problem, the expected outcome, open questions,
the disciplines that should review it and related decisions or approved
specifications. It quotes the exact excerpts of your material it relies on
and lists what it inferred rather than read. Citations Lictory could not
verify are removed.

Nothing is created until you choose. You pick the drafts you want, adjust
them, and only those become proposals, with you as their author.

<Callout title="What Litto needs">
  Drafting needs [Lictory Intelligence](/ai) enabled for your organization and a
  discipline that edits specifications. It reads up to 10 pieces of material and
  60,000 characters in total. The material is sent to the configured AI provider
  and is not stored by Lictory.
</Callout>

**Without Lictory Intelligence**, you write proposals yourself; nothing else
changes. If Litto cannot draft right now, the page says _Litto could not
draft proposals right now. You can write the work item yourself instead._

## What to watch for

- **Creating is not committing.** A proposal is a captured idea. Delivery
  starts only when someone accepts a ready revision or hands it off.
- **Plain work skips refinement.** A Task or Bug goes straight to delivery.
  If it sits under a spec-backed parent, that parent's readiness still
  applies.
- **The title is part of the specification.** Renaming a spec-backed item
  saves a new revision, just like changing its text.

## Tips

- **Start rough, early.** A title and three sentences are enough to let
  Engineering and QA see it coming. Polishing alone delays the questions that
  matter.
- **Write the headings readiness looks for.** If your project requires
  sections such as _Acceptance criteria_ or _Failure behavior_, add them as
  headings from the start, even if they are empty. Readiness checks find a
  section by its heading name, at any level, so the proposal shows what is
  still missing. See [readiness](/review/readiness).
- **Cite what you rely on.** When a proposal depends on how something works
  today, link the page that describes it and pin the version you read
  (`[[`, then **Pin this version**). Reviewers then see exactly what you saw.
- **Capture in batches.** After a planning session, use **Create another**
  to record every idea in one go, then refine them one at a time.
