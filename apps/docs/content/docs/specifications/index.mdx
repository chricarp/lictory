---
title: From proposal to ready
description: How a spec-backed work item moves from a rough proposal through refinement and review to ready, and where each part of that work happens in Lictory.
---

Refinement is how a team turns "Product wants to build this" into
"Engineering can safely commit to building this". In Lictory it happens on
one **work item**: the same `PAY-2` starts as a rough proposal, collects
revisions, discussions and reviews, becomes ready, and is then delivered in
Lictory or handed off to a delivery tool.

<Lifecycle />

The path is not a straight line. A specification can go back from review to
refinement, a ready one can stop being ready when its text changes, and
delivered work can be amended by a later revision. Lictory keeps every step
in the item's history instead of hiding the way back.

## Which work items have a specification

Every work item has a **type**. A type either carries a **specification**
or it is plain work:

| Default type | Carries a specification | What that means                                         |
| ------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Epic, Story  | Yes                     | Refined, reviewed, approved and checked for readiness   |
| Task, Bug    | No                      | A description edited directly, then planned and tracked |
| Subtask      | No                      | Plain work under another item                           |

Organization administrators can add and change types; each project enables
the ones it uses. In the create dialog, the type picker marks types with a
specification as **Specification**.

Only spec-backed items go through refinement. A Task still has a history of
its description, but no reviews, approvals or readiness checks of its own,
and comments on it are a conversation under the description.

## The stages

A spec-backed item shows where it stands in the card under its title. The
card names the stage in one sentence, shows the evidence behind it, and says
what happens **Next**:

| Stage      | The card says                                             | What moves it on                                                              |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Proposal   | **Shape the proposal**                                    | **Start refinement** when the idea is worth exploring with the team           |
| Refinement | **Agree on what to build**                                | Requesting a review moves the item to _In review_                             |
| Review     | **Review the current specification**                      | Reviewers approve the current revision; blocking discussions are resolved     |
| Ready      | **Ready for delivery**                                    | Every [readiness check](/review/readiness) passes, so Lictory sets it for you |
| Delivery   | **Accepted for delivery**, **In progress**, **Delivered** | Accepting the revision (in Lictory) or handing it off (to Jira)               |

![PAY-2 "Retry failed card captures" in review: revision 2 of the specification with its review rail, required Engineering and QA reviews and readiness checks beside the text](/screenshots/specification-review.webp)

Under the sentence, the **Spec** row shows the refinement state, the current
revision, how many readiness checks pass (for example _3/5 checks_) and any
blocking discussions. Once a revision is accepted for delivery, a
**Delivery** row adds the workflow status and the accepted and delivered
revisions.

## Refinement state and delivery status

Two separate facts describe a spec-backed item, and Lictory never merges
them into one field:

- **Refinement state** says how far the specification has got: **Draft**,
  **In refinement**, **In review** or **Ready**.
- **Delivery status** says how far the work has got: the project's workflow
  status (Backlog, Todo, In Progress, Done…) in a native project, or the
  delivery tool's status in an external one.

A specification can be **Ready** while its work has not started, and an
item can be **In Progress** while a later revision of its specification is
back **In review**. Both are visible on the item, side by side.

**Ready** is evidence, not a choice. Nobody sets it: it is set when every
readiness check passes for the current revision, and removed (back to **In
review**) when one stops passing. People with the _change properties_
capability can move an item between **Draft**, **In refinement** and **In
review** in the **Refinement** property, but not to or from **Ready** while
the checks say otherwise.

## Main and protected main

A specification's agreed text lives on **main**. While the specification is
taking shape, people whose discipline may _edit specifications_ change main
directly: they open the editor, write together and save revisions. See
[live drafts](/specifications/live-drafts).

The first time the item becomes **Ready**, main is **protected**, and it
stays protected even if the item later leaves Ready. From then on nobody
edits main directly: changes are made on a **branch** and merged through a
reviewed **pull request**, which can also propose the item's breakdown into
child work. See [branches and pull requests](/specifications/branches).

<Callout title="Propose a change from the text">
  Select a passage of a specification and choose **Suggest a change**: Lictory
  starts a branch and opens it at that passage. It works whether or not main is
  protected, so it is also the quickest way to propose a change to a ready
  specification.
</Callout>

## The project's views

The project sidebar groups refinement under **Prepare**:

- **Proposals** holds spec-backed items in **Draft** that have not started
  refinement: ideas captured but not yet worth the team's time. **New
  proposal** creates one.
- **Refinement** holds specifications being clarified, reviewed or ready, and
  not yet in delivery. Its tabs split them: **All in refinement**, **In
  progress**, **In review** and **Ready for delivery**. An item already in
  delivery comes back here only when its specification changes (an
  _amendment_); delivery carries on with the accepted revision meanwhile.
  Above the list, the project's knowledge strip shows its spaces and
  recently published pages.
- **Reviews** opens **Needs my review**: reviews asked of you or your
  disciplines on the current revision, and suggestions waiting for your
  approval. Each row says which review is asked, on which revision, and how
  many blocking discussions are open.

**All items**, under **Browse**, is the catalog of every item of every type
and stage, including done, canceled and (when you ask) archived work. See
[organizing work](/delivery/organize).

![The Refinement view of the Payments project: the All in refinement, In progress, In review and Ready for delivery tabs with their counts, the Payments knowledge strip, and each item with its next step](/screenshots/work-refinement.webp)

Refinement is the project's home page when the project has spec-backed
types. An item opened from one of these views keeps it highlighted in the
sidebar and names it first in the breadcrumb, so one click returns to the
same list.

**Ready for delivery** means the checks pass. It is not a commitment: the
team still decides when to build it, by accepting the revision in a native
project or handing it off in an external one. See
[delivery](/delivery) and [Jira](/integrations/jira).
