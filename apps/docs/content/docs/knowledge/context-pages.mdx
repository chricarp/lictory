title: Context pages
description: Keep the pages a project, a team or an iteration relies on one click away from the board, the backlog and the iteration report, and create iteration notes.

A **context page** is a page a delivery surface keeps at hand: the goal of a
sprint, the design the team is building, the runbook for what it operates,
last iteration's retrospective. Each project, each of its teams and each of
its iterations has a short, ordered list of them. A context page is
context, not delivery state: linking or removing one never moves work.

![The Context popover on the board: the project's pages and Sprint 1's pages, each with its space, Add page and Create iteration notes](/screenshots/detail-context-pages.webp)

## When to use it

- **For an iteration**: its goal and notes during the sprint, its
  retrospective after it.
- **For a team**: the team's working agreement, the runbooks of what it
  operates.
- **For the project**: the requirements, designs and decision records most
  of its work refers to.

For a page that belongs to one work item, [attach it](/knowledge/in-work#attach-a-page)
to the item instead.

## Where context pages show

The **Context** control (a book icon, with the number of pages) sits in the
header of each delivery surface and lists the pages of the lists in view:

| Surface                       | Lists shown                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Board**                     | The project's, the filtered team's and the selected iteration's |
| **Backlog**                   | The project's and the filtered team's                           |
| Each iteration in the backlog | That iteration's, as a compact icon with its count              |
| A team's backlog              | The team's and the project's                                    |
| The iteration report          | The iteration's, listed under **Pages**                         |

Each page shows its title and space and opens the page. You see only pages
you can read, and the count counts only those. An iteration's compact
control is hidden while it has no pages and you cannot add any.

## Add a context page

<Steps>
<Step>

**Open the list.** Choose **Context** on the board, backlog or team backlog,
or the iteration's icon, or go to the iteration report's **Pages**.

</Step>
<Step>

**Add page.** Under the list you want (the project, the team or the
iteration), choose **Add page** and search by title or space (_Add a page to
Sprint 1_).

</Step>
<Step>

**Pick the page.** It joins the end of the list.

</Step>
</Steps>

### Order and remove

Each page in a list has buttons to move it up or down (_Move "…" up_) and
to remove it (_Remove "…" from Sprint 1_). Removing offers **Undo**; the
page's activity keeps who linked and removed it. Put the page people need
first at the top.

## Create iteration notes

An iteration can get its notes page in one step:

<Steps>
<Step>

**Create iteration notes.** In the iteration's list (the board's or the
iteration's **Context**, or the iteration report's **Pages**), choose
**Create iteration notes**.

</Step>
<Step>

**Choose where.** _Notes for Sprint 1_ asks for the **Space** (spaces you can
edit, the project's own first) and the **Title** (_Sprint 1 notes_). It
explains what you get: _A new page with the goal, a live list of the
iteration's work, risks and a retrospective, kept with this iteration._

</Step>
<Step>

**Create and open.** Lictory creates the page from the
[iteration notes template](/spaces/templates) with the iteration's goal
filled in, links it to the iteration and opens it.

</Step>
</Steps>

![Sprint 1 notes in the Payments space: the Goal, a live work list of the sprint’s items with their status, then Risks, Notes and the Retrospective headings to fill in](/screenshots/delivery-iteration-notes.webp)

Creating the page publishes its first version, as **New page** does in the
space. Creating and linking happen together: if the link is refused, no page
is created, and trying again after a lost connection creates one page, never
two. Work never changes an existing page by itself: the notes are yours to
edit like any other page.

If you can edit no space yet, the form says so: ask for edit access to a
project space, or create one.

## Who can manage context pages

The people who plan: project administrators and project members whose
discipline can change item properties. They can also add pages to an
iteration that is already completed, since retrospectives come afterwards.
Everyone else sees the lists without the controls. Managing a list also
needs read access to the page you add.

## In external projects

A project that delivers in Jira or another tool has no native board,
backlog or iterations, so there is no iteration context. Its teams' pages
still show the **Context** control with the team's and the project's
lists, and every other knowledge link works as in a native project
([knowledge links](/knowledge)).

## What to watch for

- **Readable pages only.** Two people can see different lists, and
  different counts, on the same board. A page from a Restricted space shows
  only to its readers.
- **Archived pages leave the list** until they are restored, and pages of an
  archived space with them.
- **A team or an iteration, not both.** Each list belongs to the project,
  one team or one iteration.
- **Teams must be active**: an archived team takes no new context pages.

## Tips

- Give each sprint a context page: create its notes when you start it, and
  keep the retrospective there when you complete it.
- Keep each list short. Three well-chosen pages are read; ten are not.
- Link the project's main requirements page and design doc to the project,
  so everyone on the board is one click from the why.
