---
title: Work in your pages
description: See the live state of work items on a page, find the work a page is linked to, and create work items from page text without losing where they came from.
---

Pages show work and start work, but never hold its state. A chip, a card or
a list on a page is read from Lictory Work when someone opens it, with that
reader's access. Delivery stays with its owner: Lictory in a project that
delivers natively, the delivery tool (such as Jira) in one that hands work
off.

## When to use it

- **Mention work where you explain it**: a runbook names the bug it
  prevents, a design names the stories that build it.
- **Turn page text into work**: a requirement, an open question or an action
  from a retrospective becomes a work item that remembers the exact words it
  came from.
- **See what a page led to**: the items created from it, the items that cite
  it and the page updates proposed from them.

## Item chips and cards

Type `#` in a page to mention an item ([blocks and chips](/spaces/blocks)).
Readers see the item's status, key and title; pointing at the chip or
focusing it shows its card:

![The card of the PAY-2 chip: Story, Retry failed card captures, In Progress, In review, 2 of 4 checks with what is still missing (a blocking discussion, and Engineering and QA reviews of revision 2), owner Dev Patel, Sprint 1 and Open PAY-2](/screenshots/detail-item-chip-card.webp)

- **Status**: the delivery status. In a project that delivers externally,
  the status read back from the delivery tool, such as _In Progress in Jira_,
  or _Not handed off to Jira_ before the item is published there.
- **Refinement**: the refinement state, for items with a specification.
- Readiness: _N of M checks_ and up to two checks still missing.
- **Owner**: the assignee (the delivery tool's assignee in external
  projects), or _Unassigned_.
- **Iteration**: the sprint or cycle it is planned in.

What you see is what you can see now. Nothing on the page changes the item:
**Open PAY-2** opens it.

## Work linked to this page

Under a published page, **Work linked to this page** lists every item the
page is connected to, each once, with how it is linked:

| Shown as                                      | Because                                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| _Created from this page_                      | It was created from text on this page              |
| _Mentioned_                                   | The published version mentions it with `#`         |
| _Attached as Background_ (or Design, Runbook) | Someone attached this page to the item             |
| _Cited in its spec_                           | Its current specification revision cites this page |
| _Cited in revision 2_                         | Only an earlier revision cited it                  |

![Work linked to this page under the Payments service page: PAY-1, PAY-2, PAY-3 and PAY-5 with how each is linked (Mentioned, Cited in its spec), their delivery status and refinement, “1 of 4 done” and Context for Payments](/screenshots/spaces-page-work.webp)

The header counts _N of M done_ and, when projects, teams or iterations keep
the page as context, says so (_Context for PAY Sprint 1_). Below,
**Page updates proposed from work** lists branches opened from work items
with **Propose page update**, named by the item, with their state: _Branch_,
_Draft pull request_, _In review_, _Merged_ or _Closed_.

The list shows a few rows at first; **Show N more** adds more. Items you
cannot see are left out without a trace. The page's readers see the list,
whatever their disciplines.

### Linked work on a space's home

The home of a project or team space lists its **Linked work**: every item
linked to any of its pages, once, with how and where (_Mentioned, attached ·
Capture retry design_), open work first, and how much is in progress and
done. Organization spaces have no such list.

## Create work from page text

Turn a sentence of a page into a work item. The item keeps a link to the
exact version you were reading and a quote of the words it came from. This
works on any committed version: the published page, an earlier version from
its history, or a branch's latest commit.

![The selection toolbar over selected text on a published page: Comment, Create work item and Ask Litto](/screenshots/spaces-selection-actions.webp)

<Steps>
<Step>

**Select the text** on the page you are reading. The bar above the selection
offers **Comment**, **Create work item** and, when Lictory Intelligence is
on, **Ask Litto**. Choose **Create work item**.

</Step>
<Step>

**Check the draft.** The **Create work item** dialog suggests a **Title**
(the selection, or the nearest heading when the selection is long), and a
description that quotes the text and ends with a link to the page version
(_Source: …_). Choose the **Project** (the space's project first) and the
**Type**.

</Step>
<Step>

**Read where the quote goes.** The dialog says that the quoted text is
copied into the project, where everyone in the organization can read it,
and that later edits to the page do not change the item. In a Restricted
space it warns that people who cannot read the page will see the quote.

</Step>
<Step>

**Create work item.** The confirmation (_PAY-6 created_) says the item
records where it came from. **Done** closes it.

</Step>
</Steps>

![The Create work item dialog for selected page text: Write it or Draft with Litto, Project and Type, a Title, a description that quotes the selection with its source page and version, and the note about who can read the copied quote](/screenshots/spaces-create-from-selection.webp)

A type with a specification starts as a draft proposal to refine and review
(_Creating it does not commit anyone to delivery._); other types go straight
to delivery. The new item's **Knowledge** section shows **Created from** with
the quote and **Open source**
([pages in your work](/knowledge/in-work#the-knowledge-section)).

### From an open task

Every open task on a page you are reading that does not mention a work item yet
shows **Create work item** (on hover or keyboard focus, always on touch
screens). The dialog takes the task's text as the title and suggests a type
without a specification, such as a task.

### Mention the new item on the page

If you can edit the page's published line (a Direct space) and were reading
its latest version, the confirmation offers **Add to the page**. It
opens the live draft and mentions the new items right after the text or the
task they came from. This is an ordinary draft edit: nothing is published
until you **Publish**, and if the text is gone from the draft, the mention
goes at the end of the page for you to move.

### Draft proposals with Litto

With Lictory Intelligence on, the dialog's **How** choice offers **Write it**
or **Draft with Litto**. **Draft proposals** sends the selected text to the
AI provider your organization configured (the dialog says so; it is not
stored) and returns draft proposals that cite the text. Tick the ones you
want and choose **Create N proposals**: only those are created, each with
its source. Drafts that cite nothing from the text are left out. See
[Lictory Intelligence](/ai).

## Who can do what

| Action                     | Needs                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| See chips, cards and lists | Read access to the page; each item as you can see it                         |
| **Create work item**       | A discipline that edits specifications or changes properties in some project |
| **Draft with Litto**       | Lictory Intelligence on, and a discipline that edits specifications          |
| **Add to the page**        | Edit access to the page's live draft (a Direct space)                        |

People without a discipline that creates work see no **Create work item**.

## What to watch for

- **A live draft is never a source.** While you edit, the selection offers
  no **Create work item**: an item always comes from a committed version, so
  its quote is text that exists in the page's history.
- **The quote leaves the page.** A work item is read by everyone in the
  organization. The quote stays exactly as the page said it; the item's
  title and description are yours to change.
- **Quotes are checked.** Lictory refuses a quote that is not the page's
  text in that version, so a source always shows what the page said. A long
  selection is cut to 2000 characters.
- **No duplicates on retry.** If the connection drops while items are
  created, trying again creates only what is missing.
- **Restricted spaces** keep their links private: only readers of the page
  see that an item came from it. The quote you copied into the item does not.

## Tips

- Create items from a published requirements page, one requirement at a
  time, then **Add to the page**: the page shows each item's status beside
  the requirement it implements.
- Turn retrospective actions into work items from their tasks, so the
  follow-ups land on the backlog.
- Read **Work linked to this page** before you change a page: it tells you
  whose work relies on it.
