---
title: How Work and Spaces connect
description: The six ways pages and work items link to each other, who creates each link, and who can see it.
---

Pages in [Lictory Spaces](/spaces) and work items in Lictory Work refer to
each other. A runbook mentions the bug it prevents, a specification cites the
design it follows, a sprint keeps its notes at hand. These **knowledge
links** let you go from the work to what the team knows about it, and back,
without copying either into the other.

Each link has one owner and one way of being made. None of them moves work,
changes a page or grants access.

## The six links

| Link                       | What it records                                                       | Made by                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Page reference**         | A page's published version mentions an item, a page or a decision     | Whoever publishes the page: `#`, `[[` or **Decision** in its text ([blocks](/spaces/blocks))                                         |
| **Specification citation** | A revision of an item's specification cites a page, often one version | Whoever saves the revision: `[[` in the specification ([citations](/knowledge/in-work#pinned-citations-in-specifications))           |
| **Attachment**             | A page belongs with an item, as background, design or runbook         | People who can change the item's properties: **Attach page** ([attach](/knowledge/in-work#attach-a-page))                            |
| **Source**                 | An item was created from text on a page, with the exact quote         | Whoever creates the item with **Create work item** on a page ([work from page text](/knowledge/in-pages#create-work-from-page-text)) |
| **Branch origin**          | A page branch was opened from an item's revision                      | Whoever chooses **Propose page update** on the item ([propose](/knowledge/in-work#propose-a-page-update))                            |
| **Context link**           | A project, team or iteration keeps a page at hand                     | The project's planners: **Add page** in **Context** ([context pages](/knowledge/context-pages))                                      |

Page references and citations follow what was written: change the page or
the specification and they change with it. The other four are made on
purpose. Attachments and context links can be removed again, with their
history kept on the page. Sources and branch origins are written once and
never change, so an item always knows which page version it came from.

## Where you see them

- **On a work item**, the **Knowledge** section lists every page about it
  that you can read, and the header counts them (**Knowledge 3**). Lists and
  previews show the same count as **3 pages**. See
  [pages in your work](/knowledge/in-work).
- **Under a published page**, **Work linked to this page** lists the items
  it mentions, was cited by, is attached to or was the source of, the page
  updates proposed from work, and what keeps it as context. A project or
  team space's home lists its **Linked work**. See
  [work in your pages](/knowledge/in-pages).
- **On the board, backlog, team backlogs and iterations**, the **Context**
  control lists their context pages. See
  [context pages](/knowledge/context-pages).
- **On the Refinement view**, a **Knowledge** line shows the project's
  spaces and what was published there recently.

## A link is visible only to readers of its page

Every link names a page, and you see a link only if you can read that page
now. Work items are read by everyone in the organization, but pages follow
their space's [access](/spaces/access). So:

- an item's **Knowledge** section lists only the pages you can read. Pages
  you cannot read leave no row, no title and no count;
- an attachment, a citation or a context link never lets anyone read a page
  they could not read before. Attaching a page from a Restricted space to an
  item shows it only to the people already named in that space;
- a mention of an item on a page never lets the page's readers change the
  item;
- archived pages, and pages of archived spaces, drop out of every list until
  they are restored.

Two things do travel with the item, because they are part of it: the words
of its specification (a citation's text is written by the specification's
author, and every member reads it) and anything copied into its title or
description. When you create an item from page text, the dialog says that
the quote is copied into the item, and warns when the page is in a
Restricted space.

## References follow published versions

A page links to what its **published** version says. Live drafts, branches
and pull requests are never indexed: a mention you type in a draft appears on
the item when you publish, and one you write on a branch when the branch is
merged into main. Restoring an older version changes the links with it.
Earlier versions keep what they said in their own history.

A specification links to what each **saved revision** says, and every
revision keeps its own citations. An item therefore knows which page
versions its current revision cites, and which only earlier revisions did.

Pages and specifications never change each other's state:

- Publishing a new version of a page never makes a specification's approval
  outdated. A pinned citation keeps pointing at its version and shows that a
  newer one exists.
- Adopting a newer page version in a specification is an ordinary edit: it
  creates a revision, and its approvals need another look like after any
  material change ([approvals](/review/approvals)).

## What to watch for

- **Unpublished means unlinked.** If an item does not list your page, check
  that the mention is in the published version, not only in your draft or
  branch.
- **Citation text is item content.** The words of a `[[` citation in a
  specification are visible to everyone who reads the item, even when the
  page itself is restricted. Choose a description you are happy for everyone
  to see.
- **Delivery mode does not matter.** Links work the same in projects that
  deliver in Lictory and in projects that hand work off to Jira; only
  iteration context needs native iterations
  ([context pages in external projects](/knowledge/context-pages#in-external-projects)).
- **No AI needed.** Every link is made by a person or derived from what
  people published. With Lictory Intelligence off, everything here works.

## Tips

- Cite pinned page versions in specifications, so reviewers approve against
  the exact text you read.
- Propose page updates from delivered work: the page's history then says
  which item and revision changed it.
- Give each sprint a context page, and attach runbooks to the items they
  support.
