Every work item has a **Knowledge** section: the pages that mention it, the
pages attached to it, the pages its specification cites, the page it was
created from and the page updates proposed from it. It is the reading
context for the specification, so the next reviewer finds the background
without asking where it is.

![The Knowledge section of PAY-2: the attached design page Capture retry design, mentioned in its latest version and cited by revision 2; Missing refund runbook, cited at v1, with Newer version v2 published and Compare; Payments service, mentioned under Retry policy; and the page update Explain automatic capture retries to support, In review](/screenshots/knowledge-item.webp)

## When to use it

- **Before you review or refine** an item, read what its Knowledge section
  lists: designs, runbooks and decisions the team already wrote down.
- **While you write a specification**, cite the page version you relied on.
- **When delivered work changes how something behaves**, propose the page
  update from the item, so the page and the item stay in step.

## The Knowledge section

The section sits under the specification, after the breakdown and before
**Relations**. Each page you can read appears once, with its title, its
space and, for an attachment, its purpose (**Background**, **Design** or
**Runbook**). Under the title it says how the page is linked:

| What it says                                         | What it means                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mentioned in latest** under _"heading"_            | The page's published version mentions the item, in that section (or _in N places_)                                |
| _Attached at_ **v2**                                 | Someone attached the page pinned to version 2                                                                     |
| _Revision 3 cites_ **v1**                            | The current revision of the specification cites version 1 (_and the latest_ when it also cites the page unpinned) |
| _Cited by revision 3_                                | The current revision cites the page without a pinned version                                                      |
| _Cited by revision 2, not by the current revision 3_ | Only an earlier revision cited it                                                                                 |

Below that, an excerpt quotes the published text that mentions the item.
When the row also speaks of an older pinned version, the excerpt says which
version it quotes: _Latest mention:_ for the published text, and _v2
(pinned):_ for the pinned version's own words in the same place when they
differ. You never read today's wording as the evidence an approval relied
on.

Every version is its own link. **Mentioned in latest** opens the published
version at the mention; each **vN** opens that exact version; the title
opens the one version the item relies on (its pin), or the latest at the
mention when there is no single pin. When the page has published since a
pin, _Newer version v3 published ·_ **Compare** opens the two versions side
by side in the page's history.

Two more parts appear when they apply:

- **Created from** _page · space_, above the list: the item was created from
  text on that page. It shows the quote, and **Open source** opens the exact
  version at the block it came from.
- **Page updates proposed from PAY-2**, below the list: each branch opened
  with **Propose page update**, named by its change, with the page, _From
  revision N_, who opened it and when, and its state: **Not proposed yet**,
  **Draft**, **In review**, **Merged**, **Closed** or **Branch deleted**.

With nothing linked yet, the section explains which pages will appear there.

### Counts in the header and in lists

The item's header shows **Knowledge 3** beside **Discussions** and
**Changes** when pages are linked: the number of pages you can read in the
section. It brings the section into view. On screens that replace the
specification (a branch, a pull request, the version history) it opens the
section, read-only, in a popover, so you have the background during a
review.

![The item header: Knowledge 3, 3 discussions with a red marker for a blocking one, and Changes](/screenshots/detail-item-header.webp)

Item lists and the item preview show the same count as **3 pages** (_3
pages about this item_ in the preview); it links to the section. Pages you
cannot read are never counted.

## Attach a page

Attach a page that belongs with the item but does not mention it: the design
it follows, the runbook for operating it, background worth reading first.

<Steps>
<Step>

**Choose the page.** In the **Knowledge** section, choose **Attach page**
and search by title or space (_Attach a page to PAY-2_). Pages that already
mention the item or are cited by it are offered first.

</Step>
<Step>

**Say why.** Under **Purpose**, choose **Background** (_Context worth
reading first_), **Design** (_How it is meant to look or work_) or **Runbook**
(_How to operate or support it_).

</Step>
<Step>

**Pin a version, if it matters.** Tick **Pin the version published now** to
attach exactly today's version. Otherwise the item follows the page as it
is published.

</Step>
<Step>

**Attach page.** The page joins the list with its purpose.

</Step>
</Steps>

![The attach step in the Knowledge section: Attach “Capture retry design” to PAY-3, Purpose with Background, Design (selected) and Runbook, Pin the version published now, Cancel and Attach page](/screenshots/detail-attach-page.webp)

To detach a page, point at its row and choose the **×** (**Detach**). The
confirmation offers **Undo**. Detaching keeps the history: the page's
activity records who attached and detached it.

Attaching and detaching need a discipline that can change the item's
properties in its project, and read access to the page. Everyone who can
read the page sees the attachment.

## Propose a page update

When the work changes something a page describes, propose the change from
the item. Lictory opens a branch of the page linked to the item's current
revision; the page's own rules decide when it is published.

<Steps>
<Step>

**Choose the page.** In the **Knowledge** section, choose **Propose page
update** and pick a page (_Propose an update to_). Only pages in spaces you
can edit are offered, the ones attached to or mentioning the item first.

</Step>
<Step>

**Say what changes.** Under **What changes**, describe the change in a few
words, for example _Document the finance approval time_. The dialog says
that a branch opens in the editor, linked to _PAY-2 revision 4_, and either
_Its changes are published after review._ (a Reviewed space) or _Nothing is
published until you merge it._ (a Direct space).

</Step>
<Step>

**Check the branch name** (optional). **Advanced** shows the suggested
**Branch name**, made from the item key and the change
(`pay-2-document-the-finance-approval-time`). Change it if you like.

</Step>
<Step>

**Create branch and edit.** The page opens in the branch's live draft.
Write the change, **Commit** it, and open a pull request: its title starts as
the change you described. Reviewers then approve and someone merges it, as
for any [branch](/spaces/branches).

</Step>
</Steps>

![The Propose page update popover: Propose an update to “Missing refund runbook”, the note that the branch is linked to PAY-3 revision 2 and nothing is published until it is merged, What changes, Advanced with the branch name, Back and Create branch and edit](/screenshots/detail-propose-update.webp)

The item lists the update under **Page updates proposed from PAY-2** and
follows it to **Merged**. The page lists it under **Page updates proposed
from work**, and the branch keeps its link to the item and revision even
after it is merged or deleted.

Proposing needs edit access to the page's space. Work never publishes a page
directly: in a Reviewed space the update is published only after an
approved pull request merges, in a Direct space only when someone merges it.

## Pinned citations in specifications

A specification can link to a page like any text: type `[[` in the
specification editor and pick the page. To cite the exact version you read,
press `Shift Enter` in the picker, or choose **Pin this version**. The
citation then shows `vN`, opens that version, and marks when a newer version
is published; its card says _Version N is published._ with **Compare in
history**.

![A pinned page citation in a specification: the Missing refund runbook chip with v1 and a marker, and its card saying it cites version 1, version 2 is published, with Compare in history and Open this version](/screenshots/detail-pinned-citation.webp)

The citation belongs to the revision you save. The item's Knowledge section
then says _Revision 4 cites_ **v1**, and an approval of that revision is an
approval of the specification as it read with that page version.

When the page publishes a new version, nothing changes on the item by
itself: the citation stays at v1, and the Knowledge row offers **Compare**.
To adopt the newer version, edit the citation and save: that is an ordinary
revision, so reviewers who approved the earlier one are asked to look again.

## On the Refinement view

The project's **Refinement** view shows a **Knowledge** line under its
stage tabs (**All in refinement**, **In progress**…): up to four of the project's spaces (its own first, then its teams'),
**+N more** when there are others, and the three pages published there most
recently. When the project has no space yet, project administrators see
**Create a space for …'s pages** instead.

![The Knowledge line on the Refinement view: the Payments space, then the recently published pages Missing refund runbook, Decision records and Capture retry design](/screenshots/knowledge-refinement-strip.webp)

## What to watch for

- **Only pages you can read.** Two people can see different Knowledge
  sections on the same item. Nothing hints at pages you cannot read.
- **Only published text.** A page appears here when its published version
  mentions the item. A mention in a draft or on a branch counts once it is
  published or merged
  ([references follow published versions](/knowledge#references-follow-published-versions)).
- **A page update is a proposal.** **Propose page update** never changes the
  published page. If the space is Reviewed, someone other than you must
  approve the pull request.
- **Citations are item content.** The words you write for a `[[` citation
  are part of the specification, which everyone in the organization reads,
  even when the page is restricted.
- **On a phone**, the header keeps **Knowledge** beside the way back, the
  item's key and **Discussions**; the section itself is under the
  specification.

## Tips

- Cite pinned page versions in specifications, so the approval says exactly
  what was agreed against.
- Propose page updates from delivered work, before you close the item. The
  page's history then shows which item and revision changed it.
- Attach the runbook to the items it supports, with the purpose **Runbook**,
  so whoever operates the change finds it from the item.
