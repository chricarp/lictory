Your account is yours, not an organization's: its name, handle, sign-in
methods and appearance apply in every organization you belong to. Open it
from your name at the bottom of the sidebar → **Account settings**; **Back**
returns you to where you were.

![Account settings: Profile, Handle, Email, Appearance, Password, Two-factor, Passkeys, Connected accounts, Sessions, Access tokens, Event webhooks and Delete account in the list on the left, and the profile, handle and email on the right](/screenshots/account-settings.webp)

Five groups: **Identity** (profile, handle, email), **Preferences**
(appearance and keyboard), **Sign-in & security** (password, two-factor,
passkeys, connected accounts, sessions), **Integrations** (access tokens
and event webhooks) and **Danger zone** (delete account).

## Identity

- **Profile**: your name and photo, as collaborators see them on
  discussions and reviews. **Upload photo** takes a JPEG, PNG or WebP under
  4 MB; remove it to show your initials.
- **Handle**: unique across the installation; people type `@handle` to
  mention and find you. Change it with **Save handle**; existing mentions
  keep pointing to you.
- **Email**: where you sign in and where notifications go. A new address
  takes effect once you confirm it from the email sent there ("Check the new
  address to confirm this change"). The section shows whether your address
  is **Verified**.

## Appearance

Choose a **Mode** (**System**, **Light** or **Dark**) and a **Theme**
(Lictory, Graphite, GitHub, Catppuccin, Rosé Pine or Nord). Every theme has
a light and a dark variant; **System** follows your device. The choice
follows your account to every device.

![The PAY-2 specification in the dark variant of the Lictory theme, with the Discussions panel and the review rail beside the text](/screenshots/work-discussions-panel-dark.webp)

## Keyboard

**Single-key shortcuts** turns the one-key shortcuts (`C`, `/`, `[`, `G`
chords, `J`, `K`, `O`) on or off. They are on by default; turn them off for
speech input or if you press keys by accident. Shortcuts with `Mod` or `Alt`
always work. See [keyboard shortcuts](/basics/shortcuts).

## Sign-in and security

- **Password**: **Update password** with your current one. Changing it
  signs out every other session. If you signed up through Google,
  Microsoft or your company's sign-in, the section says **Not set** and
  **Email me a link** adds a password.
- **Two-factor**: **Set up authenticator app** shows a QR code to scan
  with any authenticator app (1Password, Google Authenticator, Microsoft
  Authenticator…). Enter the current code and **Turn on**. Save the backup
  codes: each works once, and they are shown only once (**Create new backup
  codes** replaces them). Sign-in then asks for a code after your password.
  Google, Microsoft and company sign-ins rely on the provider's own checks
  instead.
- **Passkeys**: **Add passkey** to sign in with your device's fingerprint,
  face or screen lock, or a security key. Removing one here does not remove
  it from your device or password manager; delete it there too.
  Installations that allow only company sign-in have no passkeys.
- **Connected accounts**: **Connect** an identity provider your
  installation offers (Google, Microsoft, your company's single sign-on) to
  sign in with it as well. Only an account with the same email address can
  be connected. You cannot **Disconnect** your only way to sign in; set a
  password first.
- **Sessions**: how many browsers and devices are signed in.
  **Sign out other sessions** signs out everywhere except here.

<Callout title="Lost your authenticator and your backup codes?">
  An administrator of an organization you belong to can reset your two-factor
  authentication
  ([security](/organization/security#reset-a-members-two-factor)). Your password
  and passkeys stay; you set up two-factor again afterwards.
</Callout>

Turning two-factor off, adding a passkey, disconnecting an account and
deleting your account need a recent sign-in ("For your security, sign out
and sign in again…").

## Access tokens and event webhooks

**Personal access tokens** are for scripts and tools that act as you, with
your permissions in every organization you belong to. Each has a name,
**Read & write** or **Read only** access and an expiry; its actions are
recorded under its name. **Event webhooks** wake such a tool when something
waits on you.

To connect an AI agent, create it in **Factory → Agents** instead: its
scope, token and wake-ups live on its own page, and Account settings only
links there. See [Agents](/factory/agents) and
[AI agents and MCP](/integrations/agents).

Tokens never see your password, and cannot create tokens, connect
integrations or change your account. **Revoke** disconnects a token at
once.

## Delete your account

**Delete account…** removes your account for good. Confirm with your
password (if you have one) and **Delete permanently**.

- Every way to act as you ends: sessions, password, connected accounts,
  passkeys, two-factor, and every agent token.
- Your email address, handle and photo are removed, and your memberships of
  every organization, project, discipline, team and restricted space end.
  Your private Litto conversations, your Inbox, notification preferences and
  watched items are deleted.
- What you contributed stays unchanged: revisions, comments, reviews,
  approvals and decisions keep your name, shown as a former member. An
  organization that must remove personal data from content edits it like
  any other content.
- The email address becomes free; signing up with it later makes a new
  person.

Lictory refuses the deletion, and changes nothing, when you are the only
owner of an organization (make another member an owner, or
[delete the organization](/organization/data#delete-an-organization) first)
or the installation's only instance administrator. An organization already
scheduled for deletion does not stop you; it is still deleted on its date,
and if you were its only owner nobody can cancel that any more.

## Tips

- Choose a short, recognizable handle: people type it to ask you
  something, and a mention puts the question in your Inbox.
- Turn on two-factor before your organization requires it; an organization
  that requires it holds you out until you do.
- Create one agent token per agent and tool, named for what it does
  ("Claude spec reviewer"): the audit log then says which agent did what,
  and revoking one leaves the others working.
