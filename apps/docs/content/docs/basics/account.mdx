Your account is yours, not an organization's: the same name, handle, sign-in
methods and appearance apply in every organization you belong to. Open it from
your name at the bottom of the sidebar → **Account settings**. **Back**
returns you to where you were.

![Account settings: Profile, Handle, Email, Appearance, Password, Two-factor, Passkeys, Connected accounts, Sessions, AI agents and Delete account in the list on the left, and the profile, handle and email on the right](/screenshots/account-settings.webp)

The page has five groups: **Identity** (profile, handle, email),
**Preferences** (appearance), **Sign-in & security** (password, two-factor,
passkeys, connected accounts, sessions), **Integrations** (AI agents) and
**Danger zone** (delete account).

## Identity

- **Profile**: your name and photo, as collaborators see them on
  discussions and reviews. **Upload photo** takes a JPEG, PNG or WebP
  smaller than 4 MB; remove it to show your initials.
- **Handle**: unique across the installation. People type `@handle` to
  mention you and find you. You can change it with **Save handle**;
  existing mentions keep pointing to you.
- **Email**: where you sign in and where notifications go. A new address
  takes effect once you confirm it from the email Lictory sends there
  ("Check the new address to confirm this change"). The section shows
  whether your address is **Verified**.

## Appearance

Choose a **Mode** (**System**, **Light** or **Dark**) and a **Theme**
(Lictory, Graphite, GitHub, Catppuccin, Rosé Pine or Nord). Every theme has
a light and a dark variant; **System** follows your device and switches when
it does. The choice is saved to your account, so it follows you to every
device you sign in on.

## Sign-in and security

- **Password**: **Update password** with your current one. Changing it
  signs out every other session. If you signed up through Google,
  Microsoft or your company's sign-in, the section says **Not set** and
  **Email me a link** adds a password.
- **Two-factor**: **Set up authenticator app** shows a QR code to scan
  with 1Password, Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or any other
  authenticator app. Enter the current code and **Turn on**. Save the backup
  codes it shows: each works once, and they are shown only once (**Create
  new backup codes** replaces them). Sign-in then asks for a code after your
  password. Google, Microsoft and company sign-ins rely on the provider's
  own checks instead.
- **Passkeys**: **Add passkey** lets you sign in with your device's
  fingerprint, face or screen lock, or a security key. Removing one here
  does not remove it from your device or password manager; delete it there
  too. Installations that allow only company sign-in have no passkeys.
- **Connected accounts**: **Connect** an identity provider your
  installation offers (Google, Microsoft, your company's single sign-on) to
  sign in with it as well. Only an account with the same email address can
  be connected. You cannot **Disconnect** your only way to sign in; set a
  password first.
- **Sessions**: how many browsers and devices are signed in.
  **Sign out other sessions** signs out everywhere except here.

<Callout title="Lost your authenticator and your backup codes?">
  An administrator of an organization you belong to can reset your two-factor
  authentication
  ([security](/organization/security#reset-a-members-two-factor)). Your password
  and passkeys stay; you set up two-factor again afterwards.
</Callout>

Some actions ask you to sign out and in again first ("For your security,
sign out and sign in again…"): turning two-factor off, adding a passkey,
disconnecting an account and deleting your account need a recent sign-in.

## AI agents

**AI agents (MCP)** creates personal access tokens for agents that work in
Lictory as you: a coding agent in your terminal or editor. Each token is a
named agent, with **Read & write** or **Read only** access and an expiry.
Its actions are recorded under the token's name. See
[AI agents and MCP](/integrations/agents).

Agents never see your password, and cannot create tokens, connect
integrations or change your account. **Revoke** disconnects an agent at
once.

## Delete your account

**Delete account…** removes your account for good. Confirm with your
password (if you have one) and **Delete permanently**.

What happens:

- Every way to act as you ends: sessions, password, connected accounts,
  passkeys, two-factor, and every agent token.
- Your email address, handle and photo are removed, and your memberships of
  every organization, project, discipline, team and restricted space end.
  Your private Litto conversations, your Inbox, notification preferences and
  watched items are deleted.
- What you contributed stays as it is: revisions, comments, reviews,
  approvals and decisions keep your name, shown as a former member. What you
  wrote is not changed; an organization that must remove personal data from
  content edits it like any other content.
- The email address becomes free, so you can sign up again with it later as
  a new person.

Lictory refuses the deletion, and changes nothing, when you are the only
owner of an organization (make another member an owner, or
[delete the organization](/organization/data#delete-an-organization) first)
or the installation's only instance administrator. An organization already
scheduled for deletion does not stop you; it is still deleted on its date,
and if you were its only owner nobody can cancel that any more.

## Tips

- Choose a short, recognizable handle: people type it in discussions to ask
  you something, and a mention is what puts a question in your Inbox.
- Turn on two-factor before your organization requires it; an
  organization that requires it holds you out until you do.
- Create one agent token per agent and tool, named for what it does
  ("Claude spec reviewer"). The audit log and history then say which agent
  did what, and revoking one leaves the others working.
