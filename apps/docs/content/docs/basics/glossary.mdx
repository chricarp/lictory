Lictory uses a small set of words, and uses them the same way everywhere:
in the app, in these guides and in what agents read. Older guides may say
_product_ where Lictory now says **project**.

## Refining work

### Proposal

The first, rough expression of a change someone wants: a problem, the
intent, notes, links. A proposal is expected to be incomplete; refinement
makes it useful. See [proposals](/specifications/proposals).

### Specification

The structured description of the behavior being agreed on. It is the
primary thing people review, and it belongs to a work item of a type that
has one (Epic or Story by default). See [specifications](/specifications).

### Revision

A saved version of a specification. Revisions never change once saved:
they are the history, what diffs compare and what approvals refer to. See
[history](/specifications/history).

### Live draft

The shared working copy of a specification or a page that several people
edit at the same time. A draft is never history: saving it makes a revision
(or a commit on a branch, or a published page version). See
[live drafts](/specifications/live-drafts) and [editing pages](/spaces/editing).

### Branch and pull request

A branch is a separate line of work on a specification or a page; a pull
request asks for it to be reviewed and merged into the main text. Once a
specification is ready, its main text changes only this way. See
[branches](/specifications/branches) and [page branches](/spaces/branches).

### Thread (discussion)

A conversation attached to exact context: a passage of text, a section, a
revision or a pull request. A thread is open or resolved, and blocking or
not. Resolving keeps its history. See [discussions](/specifications/discussions).

### Suggestion

A proposed text change that can be accepted or rejected, not a comment.
Accepting one creates a new revision. New changes are proposed on branches;
suggestions remain for existing records. See [suggestions](/specifications/suggestions).

### Decision

A durable record of a conclusion reached in refinement ("Retries reuse
the original idempotency key"), often promoted from a discussion. See
[decisions](/specifications/decisions).

## Reviewing

### Review

A request for a discipline (or a person in it) to evaluate a revision. A
review can be required or optional. See [review requests](/review/requests).

### Approval

A reviewer's decision that a specific revision meets their
responsibility. An approval belongs to the revision it was given for: when
the text changes materially, it becomes outdated and the review is asked for
again. See [approvals](/review/approvals).

### Check (readiness check)

An evaluation of whether the work is ready: the specification is written,
no blocking thread is open, required reviews approved the current revision,
and whatever else the project's Definition of Ready asks. The item is
**ready** when every check passes; readiness is evidence, not a label. See
[readiness](/review/readiness).

## Delivering

### Work item

A unit of work in a project, with a key such as `PAY-142` that never
changes. Its **work item type** (Epic, Story, Task, Bug, Subtask by
default, defined for the whole organization) says whether it carries a
specification. See [find your way around](/basics/navigation#a-work-item-page).

### Delivery item

A unit of implementation work, an ordinary work item. An approved
specification is broken down into child items that record the revision they
came from. See [breakdown](/delivery/breakdown).

### Workflow status

Where a work item stands in delivery (Backlog, Todo, In Progress, Done…).
Each project defines its statuses, each in a fixed category: backlog,
unstarted, started, done, canceled. Delivery status is separate from the
refinement state (draft, refinement, in review, ready). See
[the board](/delivery/board).

### Iteration

A time-box of planned work: a **sprint** (started and completed by hand)
or a **cycle** (scheduled automatically). A project has at most one active
iteration. See [iterations](/delivery/iterations).

### Relation

A directed link between work items: blocks, relates to, duplicates. See
[relations](/delivery/relations).

### Publish and hand off

In a project delivered in Jira, ready work is handed off (published) to
Jira, which then owns its delivery status. Publishing is not approval:
approval comes first. Publishing a page in Spaces is a different act: it
makes a version the page's published version. See [Jira](/integrations/jira)
and [publishing pages](/spaces/publishing).

## Knowledge

### Space

A home for knowledge: nested pages people write together. A space
belongs to the organization, a project or a team, whose members can edit
it, or is restricted to named people. See [Spaces](/spaces) and
[access](/spaces/access).

### Page

A document in a space, with its own history of versions. Pages are not
work items: they have no status and no readiness. See [pages](/spaces/pages).

### Knowledge

The links between pages and work: pages that mention an item, pages a
specification cites (optionally pinned to a version), pages attached to an
item, and pages kept as context of a project, team or iteration. See
[knowledge](/knowledge).

## People and access

### Organization

A company, or a large department of one, using Lictory: its people,
projects, spaces and settings. See [organization and access](/organization).

### Project

A product area inside an organization. It owns its work items and their
keys, its members and disciplines, workflow, iterations, delivery mode and
readiness policy. See [projects](/organization/projects).

### Team

A group of people working on one part of a project. A team owns no
configuration; boards and backlogs can be narrowed to it. See
[teams](/delivery/teams).

### User

A person with an account on your Lictory installation. See
[your account](/basics/account).

### Application role

What a person may administer in an organization: owners and admins manage
the organization and every project; members work in it. Within a project,
the **project role** (admin or member) decides who configures it. Roles
grant no work item capabilities. See [roles](/organization#three-separate-questions).

### Discipline

A kind of work a person does in one project, such as Product, Engineering
or QA. Reviews are requested from disciplines, and disciplines decide what
their members may do with work items. See [disciplines](/organization/disciplines).

### Instance administrator

A person who manages the installation itself: its accounts, its instance
administrators and its organizations. It grants no access to any
organization's work. See [instance administration](/organization/instance).

## Assistance

### Lictory Intelligence

Lictory's optional AI: reviews that point out gaps and contradictions,
proposed fixes, and Litto. Its findings and changes are proposals; people
decide. Everything works without it. See [Lictory Intelligence](/ai).

### Litto

Lictory's assistant, which answers questions about your work and pages
with citations and proposes changes you confirm. See [Litto](/ai/litto).

### Agent

A tool outside Lictory, such as a coding agent in your terminal, that
works through MCP with a personal access token, as the person who created
it. See [AI agents and MCP](/integrations/agents).
