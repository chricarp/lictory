---
title: Getting started
description: Set up a team in Lictory and take a first specification from proposal through review and approval to delivery.
---

**Joining a team that already uses Lictory?** Start at [Sign in](#sign-in).
Setting it up for your team? Lictory runs on your own server: the first step
installs it, and the rest happen at your installation's address (for example
`https://lictory.example.com`).

<Steps>
<Step>

### Install Lictory (administrators)

Follow [Install](/self-hosting/install) to set up a server with the `lictory`
command, then open its address in your browser. The
[overview](/self-hosting) lists what the server needs. To look around first,
[try Lictory on your computer](/self-hosting/try) with one command.

</Step>
<Step>

### Sign in

Open your installation's address and sign in the way your team was invited:
accept the invitation email, use your company's single sign-on, or sign in
with an account you already have. Then choose a handle: it is how teammates
mention you, and you can change it later in account settings.

<Callout title="For administrators">
  Sign in first with the administrator the installer created (`sudo lictory
  admin setup` adds one later), then invite people as the [access
  policy](/self-hosting/configuration#access-policy) allows.
</Callout>

</Step>
<Step>

### Create your first project

Open your organization and name its first project. The project **key**
becomes part of every identifier (`PAY-142`) and does not change, so pick it
with care; the name can change later.

Guided setup then asks only for the decisions that change how the project
behaves:

- **Where does delivery happen?** _In Lictory_ (boards, backlog and
  iterations next to the specifications) or _In Jira_ (export ready work;
  Jira owns status and sprints). See [delivery](/concepts/delivery).
- **When work starts before it's ready** (in Lictory only): block, warn
  (the default) or allow. See [readiness policy](/concepts/readiness#readiness-policy).
- **How does the project plan?** (in Lictory only): continuously, in sprints
  or in automatic cycles, and which estimate scale to use.
- **Lictory Intelligence**: whether AI may review specifications, chat and
  propose fixes. Everything works without it.
- **Invite people**: optional, you can do it later.

Work item types, the workflow and the Product, Engineering and QA
disciplines are created for you, and you become a project administrator in
Product. Every choice can be changed later in settings.

<Callout title="More than one organization">
  Where the access policy lets everyone create organizations, people without one
  can create their own from the welcome page.
</Callout>

</Step>
<Step>

### Invite your team

In **Organization settings → Members**, invite people by email or by their
Lictory username. They join as organization members when they accept. Then,
in **Project settings → Members** and **Disciplines**, add them to the project
and to the disciplines they work in: an engineer to Engineering, a tester to
QA. Disciplines decide who can be asked to review and what each person may do
with work items ([disciplines](/organization/disciplines)). You can also
choose the project and disciplines in the invitation itself
([members](/organization/members)).

</Step>
<Step>

### Write a first proposal

Open the project's **Proposals** and choose **New proposal**. Describe the
problem and the intended behavior; it is fine to start rough. Add the
sections your reviewers will look for, such as _Acceptance criteria_ and
_Failure behavior_. Type `#` to mention another work item and `[[` to cite a
page.

Teammates can edit the specification with you at the same time: the text is
a shared [live draft](/specifications/live-drafts), saved as you type.
**Save revision N** turns what is written into a
[revision](/specifications/history), the version people review.

</Step>
<Step>

### Request reviews

From the item's review rail (**Review & details** on a narrow screen),
**Request review** from the disciplines that need to agree, for example
Engineering and QA. Reviewers comment on the exact text they are concerned
about, mark a discussion **blocking** when it must be settled first, and
**Approve** or **Request changes** ([reviews](/review/requests)).

Revise the specification until the open questions are settled, and record
what you agreed as a [decision](/specifications/decisions). Each material
revision makes earlier approvals stale and asks the same reviewers again, so
an approval always refers to the text that was actually reviewed
([approvals](/review/approvals)).

</Step>
<Step>

### Reach ready

When every [readiness check](/review/readiness) passes (by default: the
specification is written, no blocking discussion is open, and every required
review approved the current revision), the item becomes **ready**. From then
on its main text is protected: further changes go through a
[branch](/specifications/branches) and a reviewed pull request, which can
also propose the [breakdown](/delivery/breakdown) into Stories and Tasks.

</Step>
<Step>

### Deliver

- **In Lictory**: choose **Accept revision N for delivery** to record what
  the team agreed to build, plan it into a sprint or cycle and move it across
  the **Board**. Moving it to Done records the delivered revision. See
  [Deliver](/delivery).
- **In Jira**: connect a Jira site in **Organization settings →
  Integrations**, choose the Jira project and type mapping in **Project
  settings → Delivery**, then **Hand off** the ready work from the project's
  **Hand-off** view. See [Jira](/integrations/jira).

</Step>
</Steps>

## Write it down in Spaces

Knowledge that is not a work item (a runbook, how the team works, an
architecture note) belongs in a **space**. Choose **New space** under
**Spaces** in the sidebar, pick its location (the organization, a project or
a team, whose members can then edit it), and add a first page with **New
page**. Teammates can edit it with you at the same time; **Publish** makes
what you wrote the version everyone reads, and every version stays in the
page's history. Type `#` to mention a work item: the item then lists the
page in its **Knowledge**, and a specification can cite the page in turn
([knowledge](/knowledge)). The [Spaces guide](/spaces) covers everything
else, from comments to reviewed publishing.
