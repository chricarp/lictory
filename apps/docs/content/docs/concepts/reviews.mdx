---
title: Reviews, discussions and approvals
description: How disciplines review a specification, how discussions stay anchored to their context, and why every approval belongs to one revision.
---

## Reviews

A **review** asks a discipline of the project (Product, Engineering, QA,
Security…) to evaluate the specification, optionally naming one person in
it. The discipline's name is the review responsibility. Only an assigned
person, or a current member of the assigned discipline, can decide the
review: **Approve** or **Request changes**. Holding a discipline never
approves anything by itself.

Requesting a review moves a draft or refining specification to _in review_. Requests and decisions show up in the reviewer's inbox and
notifications.

## Discussions

A **thread** is a discussion attached to its context: a quoted range of the
specification, a revision, or a pull request commit. The quote is read
against the revision the thread was started on, and Lictory tells you when
that text no longer appears, so nobody has to ask what a comment referred to.

Threads are **blocking** or **non-blocking**. An open blocking thread keeps
a specification from becoming ready and a pull request from merging.
Resolving and reopening never delete messages; the activity keeps every
step. When a thread reaches an important conclusion, record it as a
[decision](/concepts/specifications#decisions).

## Approvals belong to revisions

An **approval** states that a reviewer accepted one specific revision for
their discipline. It is never a permanent flag on the item:

- A material change creates a new revision and makes the earlier approvals
  **stale**, recording who caused it, when and why. Every active review is
  requested again on the new revision with the same assignments; the old
  decisions stay in history.
- The item shows the difference plainly, for example _Engineering approved
  revision 12; the current revision is 14; Engineering's approval requires
  review._
- On a pull request, approvals record a fingerprint of the complete result
  of the merge. Any change to the source or target that alters that result
  makes them stale; changes that leave it identical keep them. Contributors
  cannot approve their own changes.

A pull request merges only when every required review approves its current
result, nobody currently requests changes, no blocking thread is open and no
conflict remains.

## Lictory Intelligence is a check, not an approval

Lictory Intelligence raises findings and questions and can propose changes on
its own branches. A project can require a clean Lictory Intelligence review as
a [readiness check](/concepts/readiness#definition-of-ready), but it never
counts as a discipline's approval and is always required beside a person's
decision. Anyone holding a discipline can override the check for one
revision. Litto or an MCP agent can record an approval only on a person's
explicit instruction, and the approval is that person's.
