Delivery mode is a per-project setting (**Project settings → Delivery**). The
refinement workflow is identical in both modes; for the same piece of work,
exactly one system owns delivery state.

## Delivery items

An approved specification is decomposed into child work items (Stories,
Tasks, Subtasks), proposed as a [breakdown](/concepts/specifications#main-branches-and-pull-requests)
and reviewed with the specification. Each child records the revision it was
derived from. Plain implementation items under a specification inherit its
readiness, so splitting unsettled behavior into Tasks cannot bypass the gate.

## Native delivery

- **Types** are an organization-wide registry that projects opt into. A type
  has a hierarchy level (Epic above Story, Task and Bug, above Subtask) and
  says whether it carries a specification.
- **Workflows** are per project: statuses you name, each in a fixed category
  (`backlog`, `unstarted`, `started`, `done`, `canceled`). Any transition is
  allowed; there are no transition rules to configure.
- **Board and backlog** show the project's work in rank order, and can be
  narrowed to a team. Each team also has a backlog of its unplanned work.
- **Iterations** are sprints (created, started and completed by hand) or
  cycles (scheduled automatically from a length and start weekday). One
  iteration per project is active at a time. Unfinished work rolls over.
- **Estimates and reports**: integer estimates (T-shirt sizes map to points),
  burndown, scope change and velocity from recorded events. A completed
  iteration's report is frozen.
- **Relations**: _blocks_, _relates to_ and _duplicates_, across projects and
  teams of the organization.

### Accepting scope

Ready means a specification can be selected, not that the team committed to
it. Accepting a revision for delivery (**Accept revision N for delivery**)
records the team's agreement; starting work records it automatically when
none exists. When the specification changes later, the accepted revision
stays the agreement and the item shows the difference until someone accepts
the new one. Moving to Done records the accepted revision as delivered.

## External delivery (Jira)

Lictory refines, reviews and approves; Jira delivers. Iterations and
estimates are Jira's, so they are turned off in Lictory. The project's
**Hand-off** view replaces the board: what is ready to hand off, what changed
since it was handed off, what Jira reports back and what needs attention.
The published revision is the scope boundary; a later revision is handed off
explicitly as an update. See [Jira](/integrations/jira).
