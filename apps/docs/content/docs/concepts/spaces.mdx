---
title: Spaces and pages
description: Lictory Spaces keeps the pages a team writes together, edited live by several people and changed through the same reviewed history as specifications.
---

**Lictory Spaces** is where a team keeps what it knows: handbooks, runbooks,
architecture notes, decision records, onboarding guides. Pages are written
together in real time, and every published version stays in the page's
history, so you can always see what changed, who changed it and who approved
it.

```text
Organization
└─ Space        a home for knowledge, optionally belonging to a project or a team
   └─ Page      nested pages; each has its own history
      ├─ main         the published version readers see
      └─ branches     proposed changes, each with its own live draft
```

Pages are not work items. They have no key, status, estimate, iteration or
readiness, and work item specifications stay where they are: on the item,
with its [revisions](/concepts/specifications) and
[reviews](/concepts/reviews). Pages and work items link to each other
instead ([below](#work-items-and-page-links)).

## Spaces and their home

A **space** belongs to the organization and, optionally, to one project or to
one team of a project: its **home**. The home decides where the space appears
(the organization's sidebar under **Spaces**, and each project's and team's
navigation) and who can edit it by default. Moving work items between teams
never changes a space.

Create a space with **New space** in the sidebar, or **Create space** in the
command palette. Give it a name, an optional icon and description, and if you
like choose **Customize address** to change its **Address**
(`/<organization>/spaces/<address>`).
Where it lives, who can open it and how it publishes start from defaults,
summarized in one line under **Access & publishing** (for example _Northwind ·
everyone can edit · direct publishing_); **Configure** picks its **Location**
(the home), its **Access** (**Inherited** or **Restricted**,
[below](#access)) and its [publishing policy](#publishing-direct-or-reviewed).
**Settings** on the space's page changes these later; each setting saves as
soon as you change it and says **Saved** beside it, and moving a space whose
access is inherited first says who will be able to edit it. Archiving a space
hides it from navigation and freezes its pages; nothing is deleted, and its
administrators can restore it.

## Pages and the page tree

A **page** belongs to one space and can sit under another page of the same
space. **New page** adds one at the end of the space; **New subpage** in a
page's actions adds one under it. Drag pages in the tree to reorder them or
nest them, or use **Move…** to do the same with the keyboard. Pages move only
within their space. Pages are addressed by their id, so moving or renaming
one never breaks a link.

**Archive** hides a page and its subpages. It is reversible: **Show archived
pages** in the tree, then **Restore**. Restoring a page brings back the
subpages that were archived with it. There is no permanent delete.

**Find a page** on a space's home narrows its pages to the titles that
match, wherever they sit in the tree. Beside a page, **Hide pages** puts the
tree away for more room (this browser remembers it) and **Show pages** brings
it back; while comments are open on a narrower screen the tree steps aside on
its own. The command palette finds spaces and page titles you can read.

## Live editing

Each page shows its published version. Editors choose **Edit** (or
**Edit unpublished draft** when unpublished changes are waiting) to open the page's
**live draft**: a shared working copy that several people edit at the same
time. Everyone sees each other's cursors with their names, and the header
says who else is there, for example _Anna is editing_.

Write in the editor directly, use Markdown shortcuts, or type `/` for blocks
(headings, lists, tables, code, images, a work item, a page link). The
toolbar keeps undo, bold, italic, strikethrough, code, **Link** and
**Comment** at hand; **Text** changes the block (heading, list, quote, code
block) and **Insert** adds a table, divider, image, work item or page link.
**Close editor** goes back to the published page; the draft stays saved. Paste,
drop or choose images to attach them to the page; like other attachments
they are stored privately and need your installation's file storage.

Beside the title, the editor tells you where your edits are:

| State                                     | What it means                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Saving…**                               | Your latest edits are on their way to the server.                                                                        |
| **Saved**                                 | The server has stored every edit you made. Only stored edits ever reach other editors.                                   |
| **Offline — changes kept on this device** | The connection dropped. Your edits stay in this tab and are sent when it returns.                                        |
| **Live editing unavailable**              | The editing service cannot be reached. Reading, history and comments keep working; try again or read the published page. |

<Callout title="Keep the tab open while offline" type="warn">
  Offline edits live only in the open browser tab, not on the server. The tab
  warns you before it closes with unsaved edits; if you close it anyway, or the
  browser quits, before the connection returns, those edits are lost. Wait for
  **Saved** before closing the page.
</Callout>

A live draft is a working copy, not history. Nobody reviews or approves it,
search and agents never treat it as the page's content, and readers do not
see it until it is published or committed. If someone restores an older
version or merges changes into the draft while you are editing, the editor
says _This draft was replaced_ and opens the current one.

## Publishing: Direct or Reviewed

Each space chooses how changes reach the published page (**Settings →
Publishing**):

| Policy               | How changes are published                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Direct** (default) | Editors edit the page's live draft and **Publish** it. Every published version stays in the history. Branches and pull requests remain available for bigger changes. |
| **Reviewed**         | The published page is protected. Changes are proposed on a branch and published only after a review approves them.                                                   |

In a **Direct** space, **Publish** takes an optional message (_What
changed?_), waits for **Saved** and turns exactly what the server has stored
into the next published version. Edits typed afterwards stay **Unpublished
changes** until the next publish. If nothing changed since the last version,
the editor says _No changes to publish_.

In a **Reviewed** space the page says _Changes to this page are reviewed.
Start a branch to propose changes._ **Start a branch** creates your branch
and opens its live draft. A version merged into a Reviewed space records the
people who approved it. A person working alone should use a Direct space: nobody can approve their
own changes.

Publishing a page only makes a version the published one inside Lictory. It
is unrelated to handing work items off to Jira.

## Versions, branches and pull requests

A page's history works like a specification's:

- A **version** (a commit) is an immutable snapshot of the page's title and
  content, with a message, its author and everyone who contributed to it.
  Versions are never changed afterwards. **Restoring** an old version creates
  a new version with its content; the history keeps both.
- A **branch** is a proposed change with its own live draft. Anyone who can
  edit the space can start one. Its author and the collaborators they invite
  edit it; everyone who can read the space can look at it. **Commit** saves
  what the server has stored as the branch's next version, the way
  **Publish** does on the page itself; uncommitted edits show as
  **Uncommitted changes**.
- A **pull request** proposes merging a branch into the published page (or
  into another branch). Anyone with comment or edit access to the space
  can be asked to review it, and reviews can be required.
- **Approvals belong to the result.** An approval records the exact result of
  the merge the reviewer inspected. Any later change to the branch or to the
  published page that alters that result makes the approval **stale**, and
  the reviewer is told. People who contributed to the change cannot approve
  it.
- **Merging** into the published page publishes the result as a new version
  and records the valid approvals on it. It needs no open blocking comments
  on the page, nobody currently requesting changes, every required review
  approving the current result, and, in a Reviewed space, at least one
  required review. A merge also waits until neither side has uncommitted
  live edits, so a published version never contains text nobody saw.

### Conflicts

When a branch and the published page both changed, Lictory combines changes
that do not overlap automatically. Edits that overlap are never merged
silently: they become **conflicts**, each on the smallest piece that can be
decided (a paragraph or heading, a whole list, a whole table, or the title).
Resolve them on the branch, conflict by conflict: keep the branch's version,
keep the published one, keep both, or write the result by hand. Resolving
creates a new version of the branch, which reviewers then approve as usual.

History is never rewritten: approvals are revoked, review requests
cancelled, pull requests closed (their reviews and discussions are kept), and
a deleted branch keeps its versions in the page's history.

## Comments

Select text and choose **Comment** (or press **Mod+Alt+M** in the editor) to
start a discussion on exactly that text, or comment on the whole page. The
**Comments** panel lists the page's discussions in document order, beside the
page on wide screens and as a sheet on narrow ones. Commented text is tinted
(red for blocking comments); pointing at a comment or at its text marks the
other.

- Comments stay attached to their text as people keep typing, and to the
  version they were written on once a draft is published or committed.
- **Mark blocking** when something must be settled first. An open blocking
  comment keeps the page's pull requests from merging, and the people who
  wrote the page are told. (In a Direct space it does not stop **Publish**.)
- **Resolve** and **Reopen** keep every message; messages are never edited
  or deleted.
- When the commented text has been deleted, the comment shows **Original
  text removed** with the quote and a link to the version it was written on.
  When the text has changed, it says **The quoted text changed**. Comments are
  never silently moved to other text.
- Mention people to notify them. Lictory also notifies reviewers when their
  review is requested or their approval goes stale, never for every edit.

## Access

Access is decided per space, never per page.

| Access                  | Who can edit                                                                           | Who can read                                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Inherited** (default) | Members of the home (organization, project or team), and people named with edit access | Every organization member                            |
| **Restricted**          | People named with edit access                                                          | Only people named in the space's **Access** settings |

- Inherited access follows membership: joining a team gives edit access to
  its spaces, leaving takes it away. Team and project membership grant access
  to spaces only; they still grant nothing on work items.
- Everyone else in the organization can view an Inherited space, but not
  comment. Name people with **Give access** to let them comment or edit:
  **Can view** (read pages and their history), **Can comment** (also take
  part in discussions and review) or **Can edit** (also create, move and
  publish pages). In an Inherited space named access only adds to what the
  home gives: **Access** settings show what each named person can do in the
  end and why (_Can edit · member of Payments_, _Can comment · named
  access_), and **Remove named access** says what they keep.
- A **Restricted** space ignores home membership: only named people can open
  it. Whoever restricts a space keeps edit access, and switching back to
  Inherited keeps the named access.
- A space's **administrators** are its creator, the administrators of its
  home project and the organization's administrators. They manage access,
  the home, the publishing policy and archiving. Managing a Restricted space
  does not let them read it: they find it under _Restricted spaces you
  administer_ and choose **Grant myself access…**, which is recorded in the
  audit log.
- Pages cannot be restricted individually. To keep part of a tree private,
  put it in its own Restricted space (it can have the same home).

A space or page you cannot read looks exactly like one that does not exist.
Every check runs on the server, for the browser, agents and live editing
alike: someone whose access is removed stops receiving live edits at once,
or within 30 seconds at most.

## Work items and page links

Type `#` (or choose **Work item** after `/`) to mention a work item such as
`PAY-142`. Readers see it as a live chip with its current status, refinement
state and readiness; someone who cannot see the item sees only its key. Type
`[[` (or **Link to page**) to link another page of a space you can read. A link always shows the
linked page's current title, or _Unavailable page_ for readers who cannot
open it.

## Agents

Agents connected through [MCP](/integrations/agents#spaces) work with spaces
using your access. They can list spaces and pages, read any published
version, compare versions, read the history and the discussions, and take
part in discussions. They cannot type into live drafts: they change a page
by proposing a new version as a branch with an open pull request, which
people review and merge. Publishing, deciding reviews, merging and resolving
discussions are your decisions; an agent performs them only when you
explicitly ask.
