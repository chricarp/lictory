**Lictory Spaces** keeps the pages a team writes together: handbooks,
runbooks, architecture notes, decision records. It brings the review model
of specifications to knowledge: pages are written together in real time, and
every published version stays in the page's history with who changed it and
who approved it. This page defines the concepts; the
[Spaces guide](/spaces) shows how to use them.

```text
Organization
└─ Space        a home for knowledge, optionally belonging to a project or a team
   └─ Page      nested pages; each has its own history
      ├─ main         the published version readers see
      └─ branches     proposed changes, each with its own live draft
```

## The concepts

**Space.** A home for knowledge. It belongs to the organization and,
optionally, to one project or one team: its **location**, which decides
where it is listed and who can edit it by default. A space owns no delivery
configuration. See [create and manage spaces](/spaces/create).

**Page.** A document in a space, nested under other pages as deep as needed.
Pages are not work items: no key, status, estimate, iteration or readiness.
Pages and work items link to each other instead (`#PAY-142` in a page shows
the item's live status). See [pages and the page tree](/spaces/pages).

**Live draft.** The shared working copy of a page (of its main line in a
Direct space, or of a branch) that several people edit at the same time. A
live draft is never history: nobody reviews or approves it, and search and
agents never read it as the page's content. See
[writing together](/spaces/editing).

**Version.** An immutable snapshot of a page's title and content, with a
message, its author and everyone who contributed to it. Publishing or
committing a live draft seals exactly what the server has stored into a new
version. Versions are never changed; restoring an old one creates a new
version. See [versions and history](/spaces/history).

**Publishing policy.** Each space chooses one. **Direct**: editors publish the
live draft straight to readers. **Reviewed**: the published page is
protected and changes arrive only through approved pull requests. See
[publishing](/spaces/publishing).

**Branch and pull request.** A branch is a proposed change with its own live
draft, edited by its author and the collaborators they invite. A pull
request proposes merging it into the published page or another branch;
merging into the published page publishes a new version. See
[branches and pull requests](/spaces/branches).

**Approval.** A reviewer's decision about the exact result a merge would
produce. Any change to that result makes it stale, and people who contributed
to a change cannot approve it, the same principle as
[approvals of specifications](/concepts/reviews#approvals-belong-to-revisions).

**Discussion.** A comment thread anchored to text, or to the whole page,
that follows its text across edits and versions. Blocking discussions hold
back merges. See [comments](/spaces/comments).

**Access.** Decided per space, never per page: **Inherited** from the
location's members, or **Restricted** to named people, at three levels (view,
comment, edit). Project and team membership grant access to spaces only,
never work item capabilities. See [access](/spaces/access).

## Spaces and specifications

Spaces does not replace specifications. Behavior a team is about to build is
refined in a work item's [specification](/concepts/specifications), reviewed
by the project's disciplines and checked for [readiness](/concepts/readiness).
Knowledge that outlives one piece of work belongs on a page. Publishing a page
only makes a version the published one inside Lictory; it is unrelated to
handing work items off to Jira.
