A **review** asks a discipline of the project (Product, Engineering, QA,
Security…) to evaluate the specification, optionally naming one person in it.
The discipline's name is the review responsibility: _Engineering_ reviews
feasibility and failure behavior, _QA_ testability and edge cases, and so on.
Each discipline decides only for itself, and holding a discipline never
approves anything by itself.

![The review rail of PAY-7: 2 of 2 approved, QA by Sam Rivera and Engineering by Dev Patel, both on revision 1, with Request](/screenshots/detail-review-rail.webp)

## When to request a review

Request reviews once the intent is clear enough for others to react to: the
problem, the expected behavior and the cases you already know about. A rough
specification is fine; reviewers' discussions are how it gets better. You
do not need to wait until it is finished, and you should not wait for a
meeting.

Requesting the first review moves a draft or refining specification to _in
review_.

## Request a review

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the review rail

On the item, the **Review** section of the rail lists the reviews of the
current revision (_1 / 2 approved_). On a narrow screen, open it with
**Review & details**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose Request

**Request** opens _Request review of revision N_. When several reviewing
disciplines have not been asked yet, a shortcut requests them all at once
(for example **Request Engineering and QA reviews**): required, from anyone in
each discipline.

</Step>
<Step>

### Choose the discipline and reviewer

![Request review of revision 2: Request Engineering and QA reviews in one step, or choose a Discipline and a Reviewer, Required for readiness, and Request review](/screenshots/detail-request-review.webp)

Pick the **Discipline**, then the **Reviewer**: _Anyone in Engineering_ (every
member can decide) or one member by name. Keep **Required for readiness**
ticked unless the review is only for information.

</Step>
<Step>

### Request review

Choose **Request review**. The people asked are notified.

</Step>
</Steps>

Requesting the same discipline again on the same revision adds another
reviewer to it (_A QA review already exists on this revision; another
reviewer will be added._).

### Required and optional reviews

- A **required** review counts for readiness: by default a specification is
  ready only when at least one required review was requested and every
  required review approved the current revision.
- An **optional** review (shown as **Optional**) is informative. Its decision
  is recorded but does not hold back readiness.

The project's [Definition of Ready](/review/readiness) can also require
specific disciplines to approve, whether or not anyone requested them.

## Who can be asked, and who can ask

- Only disciplines with **Can be asked to review** can be requested. A named
  reviewer must be a member of that discipline. A project administrator sets
  both in **Project settings → Disciplines** ([disciplines](/organization/disciplines)).
- People whose discipline has **Request reviews** can request and cancel
  reviews. In a new project, Product, Engineering and QA all can.
- Only an assigned person, or a current member of the assigned discipline,
  can decide the review. See [approve or request changes](/review/approvals).

## Reviews follow the specification

A review belongs to a revision, but the responsibility persists. When the
specification is revised, every active review is requested again on the new
revision with the same discipline and reviewer, and the old decisions stay in
history. Nobody has to request Engineering again after each edit.

When someone opens a [pull request](/specifications/branches) to main, the
required reviews of the current revision are copied onto it.

## Cancel a review

When a review is no longer needed, choose the **×** on its row (_Cancel this
review request_). Its reviewers are told in the app. An approved review has
no **×**: it stays as the record of that decision.

## Find the reviews waiting for you

- **Reviews** in the project's sidebar lists the items whose current revision
  asks for your review, or your disciplines' (_Engineering review requested
  on revision 2 · 1 blocking discussion_), and items with a pull request
  waiting for your approval.
- **Inbox → To do** lists every review asked of you across projects, page
  reviews included, and leaves the list when you decide. See
  [the inbox](/inbox).
- On an item, the rail says _A pull request waits for your review_ when one of
  its pull requests needs your decision.

## Notifications

| Notification                  | Who receives it                                       | Default        |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Review requested              | The named reviewer, or every member of the discipline | In app + email |
| Review cancelled              | The reviewers who were asked                          | In app         |
| Review decision               | The item's owners and whoever requested the review    | In app + email |
| Pull request review requested | The named reviewer, or every member of the discipline | In app + email |

A review requested from a whole discipline reaches every member, but only
people asked by name count as involved in the item afterwards. See
[notifications](/inbox/notifications).

## What to watch for

- **No reviewing discipline?** The request form says so: a project
  administrator must enable **Can be asked to review** on a discipline first.
- **Decisions apply to the current revision only.** A review left from an
  earlier revision cannot be decided; the new revision has its own.
- **Lictory Intelligence is not a reviewer.** It can be required as a
  [readiness check](/review/readiness#lictory-intelligence-check), never as a
  discipline's review.

## Tips

- Ask whole disciplines by default and name a person only when one specific
  expert must decide. Anyone in the discipline can then pick it up.
- Request reviews early and let reviewers comment on the exact text. Each
  revision keeps the same reviewers, so early requests cost nothing later.
- Use optional reviews for people who should know about a change but do not
  own a concern in it.
