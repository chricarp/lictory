title: Approve or request changes
description: Decide a review for your discipline, understand why approvals belong to one revision, and see when an approval has gone stale.
---

An **approval** says: _I accepted this exact revision for my discipline._ It
is never a permanent flag on the item. When the text changes, the approval
still describes the revision that was reviewed, and Lictory asks again for the
new one.

## Decide a review

When a review is asked of you or your discipline, its row in the item's
**Review** rail offers:

- **Approve revision N**: the current revision satisfies your discipline's
  concerns.
- **Request changes**: it does not yet. Say why in a
  [discussion](/specifications/discussions) on the passage, and mark it
  blocking if it must be settled first.
- **Revoke my approval**: withdraw an approval you gave. The review returns to
  _Requested_.

A new decision replaces your previous one on that review. Only the person
named on the review, or a current member of the assigned discipline, can
decide it; holding the discipline in another project does not count.

<Callout title="Approve the text, not the intent">
  Approve when the revision says what will be built, including acceptance
  criteria and failure behavior, not when you agree with the idea. Anything you
  would have to ask about later is a discussion to start now.
</Callout>

When you decide, the item's owners and whoever requested the review are
notified. While changes are requested, the item also appears in its owners'
[Inbox → To do](/inbox).

## Approvals belong to revisions

Every change to a specification's title or text creates a new
[revision](/specifications/history). When it does:

1. Every approval of the previous revision becomes **stale**, recording who
   caused it, when and why (_Specification revised (revision 14)_).
2. Every active review is requested again on the new revision, with the same
   discipline and reviewer.
3. The reviewers whose approval went stale are notified (_Your approval went
   stale_, in the app and by email).

So the item never claims more than was reviewed. In words, the situation
reads:

```text
Engineering approved revision 12.
Current revision: 14.
Engineering approval requires review.
```

In the rail, the Engineering review of revision 14 shows _Requested_, and
**N earlier approvals** lists _Engineering · Dev Patel approved revision 12 ·
outdated (Specification revised (revision 13))_. The footer always names the
revision the current approvals apply to.

![The review rail of PAY-2 after revision 2: Engineering and QA requested again, and under 1 earlier approval, Dev Patel’s approval of revision 1 marked outdated because the specification was revised](/screenshots/detail-stale-approval.webp)

Changes to properties (status, assignee, priority, iteration, estimate,
labels and so on) are not revisions and never affect approvals.

### See approvals in history

**Versions** lists the approvals given on each revision and whether each is
still _valid_, _outdated_, _revoked_ or carried to a later revision. Compare
the approved revision with the current one to see exactly what a reviewer
has not seen yet. See [history](/specifications/history).

## Carried approvals

Two cases carry an approval to a new revision instead of asking again,
because the reviewers already approved that exact result:

- **A merged pull request.** Each valid approval of the pull request becomes
  an approval of the revision the merge creates. The pull request's reviewers
  approved the complete merged result, so they do not approve the same text
  twice. Reviews the pull request did not include are asked again as usual.
- **A merge that changes only the breakdown.** When the specification's
  text is unchanged, its approvals are carried to the new revision (_carried
  to revision 5: specification text unchanged_). Where the pull request has
  its own approval for the same discipline, that one replaces the carried
  one.

See [branches and pull requests](/specifications/branches#approvals-bind-to-the-merge-result)
for how pull request approvals go stale when the merge result changes.

## Approvals by agents and Lictory Intelligence

- **Lictory Intelligence never approves.** A project can require a clean
  Lictory Intelligence review as a [readiness
  check](/review/readiness#lictory-intelligence-check), always beside a
  person's decision.
- **Litto or an MCP agent** records an approval only on your explicit
  instruction. The approval is yours and belongs to the revision; the activity
  records that it was made through the agent.

## What to watch for

- **Only the current revision can be decided.** If the specification changed
  while you were reading, review the new revision; the decision form always
  names it.
- **On main, nothing stops the author of a revision from approving it.** Pull
  requests exclude their contributors. When an independent review matters,
  change the specification through a pull request.
- **Page approvals work differently.** Pages in Lictory Spaces ask named
  people, exclude everyone who typed in the change and have no discipline
  responsibilities. See [page branches and pull
  requests](/spaces/branches#approvals-belong-to-the-exact-result).

## Tips

- Before approving a revised specification, compare it with the revision you
  approved last; only the difference needs your attention.
- Request changes with a concrete reason in a discussion. A bare
  "changes requested" sends the author looking for what you meant.
- Revoke rather than wait when you realize you approved too early: the
  approval stops counting at once and the history shows why.
