A specification is not ready because someone chose _Ready_ from a menu.
Lictory checks facts, like CI for requirements: is the specification written,
is any blocking discussion open, did the required reviews approve this
revision. When every check passes, the item becomes **ready** by itself; when
one stops passing, it is no longer ready.

![Readiness of PAY-2: 2 of 4 checks; the specification is not empty and a required review is requested pass, “No blocking discussion is open” fails with 1 blocking discussion open, and the approvals wait on Engineering and QA for revision 2](/screenshots/detail-readiness-checks.webp)

## Read an item's readiness

The **Readiness** section of the review rail shows _N / M checks_, one bar
segment per check, and every check with what it verified. A failing check
says what would make it pass:

- _The description is empty._
- _1 blocking discussion open._ (in red: it stops readiness, it is not merely
  waiting)
- _Request at least one required review._
- _Waiting on QA for revision 2._
- _Add a “Failure behavior” section._ or _The “Failure behavior” section is
  empty._
- _1 of 2 child specifications is not ready._

When all pass, the section says the specification is ready for delivery and
that readiness does not accept, schedule or start the work. Ready is
eligibility; [accepting it for delivery](/delivery) is a separate decision.

### Refinement state

Spec-backed items have a refinement state next to their delivery status:
_draft_, _refinement_, _in review_ and _ready_. The two are never one field.
Requesting the first review moves a draft to _in review_; Lictory sets _ready_
when the checks pass. The first time a specification becomes ready, its main
is protected and later changes go through
[pull requests](/specifications/branches).

## The default checks

A project that never edits its Definition of Ready, and any type it adds
later, uses Lictory's defaults:

| Check                                                 | Passes when                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| _The specification is not empty_                      | The current revision has text                               |
| _No blocking discussion is open_                      | Every blocking discussion is resolved                       |
| _At least one required review is requested_           | A required review exists on the current revision            |
| _Every required review approved the current revision_ | Each required review has a valid approval of this revision  |
| _Every child specification is ready_                  | Every spec-backed child is ready (skipped without children) |

## Define what ready means

Project administrators edit the Definition of Ready in **Project settings →
Readiness**, per work item type: _A Story is ready when…_ The checks are
grouped by what they look at.

![The Readiness settings: the Definition of Ready per type (Epic and Story) with its built-in checks switched on, options to require a section, a discipline’s approval, a Lictory Intelligence review or a sign-off, and When work starts before it’s ready set to Warn](/screenshots/project-readiness.webp)

| Group                               | Checks you can add                                                             |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Written**                         | **Require a section**: a heading whose section is not empty                    |
| **Reviewed**                        | **Require a discipline’s approval**: chosen disciplines approved this revision |
| **Checked by Lictory Intelligence** | **Require a Lictory Intelligence review**                                      |
| **Confirmed by people**             | **Add a sign-off**: a named confirmation by a person                           |
| **Decomposed**                      | The built-in children check                                                    |

Switch the built-in checks on and off in the same list. **Start from…**
copies another type's rules, or goes back to **Lictory’s defaults**.

Labels are derived from what a check verifies, so a check never claims more
than it does: a required section reads _It has a non-empty “Acceptance
criteria” section_, which proves the section exists and has content, not that
it is correct. Only sign-offs are named by people.

### Sign-offs

A sign-off is a confirmation that a check cannot compute, such as _Designs
attached_. Give it a **Sign-off name**, optional guidance shown on the item,
and who may confirm it: chosen disciplines, or **Anyone with a discipline**.

On the item, an eligible person chooses **Sign off**; the check then says
_Signed off by_ who and when. A sign-off belongs to the revision it was given
on: a new revision needs a new one. **Withdraw sign-off** withdraws it and
keeps the record.

### Lictory Intelligence check

**Require a Lictory Intelligence review** passes when the latest Lictory
Intelligence review of the item analysed the current revision and found no
blocker (or, if you choose, no blocker or major finding). The check names the
run it relies on (_Automated check · Lictory Intelligence review scored 85_),
never a discipline's approval.

- It can only be required **beside a person's decision**: the type must also
  require approved reviews, a discipline's approval or a sign-off.
- Anyone holding a discipline in the project can choose **Override for this
  revision** when the check is wrong, and **Withdraw override** later. The
  override is recorded like a sign-off.
- With Lictory Intelligence off or unavailable the check does not pass by
  itself; override it, or remove it from the Definition of Ready.

See [Lictory Intelligence reviews](/ai/reviews).

### Publish a new version

The Definition of Ready is versioned. Your edits stay a draft, marked
**New**, **Changed** or **Removed**, until you publish them:

1. Choose **Review changes…**. Lictory evaluates the draft against the
   project's items: how many keep their version, which _become Ready when you
   publish_, which _will need more work_ and why, and how many are
   unaffected.
2. Add a change summary (_Why is this changing?_) and choose **Publish version
   N**.

Items that are ready, done or canceled keep the version they were evaluated
under, so finished work never loses readiness because the rules changed. Such
an item says so (_Kept on Definition of Ready version 2 because it was ready
or done when the rules changed._) and moves to the latest version if it stops
being ready. Every other item follows the new version at once.

**History** lists every version, who published it and what it changed;
**Restore** loads an earlier one into the editor to publish it again. If
someone else publishes while you edit, Lictory asks you to start again from
their version rather than overwrite it.

## Readiness and delivery

### In a native project

The **readiness policy** (_When work starts before it’s ready_, in the same
settings) decides what happens when a spec-backed item that is not ready is
accepted for delivery or first moves into a _started_ status:

| Policy             | Effect                                                         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Allow**          | Readiness is informative only                                  |
| **Warn** (default) | The move succeeds, and the activity records the failing checks |
| **Block**          | The move is refused, naming the failing checks                 |

Plain work under a specification (Tasks, Subtasks) follows its nearest
specification's readiness, so splitting unsettled behavior into tasks does not
bypass the policy. Once a revision has been accepted for delivery, work on it
can continue while a later change to the specification is refined; a new
blocking problem on the accepted revision itself still applies the policy.
See [delivery](/delivery).

### In an external project

Only ready specifications can be handed off to Jira, whatever the policy; the
policy setting is unavailable there (_only ready work is handed off to Jira_).
Handing off an item with its children skips children that are not ready. See
[Jira](/integrations/jira).

## Find ready work

- **Refinement → Ready for delivery** lists specifications whose checks pass
  and that are not yet accepted for delivery.
- **Inbox → To do** shows _Ready for delivery_ for items you created or are
  assigned.
- People involved in an item are notified when it becomes ready (_Ready for
  delivery_, in the app and by email) and when it stops being ready (_No
  longer ready_, in the app).

## What to watch for

- **A check proves only what its label says.** A non-empty section is not a
  reviewed one; approvals are what say the behavior is right.
- **A type needs at least one check**, and at most 30. Each built-in check
  and the Lictory Intelligence check can be required once per type.
- **A type with no check that applies** to an item (for example only the
  children check, and no children) can never become ready; the item says so.
- **Publishing rules sends no notifications.** Items that change state
  because of a new version record it in their activity.

## Tips

- Add required sections for what your reviewers always ask about, such as
  _Acceptance criteria_ and _Failure behavior_. Authors then see the gap
  before anyone has to ask.
- Require a discipline's approval for the types where that discipline must
  always agree (QA on Stories, say), instead of relying on people to request
  it.
- Start with the defaults. Add a check when the same problem reaches delivery
  twice, not before.
