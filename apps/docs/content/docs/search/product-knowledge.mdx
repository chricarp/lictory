Product knowledge is what your organization has settled about how the
product behaves. Content search finds where words are written. Product
knowledge finds what was **agreed**: recorded decisions, approved and
delivered specification revisions, and published pages. It tells each kind
apart.

People use it to find earlier agreements while refining. Litto and agents
connected through MCP use it before they describe how the product behaves.

## What counts as product knowledge

| Evidence               | Shown as                     | What it establishes                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Approved revision**  | **Approved intent**          | The current revision of a specification that is **Ready** and has a granted approval: what the team agreed to build.                                          |
| **Delivered revision** | **Recorded delivered scope** | The revision accepted for delivery when the item moved to a done status, in a project delivered in Lictory: what the team recorded as delivered.              |
| **Recorded decision**  | **Recorded conclusion**      | A durable conclusion reached during refinement, with its author and the revision it was recorded on. See [decisions](/specifications/decisions).              |
| **Published page**     | Page, with its version       | Documentation in Lictory Spaces, as its published version. A page from a space that publishes through review carries more weight than one published directly. |

Each work item adds at most two revisions: its delivered revision and its
current approved revision. Older revisions remain in the item's history.

What is **not** product knowledge:

- discussions, which are transient until someone records a decision from
  them;
- specifications that are not approved, and live drafts, branches and open
  pull requests of pages and specifications;
- a **Done** status in Jira or another delivery tool. It describes an issue,
  not which revision was built, so it does not create delivered knowledge.

## Authority: which evidence wins

The kinds of evidence say different things. Litto weighs them in this order,
strongest first:

1. an approved or delivered specification revision;
2. a recorded decision;
3. a page published through review;
4. a page published directly;
5. a discussion.

Some limits apply:

- **Approval is intent, not delivery.** An approved revision says what was
  agreed. Only a delivered revision says it was delivered.
- **A decision is a conclusion.** It does not make a specification approved
  or delivered.
- **Documentation is not agreement.** When a page disagrees with an approved
  specification, the specification states the intended behavior. Litto names
  both and does not pick one silently.

In search results, a title match ranks above a match in the text. Among
results that match equally well, agreements and decisions come first, then
pages published through review, then pages published directly.

## Exact versions

Every result names the version it is about:

- an approved or delivered revision opens that revision in the item's
  history, and a decision opens the revision it was recorded on, even after
  the specification has changed. **Open current** opens the item as it is
  now;
- a page opens the exact version that matched (`?commit=` in the address),
  not today's page.

So an answer that cites "PAY-2 revision 2" or "Capture retry design, version
3" can be checked against what was actually agreed or published then.

## What is left out

- **Archived** work. Archiving an item removes it, and the decisions
  recorded on it, from product knowledge. Archived projects, pages and spaces
  are left out too. Restoring brings them back; nothing is deleted.
- **Pages you cannot read.** Pages come only from spaces you can read
  ([space access](/spaces/access)).

Done and canceled items are not archived, so finished work stays findable.

## Find related agreements on an item

While refining, check whether the team already settled a question:

<Steps>
<Step>

Open the work item and go to its **Decisions** section.

</Step>
<Step>

Choose **Find related agreements**.

</Step>
<Step>

Type in **Search decisions and specifications…** and choose **Search**.

</Step>
</Steps>

Each result shows its key, revision and kind (**Recorded conclusion**,
**Approved intent** or **Recorded delivered scope**) with an excerpt. Results
come from the item's project and do not include pages; use
[search](/search) or ask Litto to include documentation.

## Litto and agents

[Litto](/ai/litto) searches product knowledge before it describes
established behavior. Its answers cite the revision number of a
specification or the version of a page, with links to that version. When it
read pages, they are listed under the answer with their space and version.

Agents connected through [MCP](/integrations/agents) use the same search
(`searchProductKnowledge`) with your access. `searchKnowledge`, the content
search, finds where something is written; its matches are for navigation,
not evidence.

When Litto drafts proposals from rough material, it grounds them in your
project's recent decisions and approved or delivered revisions, not in pages
([draft proposals](/ai/litto#draft-proposals)).

## What to watch for

- **Unapproved work is invisible here.** A specification under review is not
  product knowledge until it is approved and **Ready**. Use
  [search](/search) to find work in progress.
- **External delivery does not record delivered scope.** In projects handed
  off to Jira, only approved revisions and decisions appear.
- **Matching is a phrase match.** Search for a short, distinctive phrase.

## Tips

- **Record decisions.** When a discussion settles something ("Retries reuse
  the original idempotency key"), record it as a
  [decision](/specifications/decisions). Otherwise it is lost when the
  discussion is resolved.
- **Publish important pages through review.** Pages in a space that publishes
  through reviewed pull requests rank above directly published ones, and the
  approvals show who agreed ([publishing](/spaces/publishing)).
- **Cite pinned page versions in specifications.** An approved revision that
  cites an exact page version keeps both pieces of evidence connected
  ([knowledge](/knowledge)).
- Ask Litto "What did we agree about refund limits?" before you write a new
  proposal, and start from what it cites.
